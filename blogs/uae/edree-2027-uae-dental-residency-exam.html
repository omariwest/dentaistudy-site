EDREE 2027: UAE Dental Residency Exam and Application Planning

The Emirates Dental Residency Entrance Examination (EDREE) is the UAE residency entrance examination for dental graduates. It is administered through the National Institute for Health Specialties (NIHS) examination portal hosted by UAE University and is used by UAE dental training programs as one of the qualifying examinations for residency or specialty admission.

For 2027 candidates, there is an important limitation: as of 18 November 2026, the public NIHS/UAEU pages do not yet show a confirmed 2026–27/2027 EDREE sitting calendar. The official FAQ says the number of examinations can change each year and that exam dates are announced in advance.

That means this article does not invent a 2027 EDREE date. Instead, it explains the current official registration rules, fee, eligibility, exam structure, result timeline and how to plan your residency application while waiting for the next sitting calendar.

Quick Answers

  • Who can take EDREE? Eligible dental graduates and final-year dental students who satisfy the relevant UAE residency-training criteria.
  • Current examination fee: AED 1,000, paid online.
  • Can the fee be refunded? No. The current NIHS application page states that paid examination fees are non-refundable.
  • Can you change the exam date after enrolment? No, under the current published rule.
  • How many attempts are allowed? Up to three attempts in a year starting from the first attempt to obtain a pass.
  • How many questions? The current NIHS exam-structure page says the residency entrance examinations usually contain 120 MCQs completed in three hours.
  • How fast are results released? NIHS says an electronic statement of results is issued within 10 working days.
  • How long are results valid? The current NIHS results page says results are valid for 24 months from submission to the authorities.
  • Are 2027 EDREE dates confirmed? Not on the public NIHS pages as of 18 November 2026.

What EDREE Is

EDREE is the dental counterpart to the Emirates Medical Residency Entrance Examination.

The NIHS exam portal describes the residency entrance examination as a qualifying examination designed to assess readiness for intensive residency training.

Passing the exam does not by itself create a residency place.

After passing, you still apply to the training authority or university whose residency or specialty program you want to join.

Current MBRU residency-admission criteria, for example, accept:

  • EDREE for dental applicants; or
  • the Saudi Dental Licensure Examination (SDLE).

The program itself then applies its own academic, interview, portfolio and licensing requirements.

No Confirmed 2027 Sitting Calendar Yet

The NIHS FAQ states that the number of examinations can change each year depending on residency intake and other factors.

It also states that dates are announced to the public in advance.

As of our verification date, the public exam pages provide the registration system and current rules but not a new 2027 EDREE date table.

So a trustworthy 2027 plan should be:

  1. create/check your NIHS exam account;
  2. monitor the official examination page for an open EDREE sitting;
  3. keep your residency application documents ready;
  4. avoid booking travel around an unofficial date from a prep provider or social-media group.

Current EDREE Registration Process

NIHS says registration must be completed online.

The process is:

  1. create/register your account;
  2. complete the relevant registration information;
  3. open the examinations section;
  4. select an examination that is currently accepting enrolment;
  5. pay the examination fee online;
  6. receive automatic enrolment and a payment receipt.

Every applicant receives an acknowledgement and reference number for examination-registration communication.

The current portal also states that a candidate may register for only one examination at a time.

Current Examination Fee

The current all-inclusive online fee is:

AED 1,000

The portal describes this as covering registration, processing and handling.

Payment is made online by card.

The published rule says:

  • fees are non-refundable;
  • once enrolled for a particular examination, the candidate cannot change the examination date;
  • the paid fee cannot be transferred to another examination.

Because EDREE dates are not yet published for the next cycle, do not pay until you are certain the selected sitting is workable.

Eligibility

The current NIHS application page says the residency entrance examinations are open to:

  • dental graduates;
  • final-year dental students;
  • UAE nationals and other nationalities;

provided they satisfy the residency-training application criteria of the relevant UAE health authority or institution.

The exam portal itself does not replace the admission rules of the residency provider.

That distinction is important.

You can be eligible to sit EDREE but still fail to meet a specific residency program's:

  • GPA rule;
  • internship requirement;
  • graduation recency rule;
  • English-language requirement;
  • licensing requirement;
  • portfolio or interview threshold.

Current Attempt Rule

NIHS currently states that eligible candidates may take EDREE up to three times per year starting from the first attempt to obtain a pass score.

After obtaining a pass, candidates are not eligible to keep retaking the exam within the same academic year simply to improve the mark.

The NIHS FAQ also notes that every attempt appears on the transcript and is forwarded to the relevant health authorities.

More retakes can therefore affect how an application is viewed even when the final result is a pass.

Exam Format

The current NIHS exam-structure page describes the residency entrance examination as:

  • primarily computer-based;
  • multiple-choice;
  • usually 120 questions;
  • completed in 3 hours;
  • designed to assess application of clinical knowledge, diagnosis and management.

A paper format can be used if circumstances require it.

NIHS also provides a dedicated EDREE blueprint and sample questions.

Current EDREE Blueprint

The current EDREE Applicant Guide is labeled 2025–2026.

That is the latest official EDREE blueprint visible on the NIHS portal as of 18 November 2026.

It organizes the exam around major dental disciplines and higher-order clinical reasoning, including:

  • endodontics;
  • restorative dentistry;
  • prosthodontics;
  • pediatric and preventive dentistry;
  • orthodontics;
  • oral and maxillofacial surgery;
  • periodontics;
  • oral medicine and oral pathology;
  • oral radiology;
  • ethics, jurisprudence and practice management;
  • patient safety and occupational dental hazards.

The blueprint also states that the FDI two-digit tooth-numbering system is used.

Do not call this a “2027 blueprint.” Use it as the current official preparation document until NIHS publishes a replacement.

Results

NIHS says every candidate receives an electronic statement of results with:

  • score;
  • ranking.

The current results page says this is issued within 10 working days of the examination.

Candidates who authorize it can also have results sent to the organization of their choice.

The same page states that results are valid for 24 months from the date of submission to the authorities.

Because this wording is specific, candidates should verify how the target residency authority applies that validity when submitting a 2027 application.

After Passing EDREE

Passing EDREE is one requirement, not the full application.

You then apply to the relevant residency or specialty program.

Current MBRU GME criteria include:

  • a recognized medical/dental degree;
  • completed internship requirements;
  • normally being within five years of graduation;
  • normally not having a clinical-practice gap longer than 24 months;
  • a qualifying residency entrance examination such as EDREE or SDLE;
  • program-specific English requirements;
  • portfolio and interview stages;
  • eventual licensing requirements.

Different UAE authorities may use different timelines and selection rules.

Current MBRU Timeline: Do Not Misread the Academic-Year Label

MBRU currently displays a 2026–27 Admission Timeline for its GME intake with:

  • applications opening 19 February 2026;
  • application deadline 31 March 2026;
  • MMI in early May 2026;
  • specialty interviews in late May 2026;
  • cross-match 29 June 2026;
  • conditional offers in early July 2026;
  • program commencement 28 September 2026.

Those dates are already past. They are useful as evidence of how the process is structured, but they are not a 2027 application calendar.

Do not copy that table and label it “2027 residency applications.”

Wait for the next official intake timeline from the authority you plan to apply to.

A Practical 2027 Planning Timeline

Since the next EDREE sitting dates are not yet public, use a milestone plan rather than invented calendar dates.

Now

  • confirm your EDREE account access;
  • download the current EDREE blueprint;
  • prepare passport/identity documents;
  • prepare dental degree and internship documents;
  • identify the exact UAE residency program you want;
  • review its GPA, English, recency and portfolio requirements.

When EDREE registration opens

  • confirm the exact exam date before paying;
  • remember the AED 1,000 fee is non-refundable;
  • do not enrol in a date you cannot attend;
  • keep the registration reference number and receipt.

After the exam

  • monitor the portal for the electronic result;
  • use the result within the applicable residency-application window;
  • check whether your target authority requires the transcript to be submitted directly.

Before residency applications open

  • prepare program-specific documents;
  • complete ECE evaluation if required for an overseas degree;
  • prepare English-language evidence if required;
  • update your portfolio;
  • verify licensing requirements separately.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating an unofficial prep-site date as the official 2027 EDREE schedule.
  • Assuming EDREE admission equals residency admission.
  • Paying the AED 1,000 fee before confirming you can attend.
  • Assuming you can transfer the fee to another exam date.
  • Ignoring the three-attempt rule.
  • Using an old blueprint without checking the NIHS portal for an update.
  • Confusing one university's application calendar with a national UAE residency calendar.
  • Forgetting that the target program can impose stricter academic and licensing criteria.
