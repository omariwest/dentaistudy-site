SDLE 2027: Registration, Prometric Booking and Exam Fees

The Saudi Dental Licensure Examination (SDLE) is the Saudi Commission for Health Specialties examination used to assess whether a dental candidate meets the minimum knowledge and clinical-reasoning standard for professional practice.

Unlike a single annual exam date, SCFHS publishes testing windows. For 2027, its Professional Practice Licensure Exams page currently lists nine 2027 windows from January through October.

The official public pages also confirm the registration sequence: apply for examination eligibility through Mumaris+, receive a scheduling permit, then book an available appointment through Prometric.

There is one fee point that needs careful wording: SCFHS publicly lists SAR 120 as the cost of the Professional Practice Licensure Exams Eligibility service. The current public SCFHS pages checked for this article do not publish one fixed SDLE Prometric appointment price. Candidates should therefore verify the actual test-booking charge shown during the live booking process rather than relying on an old third-party figure.

Quick Answers

  • Who runs SDLE? Saudi Commission for Health Specialties (SCFHS).
  • How is it delivered? Computer-based at approved Prometric centres in Saudi Arabia and internationally.
  • How do you register? Mumaris+ eligibility application → scheduling permit → Prometric booking.
  • Confirmed SCFHS eligibility-service fee: SAR 120.
  • Is there one fixed public Prometric exam fee? Not on the current SCFHS public pages checked; verify the live booking amount.
  • How many questions? 200, split into two 100-question blocks.
  • Exam time? Two 120-minute blocks with a scheduled 30-minute break.
  • Passing score? 542 on a reporting scale of 200–800.
  • How many attempts? Current applicant guidance says eligible candidates may take SDLE up to four times a year while working toward a pass.
  • Results timing? 2–6 weeks after the end of the test window.

Who Is Eligible?

The current SDLE Applicant Guide says you may apply if you:

  • hold a recognized primary dental degree such as BDS or equivalent from an accredited health-science program; or
  • have commenced training during the internship year; or
  • are a student one year away from graduation.

The guide also notes that Saudi university/college students can sit SDLE during the final year of undergraduate study.

Professional classification, registration and licensing are separate administrative stages. Passing SDLE by itself does not grant a Saudi practice licence.

Step 1: Apply Through Mumaris+

SCFHS has a Professional Practice Licensure Exams Eligibility service for examinations including SDLE.

The current service page lists:

  • service channel: Mumaris+;
  • service duration: approximately 5 business days;
  • service cost: SAR 120.

The process is:

  1. sign in to Mumaris+;
  2. choose the licensure-exam eligibility service;
  3. complete the application details;
  4. pay the service fee;
  5. monitor the application status;
  6. receive the final decision.

This SAR 120 is an eligibility-service fee, not proof that the entire SDLE testing cost is SAR 120.

Step 2: Receive the Scheduling Permit

Once the application is processed, SCFHS issues a scheduling permit containing your eligibility period.

You receive an email explaining how to access it.

Do not try to book directly with Prometric before the eligibility process has produced the permit.

Your test appointment must fall inside the permitted eligibility period.

Step 3: Book Through Prometric

After receiving the scheduling permit, the current Applicant Guide says you can visit the specified Prometric booking site and schedule an available appointment.

Important points:

  • scheduling may not be available more than three months in advance;
  • candidates can test at SCFHS-approved Prometric centres locally or internationally as locations appear during scheduling;
  • the booking date must fall inside both the relevant SCFHS testing window and your own eligibility period;
  • seat availability is not guaranteed simply because the window exists.

For a dedicated booking guide, see SDLE Prometric Booking & International Centers.

What Does SDLE Cost in 2027?

There are different costs in the wider Saudi licensing pathway.

Confirmed public fee: eligibility service

SCFHS currently publishes SAR 120 for the Professional Practice Licensure Exams Eligibility service in Mumaris+.

Prometric test appointment fee

The current SCFHS Applicant Guide explains Prometric scheduling but does not publish one fixed public SDLE test-appointment amount.

That charge can be shown during the live booking/payment process and may depend on the applicable setup.

For this reason, DentAIstudy does not quote an unofficial 2027 Prometric fee as fact.

Before budgeting:

  1. confirm the current SAR 120 eligibility-service charge;
  2. complete eligibility;
  3. check the exact Prometric amount shown for your test location;
  4. add any card, travel or visa costs separately.

For the broader licensing budget, see SDLE Cost Breakdown.

Current SDLE Format

The current 2026 Applicant Guide describes SDLE as:

  • computer-based;
  • 200 questions;
  • two blocks of 100 questions;
  • 120 minutes per block;
  • one scheduled 30-minute break;
  • four answer choices per question;
  • up to 10% pilot questions.

Questions include both knowledge recall and scenario-based items testing:

  • interpretation;
  • analysis;
  • decision-making;
  • reasoning;
  • problem-solving.

Passing Score

The current SCFHS standard is 542 on a reporting scale of 200–800.

SCFHS describes this as the result of a formal standard-setting process reviewed by the Central Assessment Committee.

Do not convert 542 into an unofficial percentage-correct requirement.

For a dedicated explanation, see SDLE Passing Score 542 Explained.

Results

SDLE results are not provided immediately at the test centre.

The current Applicant Guide says psychometric analysis is completed after the test window closes and results are announced within 2–6 weeks after the end of the test window.

Candidates receive:

  • a statement of results;
  • a feedback report comparing performance with other test takers.

For details, see SDLE Results Timing.

Attempts

Current Applicant Guide notes say eligible candidates may take SDLE up to four times a year starting from the first attempt to obtain a pass score.

The same guidance also says:

  • you cannot sit twice in the same testing window;
  • if you do, the first dated result is announced and the second sitting counts as an attempt but the result is invalid;
  • scheduling your allowed attempts within the year is your responsibility;
  • after passing, candidates can receive further improvement attempts for residency-selection purposes under the applicable rules.

SCFHS's broader Professional Practice Licensure Exams page also describes additional/improvement attempts depending on the first result.

Use your actual Mumaris+ status and current SCFHS rules before planning a retake.

Test-Day Identification

Bring:

  • your scheduling permit;
  • acceptable unexpired identification.

Current guidance accepts:

  • passport;
  • Saudi national/residence identity card where applicable.

Your name on the permit must match your identification.

If you arrive more than 30 minutes late or do not have the required permit/ID, you may be denied admission and the event can count as an attempt unless an acceptable documented reason is approved.

Candidate Scenarios

Scenario 1: You want January 2027

The current first window is 3–21 January 2027.

You need your Mumaris+ eligibility and scheduling permit early enough to find a Prometric seat inside that period. Waiting until the final days of December creates unnecessary availability risk.

Scenario 2: Your preferred local centre is full

The Applicant Guide allows approved Prometric centres locally and internationally as they appear during scheduling.

You can consider another centre if it fits your eligibility period and travel circumstances.

Scenario 3: You cannot see a date more than three months away

That can be normal. The current guide says scheduling may not be available more than three months in advance.

Scenario 4: You are budgeting for the exam

Use SAR 120 as the confirmed current eligibility-service fee only. Do not assume it is the full testing cost. Check the live Prometric amount before finalizing your budget.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating the SAR 120 eligibility-service fee as the entire SDLE cost.
  • Using an old third-party Prometric fee as a guaranteed 2027 price.
  • Trying to schedule before the permit is issued.
  • Assuming a published window guarantees a seat at your preferred centre.
  • Booking outside the eligibility period.
  • Sitting twice in the same testing window.
  • Expecting instant results.
  • Using an older 300-question SDLE format instead of the current 200-question guide.
