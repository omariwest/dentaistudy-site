ORE Exam Dates 2027: Part 1 and Part 2 Confirmed Sittings

The General Dental Council (GDC) has confirmed five Overseas Registration Examination (ORE) sittings for January to April 2027, two for Part 1 and three for Part 2. It has not published anything after that. Booking has also changed: the exam is now delivered by UCL Consultants Ltd under a new contract, and candidates book through their MyGDC account.

This article is the calendar. It lists what the GDC has confirmed, what it has not announced, and what to plan around. It does not repeat the booking steps, which are in our Part 1 booking guide and Part 2 booking guide.

How to read it: Official means the GDC published it. Calculation is our arithmetic. Advice is our suggestion.

Quick Answers

  1. Part 1 (official): 2–3 February 2027 and 6–7 April 2027, 600 places per sitting.
  2. Part 2 (official): 21–24 January, 4–7 March and 15–18 April 2027, 200 places per sitting.
  3. The only exact booking date published: the January Part 2 window opens on Tuesday 24 November 2026 at 14:30 UK time. The GDC says sittings open for booking eight weeks in advance, but had not published dates for the other four.
  4. Fees for 2026/27 (official): Part 1 £485, Part 2 £6,967, application processing £115. A Medical Emergencies-only retake costs £686.
  5. Five-year rule (official): you must pass the ORE within five years of first attempting Part 1. Priority booking arrangements exist for candidates nearing that limit and for refugees.
  6. Do not plan around later 2027 sittings. The GDC's first contract year runs from 31 May 2026 to 30 May 2027, and nothing beyond it has been announced.

What changed under the new contract

The GDC signed contracts with UCL Consultants Ltd in March 2026, and the first exams under the new contract ran from August 2026. Three changes affect your planning:

  • Part 1 is computer-based, in two three-hour papers, at London venues named by the GDC (Teamco, Escalla and Tobacco Dock). The exam provider contacts you with your allocated venue.
  • Part 2 is held at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, Rockefeller Building.
  • Booking now runs through MyGDC. The GDC also said a new candidate portal with a revised booking system should be in place for exams scheduled in 2027. It had not published when, so expect the booking experience to change.

Part 1 gives you four attempts, as does Part 2. Part 2 has four components: an operative test on a dental manikin, an OSCE, a diagnostic and treatment planning exercise, and a Medical Emergencies practical.

Booking: what is and is not announced

Official:

  • The GDC says all sittings open for booking eight weeks in advance.
  • Places are taken fast, normally on the day the window opens.
  • Times in your account are UK time.
  • A sitting shows as "unavailable" eight hours before it opens, with its opening time.
  • You can log in on only one device at a time, and sessions end after 15 minutes of inactivity.
  • When all places are taken, the tab shows "closed – examination full" for two hours.

Not announced on 17 November 2026: booking dates for the 2 February Part 1, 4 March Part 2, 6 April Part 1 and 15 April Part 2 sittings, and any sitting after the contract year.

Priority arrangements

The GDC says candidates nearing the five-year limit for Part 2, and those with refugee status, are offered priority access to places. Its booking page describes priority candidates as those who may only have a few sittings available before their time limit expires. They receive a separate email with details of how to secure a place.

  • If your refugee status has already been verified, you do not need to share the documents again. If not, the GDC must verify them before it can organise the booking.
  • A priority candidate who books successfully must pay within 48 hours. For other bookings the GDC says payment must be made when booking, and an unpaid booking is not processed after one hour.
  • After priority places are allocated, remaining places are booked first come, first served.

The five-year rule

The GDC's booking page states that you must pass the ORE within five years of first attempting Part 1. The pages reviewed do not set out what happens when the limit is reached, so check the GDC's ORE application and policy pages or contact its Examinations team.

Advice: count your remaining chances in sittings, not years. For example, only three Part 2 sittings are published in 2027, and Part 2 places are limited.

Candidate planning scenarios

Advice built on the dates above.

Scenario 1: ready for Part 1. The next published Part 1 sittings are 2–3 February and 6–7 April, but the booking dates were not announced. Make sure you can log in to MyGDC, check your email regularly, and have the £485 fee ready. If both sittings fill, no further Part 1 sitting has been published.

Scenario 2: waiting for Part 2. The January window opens 24 November and costs £6,967 in full at booking. Have funds or a sponsor's payment ready, and tell any third-party payer about the payment deadlines. Plan for the later sittings (March and April), whose booking dates have not been published.

Scenario 3: Part 1 in February, Part 2 in April. The April Part 2 window probably opens about mid-February (calculation), and Part 1 results are expected around mid-March. A February Part 1 candidate is unlikely to have a result before the April booking window opens.

Scenario 4: near the five-year limit. Watch for the separate priority email. Do not assume a later date is available, because only the sittings above are published.

Scenario 5: sitting is full. Keep checking email and the GDC's booking dates page. See below.

If a sitting is full

The GDC says that if you cannot find an ORE available for booking, it "normally means that there are no places available." It also says that if places are freed by withdrawals, it will reopen the booking window and offer them to all candidates. It will email you and post the information on its booking dates page.

Advice: do not book flights or accommodation until your place is confirmed and paid for. If an error message appears when the window opens, it usually means the sitting is now full.

Common mistakes

  • Planning around unpublished sittings. Nothing beyond 30 May 2027 is announced.
  • Assuming the eight-week window is exact. January's window opens 58 days ahead.
  • Forgetting UK time. Booking times are in UK time.
  • Logging in on several devices. The GDC says this logs you out.
  • Booking without being able to pay. The GDC says a booking you do not pay for is not processed, and trying to book without intent may stop you booking the next sitting.
  • Missing priority emails. They are sent separately.
  • Assuming the five-year rule resets. The GDC states you must pass within five years of first attempting Part 1.
  • Relying on old pages. Fees and delivery arrangements changed in 2026.

FAQs

1. What are the confirmed ORE dates in 2027? Part 1: 2–3 February and 6–7 April. Part 2: 21–24 January, 4–7 March and 15–18 April. Those are the five sittings the GDC had confirmed at verification.

2. When does booking open? The January Part 2 window opens on 24 November 2026 at 14:30 UK time. The GDC says sittings open eight weeks in advance, but had not published dates for the others.

3. How much do the exams cost? Part 1 is £485 and Part 2 is £6,967, with a £115 application processing fee. You pay in full when booking. A Medical Emergencies-only retake costs £686.

4. What if I cannot get a place? Check email and the GDC's booking dates page. The GDC reopens booking windows when places become free through withdrawals.

5. What is the five-year rule and who gets priority? You must pass the ORE within five years of first attempting Part 1. The GDC offers priority access to candidates nearing the Part 2 limit and to candidates with refugee status.
