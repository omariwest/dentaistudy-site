ORE Part 2 Booking 2027: January, March and April Sittings

The GDC has confirmed three ORE Part 2 sittings in early 2027: 21–24 January, 4–7 March, and 15–18 April. Each sitting has 200 places, and all are held at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, Rockefeller Building.

The January sitting is the only one whose exact booking opening had been published at our last verification: Tuesday 24 November 2026 at 14:30 UK time. The March and April exam dates are confirmed, but their exact booking-opening timestamps were not yet listed.

This guide is about securing a Part 2 place, understanding priority access and the five-year rule, and knowing what to do when a sitting fills.

Who is eligible to book Part 2?

You must first pass ORE Part 1. Part 2 is the practical stage of the Overseas Registration Examination.

The GDC allows four attempts at Part 2. The ORE as a whole must be completed within five years of your first Part 1 attempt.

That five-year clock matters much more at Part 2 because a candidate can be clinically ready but still unable to secure a seat immediately. The GDC recognises this risk and provides priority arrangements for candidates who may have only a small number of sittings left before their time limit expires.

January 2027: exact booking information

The GDC's booking-dates page lists the January 2027 Part 2 sitting for 21–24 January 2027 and states that booking opens on Tuesday 24 November 2026 at 14:30 UK time.

Before 14:30

  • confirm you can access your online GDC account;
  • use only one device;
  • have the full £6,967 fee or approved payment arrangement ready;
  • read any priority email separately if you qualify;
  • remember that the time shown in your account is UK time.

The booking tab can show the exam as unavailable before opening. If you are already signed in when it opens, refresh the browser.

Payment timing

For normal booking, the GDC says you need to pay when you book and that an unpaid booking will not be processed after the allowed payment period.

Priority candidates receive separate instructions; the current booking notice says a successful priority candidate must make payment within 48 hours.

If a third party will pay for you, coordinate this before the window opens.

March and April 2027: confirmed exams, unconfirmed booking timestamps

The GDC has confirmed:

  • 4–7 March 2027 — 200 places;
  • 15–18 April 2027 — 200 places.

As of 17 November 2026, the GDC had not published the exact opening dates and times on its booking-dates page.

Its general rule says all ORE sittings open for booking approximately eight weeks in advance. That is useful for planning when to watch the website, but the actual opening should be taken from the GDC notice, not calculated independently.

First come, first served after priority places

Demand is high. After priority places are allocated, the GDC books remaining candidates on a first-come, first-served basis.

This means an advertised 200-place sitting is not a 200-place public pool if some places are reserved for priority candidates.

The GDC also warns that an error message during a high-demand opening usually means the exam has become full.

Priority booking

Candidates approaching the five-year limit

The GDC says candidates who may have only a few Part 2 sittings left before their five-year deadline receive separate priority information.

This is not a reason to wait until the final year. It is a protection mechanism for candidates whose remaining opportunities are genuinely limited.

Candidates with refugee status

The GDC also offers priority arrangements for eligible dental professionals with refugee status. Its wider ORE guidance says eligible refugees can request priority access for up to two attempts at Part 1 and two attempts at Part 2.

If your status has already been verified, the GDC says you do not need to provide the same documentation again. If verification is still needed, complete it before the booking window.

The five-year rule

You must pass the ORE within five years of first attempting Part 1.

Do not count from:

  • the date your ORE application was approved;
  • the date you first joined the candidate list;
  • the date you passed Part 1;
  • the date you first attempted Part 2.

The key starting point is the first Part 1 attempt.

For a candidate near the deadline, every published Part 2 sitting matters. If your timeline is tight, use the GDC's own priority communication and do not assume another 2027 sitting will be added.

See ORE Five-Year Deadline and Attempts Rule for the broader planning issue.

Part 2 venue, format and fee

All three current 2027 sittings are at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, Rockefeller Building.

The current fee is £6,967.

Part 2 has four components:

  1. an operative test on a dental manikin;
  2. an OSCE;
  3. a diagnostic and treatment-planning exercise;
  4. a practical medical-emergencies assessment.

A candidate who fails only the Medical Emergencies component may be eligible to retake only that component at a later Part 2 sitting. The current ME-only retake fee is £686.

For a format-focused guide, see ORE Part 2 OSCE Stations Breakdown.

If the exam is full

The GDC says that if places become available because of withdrawals, it can reopen the booking window and offer those places to candidates.

You should:

  • monitor your email;
  • monitor the official booking-dates page;
  • keep payment funds ready;
  • avoid buying travel before you have a confirmed paid place.

A reopened window can be short and competitive. Treat it as an opportunity, not a guaranteed second booking round.

Results and what happens after Part 2

The GDC says it aims to send Part 2 results within 20 working days.

Once you pass Part 2, you can apply for registration with the GDC. Passing the exam is therefore not the final administrative step; you still need to complete the registration process.

If you fail only the Medical Emergencies component, follow the current GDC rule for the ME-only retake. If you fail more broadly, another full Part 2 attempt may be needed if you remain eligible under the attempt and five-year limits.

Candidate planning examples

Scenario 1: Part 1 already passed, targeting January

Your most important date is 24 November 2026 at 14:30 UK time. Confirm login, payment readiness and any priority status before that moment. If you secure the seat, then arrange travel for January.

Scenario 2: January fills before you book

Do not assume you are finished for 2027. March and April sittings are confirmed. Watch for their official booking timestamps and any reopened January availability after withdrawals.

Scenario 3: close to the five-year deadline

Check the exact date of your first Part 1 attempt. Contact the GDC if your remaining Part 2 opportunities are limited and follow any priority instructions. Do not rely on an unannounced later-2027 session.

Scenario 4: waiting for a Part 1 result

A future Part 2 booking window may open before your Part 1 result is released. You cannot treat a future Part 2 seat as available to you until you are eligible. Plan around confirmed result timing and published booking windows rather than assuming an immediate progression.

Common booking mistakes

  • Using a calculated March/April booking date as if the GDC announced it.
  • Logging in on multiple devices.
  • Trying to book without the £6,967 payment plan ready.
  • Ignoring a separate priority email.
  • Assuming 200 advertised places means 200 general-public seats after priority allocation.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before the seat is confirmed.
  • Counting the five-year rule from the wrong date.
  • Assuming another Part 2 sitting will appear later in 2027.
