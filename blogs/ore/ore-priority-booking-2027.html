ORE Priority Booking 2027: Five-Year Limit & Refugee Arrangements

Priority booking for the Overseas Registration Examination (ORE) is a targeted allocation mechanism operated by the General Dental Council (GDC) before standard first-come, first-served booking windows open. Because general ORE places frequently fill within 30 minutes of opening, priority access exists to protect two specific groups of candidates: dentists approaching their statutory five-year limit to pass ORE Part 2, and dental professionals with verified UK refugee or humanitarian protection status.

No separate "2027 Priority Booking Policy" document has been published by the GDC. Instead, bookings for the confirmed 2027 sittings are governed by the GDC's current ORE guidance (About the ORE, ORE booking dates, Booking your exam, and Information for refugee and displaced dental professionals), alongside the GDC's 6 May 2026 contract announcement confirming 2026/27 sittings and fees under the General Dental Council (Dentists) (Fees) Regulations 2026.

Quick Answers

  • Who qualifies for ORE priority booking? Only two groups: (1) candidates approaching the five-year time limit who may only have a few sittings left before their deadline expires, and (2) candidates on the ORE candidate list with verified Home Office refugee or eligible protection status.
  • When does the five-year clock start? On the exact date you first attempt ORE Part 1—not when you apply, join the candidate list, or pass Part 1. You must pass ORE Part 2 within five years of that first Part 1 sitting.
  • How many priority attempts do refugee candidates receive? Eligible refugee candidates may request priority booking for up to two attempts at Part 1 and up to two attempts at Part 2. These sit inside—not on top of—the standard maximum of four attempts per part.
  • How is priority communicated? The GDC emails eligible priority candidates separately in advance of the general booking window with specific instructions on how to secure a place.
  • How long do priority candidates have to pay? Priority candidates must pay the full examination fee within 48 hours of securing a priority place (compared with 1 hour in standard general booking).
  • Do priority candidates pay reduced fees? No. Current 2026/27 fees apply equally to priority and general candidates: £485 for Part 1, £6,967 for Part 2, and £686 for a Medical Emergencies retake.

What Is Confirmed for 2027 vs. What Has Not Yet Been Published

To avoid relying on assumptions or outdated prep-forum rumours, it is essential to separate confirmed GDC policy for 2027 from administrative details that have not yet been published.

Officially Confirmed for 2027

  • Contract Year 1 Sittings (UCL Consultants Ltd): Following its March 2026 contract award to UCL Consultants Ltd, the GDC confirmed two Part 1 sittings and three Part 2 sittings in early 2027:
  • ORE Part 1: 2–3 February 2027 (600 places) and 6–7 April 2027 (600 places).
  • ORE Part 2: 21–24 January 2027 (200 places), 4–7 March 2027 (200 places), and 15–18 April 2027 (200 places).
  • Priority Policy Continuation: In its 6 May 2026 announcement confirming those sittings, the GDC explicitly reaffirmed that candidates nearing the five-year limit for Part 2 and candidates with refugee status remain the groups offered priority access to exam places.
  • Current Fees: The updated fees introduced under the General Dental Council (Dentists) (Fees) Regulations 2026 (£115 application fee, £485 Part 1 fee, £6,967 Part 2 fee, £686 Medical Emergencies retake fee) apply across the 2026/27 contract year, with the GDC stating that the Part 2 fee was set with a view to remaining broadly stable over the next five years.
  • Planned 2027 Candidate Portal: The GDC has announced development of a new candidate portal and revised booking system for 2027 exams, but candidates should continue following current MyGDC/GDC instructions until a formal launch and migration process is published.

Not Officially Published by the GDC

  • No Fixed Priority Seat Quota: The GDC does not publish a guaranteed number or percentage of priority seats reserved per sitting.
  • No Public Month-by-Month Cutoff Rule: While prep guides sometimes cite informal month counts, the GDC's public guidance does not state that every candidate entering their fifth year automatically receives a seat on a specific sitting. Public GDC wording refers to candidates "approaching the five-year time limit" and "those who may only have a few sittings available to them before their time limit expires".
  • Later 2027 Sittings and Portal Workflows: Sittings beyond April 2027 and exact technical instructions for how priority booking will operate inside the forthcoming 2027 candidate portal have not yet been published on the GDC ORE booking dates page.

The Five-Year ORE Completion Rule and When the Clock Starts

Under GDC regulations, you must pass ORE Part 2 within five years of first attempting ORE Part 1. Alongside that clock, you are permitted a maximum of four attempts at Part 1 and four attempts at Part 2. All attempts across both parts must be completed within that single five-year window.

Exact Start Point of the Five-Year Clock

Candidates frequently miscalculate their expiry date by starting from the wrong milestone. Under official GDC rules:

  1. Application submission date: Does not start the clock.
  2. Application approval / candidate list addition date: Does not start the clock (the GDC notes application processing takes around 60 working days).
  3. Date you first attempt ORE Part 1: Starts the five-year clock. This applies whether you pass or fail that first Part 1 sitting.
  4. Date you pass ORE Part 1: Does not reset or restart the clock.
  5. Date you pass ORE Part 2: Stops the clock. Once you pass ORE Part 2, there is no time limit for submitting your application for GDC registration.

Priority Booking for Candidates Approaching the Five-Year Limit

Why the GDC Offers Five-Year Priority Access

Securing an ORE place through standard booking is highly competitive. The GDC notes that all places have been taken within 30 minutes of a booking window opening, and new applicants may struggle to secure a place over a 12-month period or longer. If a candidate who passed Part 1 years earlier had to compete purely on browser speed for their final Part 2 sittings, booking congestion could cause their statutory five-year limit to expire before they ever had a fair opportunity to sit the practical exam. Priority booking exists specifically to give time-pressured candidates an opportunity to complete ORE Part 2 before their five-year window closes.

How the GDC Communicates Priority Access

Priority booking for approaching-deadline candidates is initiated by the regulator rather than through a public self-booking button:

  • Advance Individual Email: According to the GDC's About the ORE and ORE booking dates pages, priority candidates are notified in advance of the general booking window opening via a separate email containing instructions on how to secure a place.
  • Pre-General Allocation: Priority places are allocated before the general booking window opens at 14:30 UK time.
  • No Automatic Seat Assignment: Receiving a priority invitation means you are offered an advance booking route—it does not mean you are enrolled onto a sitting without responding to the GDC's instructions and paying within the deadline.

Refugee-Status Priority Access: Eligibility, Attempt Caps, and Verification

In January 2025, the GDC introduced a dedicated priority booking policy for dental professionals with refugee status to remove unnecessary barriers for dentists who cannot return to their home country to practise. This policy continues into 2027.

Who Qualifies Under GDC Refugee Guidance

According to the GDC's Information for refugee and displaced dental professionals guidance, you may qualify for ORE priority booking if the UK Home Office has granted you:

  • Refugee status under the 1951 United Nations Convention
  • Humanitarian protection
  • Indefinite leave to remain or enter (with refugee status)
  • UK resettlement scheme status, such as the Afghanistan or Ukraine resettlement schemes

(Note: Displaced dentists who lack primary qualification or regulator documents due to conflict or displacement can also ask the GDC to assess alternative evidence on a case-by-case basis during registration/eligibility review, though ORE priority booking specifically requires verified Home Office immigration status.)

The Two-Attempt Priority Cap per Exam Part

The GDC sets a clear limit on how many times refugee priority access can be used:

  • ORE Part 1: Priority booking access for up to two attempts.
  • ORE Part 2: Priority booking access for up to two attempts.

Crucially, these priority attempts count inside the standard four-attempt maximum per part. If an eligible refugee candidate uses both priority attempts on Part 1 without passing, they remain eligible for a third and fourth Part 1 attempt (provided they are still within their five-year window), but those third and fourth attempts must be booked through the standard general booking window.

When Refugee Evidence Is Checked—and Re-Verification Rules

The GDC separates ORE eligibility processing from refugee priority verification:

  1. During Initial ORE Application: You do not need to submit evidence of your refugee status when submitting your initial ORE application.
  2. After Joining the Candidate List: Once your ORE application is approved and you are added to the ORE candidate list, contact the GDC Examinations team (examinations@gdc-uk.org) to notify them of your circumstances and request priority booking access.
  3. Document Verification Before Booking: Before the GDC can organise a priority booking, you must submit acceptable proof of status:
  • Option A: A screenshot of your eVisa with a share code, OR
  • Option B: A photo of the front and back of your Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) plus a letter from the Home Office and a share code.
  1. No Repeat Submission Once Verified: The GDC explicitly confirms on ORE booking dates: "If we have already verified your refugee status, you will not need to share the documentation again. If you are yet to provide the necessary documents, we will need to verify them before we can organise the booking."

The 48-Hour Priority Payment Rule (vs. 1 Hour in General Booking)

One of the most important operational differences for priority candidates is the payment deadline:

  • Priority Booking Payment Window: Once you are successful in booking a priority place, you must complete payment within 48 hours.
  • General Booking Payment Window: In standard first-come, first-served online booking, payment must be made at the time of booking and will not be processed if not completed within 1 hour.

The GDC specifically advises that if a third party (such as a family member, employer, or sponsor) will be paying on your behalf, you must make sure they are aware of the 48-hour payment timeframe in advance.

Charity and Refugee Council Cheque Payments

Under GDC rules (Booking your exam and ORE booking dates), cheque bookings are available exclusively to candidates sponsored by a refugee council or charity. If a charity or refugee organisation is paying your exam fee:

  • Notify the GDC Examinations team (+44 (0)20 7167 6050) as soon as the booking window opening date is announced (before general booking opens) so they can confirm the exact sponsorship and cheque procedure.
  • Ensure your refugee status documents have already been submitted and verified, as the GDC cannot finalise a sponsored priority booking without verifying status first.

How General Booking Works After Priority Places Are Allocated

Priority booking operates as the first stage of each sitting's capacity allocation. Once priority places have been allocated to eligible five-year-limit and refugee candidates, all remaining seats are released to the general candidate list on a strict first-come, first-served basis.

Under current GDC booking procedures (see ORE Booking System 2027 and ORE Exam Dates 2027):

  • Eight-Week Advance Window: GDC says ORE sittings open for booking eight weeks in advance. The exact opening time must be taken from the official booking notice; for example, the 21–24 January 2027 Part 2 sitting opens on Tuesday 24 November 2026 at 14:30 UK time.
  • Pre-Opening Visibility: The sitting appears in the bookings tab as 'unavailable' eight hours before opening alongside the scheduled opening time. Candidates already logged in at 14:30 UK time must refresh their browser.
  • Single-Session Controls: You can log in on only one device at a time; opening a second device or browser window automatically logs you out, and 15 minutes of inactivity also triggers automatic logout.
  • Immediate Payment and Penalty Rules: General-booking candidates must pay within 1 hour. Selecting 'book exam and pay' without intending to sit or pay results in being blocked from booking the next available sitting.
  • Reopened Places: If booked candidates later withdraw, the GDC reopens the booking window to all eligible candidates and announces availability by email and on the ORE booking dates page.
  • 2027 Portal Transition: Candidates booking in 2027 should watch GDC emails closely for instructions on the new candidate portal and revised booking system being introduced under the UCL Consultants contract.

For sitting-specific booking tactics, see ORE Part 1 Booking 2027 and ORE Part 2 Booking 2027.

What Priority Booking Does Not Change

A common misconception is that priority status functions as an academic or regulatory concession. In reality, priority booking is strictly an administrative queue-management policy:

  1. No Waiver of Primary Eligibility: Every candidate must still satisfy all GDC overseas dentist qualification, clinical experience, character/good standing, and English language requirements before joining the candidate list.
  2. No Extra Attempts Beyond Four per Part: Failing either Part 1 or Part 2 four times permanently ends ORE eligibility, regardless of whether any attempts were booked via priority access.
  3. No Extension of the Five-Year Limit: Priority booking helps you access a sitting before your five-year deadline expires; it does not pause, suspend, or extend the five-year statutory period.
  4. No Change to Exam Standards: As the GDC stated when launching refugee priority booking, priority candidates must meet the exact same clinical knowledge and practical standards to pass the ORE and join the register.
  5. No Guarantee of Passing on a Tight Timeline: Securing a priority seat puts you in the exam room, but passing ORE Part 2—especially given the £6,967 sitting fee—requires full clinical readiness before you accept a priority booking.

  • Recommended Action: Because general booking for the 21–24 January 2027 sitting opens on 24 November 2026, Dr A should have already received priority booking contact well ahead of 24 November 2026. If she has not, she must contact examinations@gdc-uk.org and call +44 (0)20 7167 6050 immediately, ensure £6,967 is ready in a cleared account to pay within 48 hours of booking, and treat January 2027 as her final statutory opportunity.

Scenario 2: Candidate Approaching the Five-Year Limit Later in 2027

  • Profile: Dr B first sat ORE Part 1 on 18 August 2022. His five-year window expires on 18 August 2027 (DentAIstudy calculation). He passed Part 1 and has not yet sat Part 2.
  • 2027 Reality: Across confirmed early-2027 Part 2 sittings (21–24 January, 4–7 March, and 15–18 April 2027), Dr B has multiple potential windows before August 2027. However, if he fails his first Part 2 attempt in January or March 2027, he will need a rapid retake before 18 August 2027.
  • Recommended Action: Do not wait passively until summer 2027. Track the booking cycle for every Part 2 sitting from January 2027 onward, verify with the GDC Examinations team when your file enters their approaching-deadline priority cohort, and keep £6,967 liquid so a 48-hour payment window never causes a missed seat.

  • Profile: Dr C holds UK refugee status (with a Home Office eVisa and share code). Her ORE application was approved in October 2026, and she is now on the ORE Part 1 candidate list aiming for the 2–3 February 2027 or 6–7 April 2027 Part 1 sitting.
  • 2027 Reality: Because refugee status is not checked during the initial ORE application, the GDC will not automatically know she qualifies for refugee priority booking until she contacts them.
  • Recommended Action: Email examinations@gdc-uk.org immediately (well before the 8-week general booking window opens for February 2027), request priority booking under the refugee policy, and submit her eVisa screenshot and share code when instructed so verification is completed before priority invitations close.

Scenario 4: Previously Verified Refugee Candidate Sponsored by a Charity for Part 2

  • Profile: Dr D already submitted his BRP, Home Office letter, and share code for a previous priority sitting. He passed Part 1 on his second priority attempt and now wants to use his first Part 2 priority attempt in 2027, with a UK refugee charity paying the £6,967 Part 2 fee by cheque.
  • 2027 Reality: Under GDC rules, Dr D does not need to re-submit his refugee status documents because the GDC has already verified them. However, cheque payment requires prior coordination with the GDC Examinations team.
  • Recommended Action: Contact +44 (0)20 7167 6050 as soon as the GDC announces the booking window date for his target Part 2 sitting, confirm the charity cheque instructions, and ensure the sponsoring charity can execute payment within the GDC's required timeframe.

Common Mistakes That Cost Candidates Priority Access

  1. Assuming Refugee Status Is Automatically Flagged From Your Application: The GDC does not collect refugee proof during initial ORE registration. If you do not contact the GDC after being added to the candidate list, you will remain in the standard general-booking pool.
  2. Calculating Your Five-Year Deadline From Your Part 1 Pass Date: Starting your countdown from the day you passed Part 1—rather than the day you first attempted Part 1—can miscalculate your deadline by a year or more if you needed resits.
  3. Waiting Until General Booking Day to Raise a Priority Query: Priority places are allocated before general booking opens. Emailing the GDC on the morning of general booking is too late for that sitting's priority allocation.
  4. Missing the 48-Hour Payment Deadline: Securing a priority place is conditional on paying the full fee within 48 hours. International bank transfers, daily card limits, or unbriefed third-party payers frequently cause avoidable cancellations.
  5. Booking a Priority Seat Before Being Clinically Ready: With Part 2 costing £6,967 per attempt and refugee priority capped at two attempts per part, using a priority slot as a "practice run" wastes both money and scarce priority entitlement.

What to Do If You Qualify but Have Not Received GDC Priority Instructions

If you believe you meet the GDC criteria for five-year-limit priority or refugee priority booking and have not received separate priority instructions ahead of an upcoming sitting, follow this sequence immediately:

  1. Verify Your Exact Eligibility Record: Log into MyGDC and check the exact date of your first ORE Part 1 attempt (to confirm your five-year anniversary date) or verify that your Home Office refugee/protection status matches the GDC's accepted categories.
  2. Audit Your Email Inbox and Spam Filters: Search all folders for emails from @gdc-uk.org, as priority invitations and submission deadlines are sent by email in advance of general booking. Confirm your registered email address in MyGDC is active.
  3. Contact the GDC Examinations Team Directly:
  • Email: examinations@gdc-uk.org (include your GDC reference/candidate number, full name, and whether your query concerns approaching the five-year Part 2 deadline or refugee priority access).
  • Telephone (ORE Examinations Team): +44 (0)20 7167 6050.
  • Telephone (GDC General Enquiries): +44 (0)20 7167 6000.
  1. Prepare Documents and Funds in Advance: Have your eVisa/BRP share code ready if refugee verification is still pending, and ensure £485 (Part 1) or £6,967 (Part 2) is immediately available so you can settle payment within the 48-hour priority window as soon as an offer is issued.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Does every candidate in their fifth year automatically get a priority seat for ORE Part 2?

No. The GDC provides priority places for candidates approaching the five-year time limit who may only have a few sittings available before their deadline expires, and notifies eligible candidates separately by email before general booking opens. However, the GDC does not publish a guaranteed seat quota per sitting, and candidates must still respond to GDC instructions and pay within 48 hours to confirm a place.

2. Can a refugee candidate get priority booking for all four attempts at Part 1 and Part 2?

No. Under official GDC rules, eligible candidates with refugee status can request priority booking access for up to two attempts at Part 1 and up to two attempts at Part 2. If you still have remaining attempts after using two priority bookings on a part (up to the maximum of four attempts per part), subsequent attempts must be booked through standard general booking unless you qualify under the five-year Part 2 deadline priority policy.

3. If the GDC verified my refugee status for Part 1, do I need to send my eVisa or BRP again for Part 2?

No. The GDC's ORE booking dates guidance explicitly states that if the GDC has already verified your refugee status, you do not need to share the documentation again. You still need to follow the GDC's pre-sitting instructions to confirm you wish to book a priority place.

4. How long do priority candidates have to pay the exam fee compared to general candidates?

Priority candidates have 48 hours after booking a priority place to complete payment. By contrast, candidates booking during the standard first-come, first-served general window must pay at the time of booking and lose their reservation if payment is not completed within 1 hour.

5. Can the GDC extend my five-year deadline if I could not book an ORE sitting earlier?

No. The GDC states unequivocally that candidates must pass ORE Part 2 within five years of first attempting ORE Part 1. Priority booking is the mechanism the GDC uses to help time-pressured candidates access a sitting before expiry—it does not extend the five-year statutory window.
