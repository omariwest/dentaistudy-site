ORE Exam Fees 2027: Part 1, Part 2 and Total Exam Costs

Planning your budget for the General Dental Council (GDC) Overseas Registration Exam (ORE) requires understanding how fees are structured under the GDC's delivery contract with UCL Consultants Ltd , signed in March 2026. Under the revised fee schedule governing the first contract year (31 May 2026–30 May 2027), the currently published ORE exam fees 2027 follow a full-cost-recovery model that reflects the addition of Value Added Tax (VAT) and updated clinical delivery expenses.

Quick Answers: 2027 ORE Exam Fees & Payment Rules

How much is the ORE application processing fee?

(Official GDC Fact) The initial application processing fee is £115 (increased by 20% from £96) and is strictly non-refundable.

How much is the ORE Part 1 exam fee?

(Official GDC Fact) Each sitting of ORE Part 1 costs £485 (reduced by 17% from £584).

How much is the ORE Part 2 exam fee?

(Official GDC Fact) Each full sitting of ORE Part 2 costs £6,967 (increased by 65% from £4,235).

How much is the Medical Emergencies-only retake fee?

(Official GDC Fact) If you pass the Dental Manikin, OSCE, and Diagnostic & Treatment Planning exercises in Part 2 and fail only the Medical Emergencies (ME) exercise, the standalone ME retake fee is £686.

What is the minimum GDC ORE exam cost if I pass everything on my first attempt?

(DentAIstudy Calculation, Not an Official GDC Quoted Bundle) Adding the three mandatory stage fees together (£115 + £485 + £6,967) produces a minimum baseline of £7,567, excluding pre-application document checks, travel, accommodation, and post-exam GDC registration fees.

Are ORE exam fees refundable if I withdraw or cannot get a UK visa?

(Official GDC Fact) ORE examination fees are not normally refundable. The GDC explicitly states that being unable to obtain a UK visa does not qualify as an exceptional circumstance for a refund.

While the ORE Part 1 fee decreased by 17% to £485, the ORE Part 2 fee rose by 65% to £6,967, making Part 2 the largest single financial commitment on the UK dental registration pathway. Why Part 2 Increased Under the UCL Consultants Contract (Official Context)

The provider, VAT and capacity changes behind the current fee structure are covered in ORE 2027 Changes: UCL Provider, Capacity and Booking.

For practical sitting capacity and booking timing, use ORE Part 2 Booking 2027. Candidates near the five-year limit should also read ORE Priority Booking 2027.

In its May 6, 2026 announcement confirming sittings and fees under the new contract with UCL Consultants Ltd, the GDC explained three drivers behind the revised fee structure:

  1. Full Cost Recovery Policy: Since January 2025, the GDC sets ORE fees to recover the full operational cost of delivering the examinations rather than subsidising them from annual registrant retention fees.
  2. Application of VAT: Under the commercial delivery structure with UCL Consultants Ltd, VAT now applies to the ORE and is built directly into the published fee structure.
  3. Expanded Clinical Delivery at UCL Eastman Dental Institute: Part 2 is delivered in person at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, Rockefeller Building, London, with expanded capacity of 200 places per sitting across sittings from November 2026 through April 2027 (up from 144 places in September 2026).

Important Fee Stability Notice: The GDC stated in May 2026 that the £6,967 Part 2 fee was set with a view to remaining broadly stable over the five-year contract term. However, published sittings on the GDC website currently run through April 2027 (the end of Contract Year 1 on 30 May 2027). Statutory fees remain subject to periodic review, so candidates booking later 2027 sittings must always verify live fee figures on gdc-uk.org and inside MyGDC before paying.

Booking Payment Rules: Why Full Funds & Priority Status Matter

Booking an ORE sitting through your MyGDC account operates on strict real-time payment rules:

  1. Full Examination Fee Required at Booking: Under GDC booking policy, you must pay the entire examination fee (£485 or £6,967) online at the moment you book your place. You cannot reserve a seat with a deposit or pay in instalments. (Cheque bookings are restricted exclusively to candidates sponsored by a refugee council or charity who arrange the procedure with the GDC before the booking window opens.)
  2. Prepare Your Payment Card Before the Window Opens: Booking windows typically open eight weeks before each sitting, and places are allocated strictly first-come, first-served. If your bank blocks a £6,967 international or online transaction during checkout, your seat is not held while you resolve the decline. Review our ORE Part 1 Booking 2027 and ORE Part 2 Booking 2027 guides for checkout preparation steps.
  3. Priority Booking Does NOT Mean Discounted Fees: The GDC grants priority booking access to candidates nearing the five-year time limit for Part 2 and to candidates with refugee status. Priority access only allows eligible candidates to book ahead of the general queue—priority candidates pay the exact same statutory fees (£485 for Part 1; £6,967 for Part 2).

What Is and Is Not Refundable: GDC Withdrawal & Refund Rules

Because the GDC commits candidate numbers to the examination provider weeks before each sitting, its withdrawal and refund rules are significantly stricter than those of most North American or Australian dental boards.

Application Processing Fee (£115)Covers qualification, clinical experience, and English checks.0% (Strictly non-refundable).
Withdrawal Before Name Submission (~5 weeks before exam)You submit the official withdrawal form to the GDC Examinations team before your name is submitted to the examination board and the GDC successfully places a replacement candidate in your seat.Discretionary refund possible only if a replacement candidate fills your place; £0 if no replacement is found.
Withdrawal After Name Submission (within ~5 weeks of exam)Once your name is submitted to the examination board, the GDC incurs full delivery liability.0% (No refund).
Inability to Obtain a UK VisaThe GDC explicitly states candidates are responsible for ensuring legal entry to the UK before booking.0% (Does NOT qualify as an exceptional circumstance).
Severe, Unforeseen Exceptional Circumstances (e.g., serious illness or death of a close family member)Assessed case-by-case upon submitting a single complete ORE Refund Form with independent medical/death documentation, proof a valid UK visa was or could have been obtained, and flight/hotel bookings.Discretionary refund (decisions issued within 10 working days; approved refunds take up to 3 weeks).
Failing an Exam or Disagreeing With ExaminersNo appeals against results or examiner decisions are permitted.0% (Must pay full fee again to resit).

Separate Candidate Costs That Are NOT GDC ORE Exam Fees

When planning your overall budget for UK dental registration, keep GDC ORE examination fees separate from four external expense categories. For a complete multi-category investment model, see our Full ORE Cost Breakdown & Total Investment Guide.

  1. Pre-Application Eligibility & Verification Costs:
  • UK ENIC Statement of Comparability verifying your primary dental degree.
  • English Language Proficiency Testing (IELTS Academic or OET Dentistry, unless exempt)—plus retake fees if your score expires before application approval.
  • Certificates of Current Professional Status (CCPS / Good Standing) from your home licensing authority, certified document copies, and official English translations.
  1. UK Visa, Travel & London Accommodation:
  • All ORE sittings take place in person in London—Part 1 at designated London computer-testing centres (such as Teamco on Gray's Inn Road, Escalla in Kentish Town, or Tobacco Dock in Wapping) and Part 2 over four days at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, Rockefeller Building.
  • Candidates living outside London or overseas must fund UK visitor visa fees, international/domestic flights or rail fares, multiple nights of central London accommodation, and daily meals—every time they sit or resit an exam.
  1. Optional Study Materials & Practical Simulation Courses:
  • Textbooks, UK clinical guidelines, online question banks, and hands-on dental manikin practice courses or home compressor/typodont setups (note that the GDC does not endorse any commercial preparation course).
  1. Post-ORE GDC Registration & Annual Retention Fees (ARF):
  • Passing ORE Part 2 makes you eligible to apply for full registration with the GDC, which carries a separate GDC registration application fee and the statutory dentist Annual Retention Fee (ARF).

Common Financial & Booking Mistakes Candidates Make

  1. Budgeting Only for Part 1 (£485) Without Planning for Part 2 (£6,967): Because Part 1 is now £485, some candidates underestimate the 14-fold jump in examination fees required once they pass Part 1 and compete for a Part 2 seat.
  2. Booking a Sitting Before Securing a UK Visa: Because visa refusal or processing delays are explicitly excluded from the GDC's exceptional circumstances refund policy, booking a £6,967 Part 2 seat without confirmed UK entry permission risks losing the entire fee.
  3. Triggering a Bank Fraud Decline on Booking Morning: Trying to pay £6,967 online in seconds when booking opens without pre-notifying your card issuer often results in a blocked transaction and a lost seat.
  4. Submitting an Incomplete Exceptional Circumstances Refund Claim: Under GDC refund rules, you can only apply once and cannot submit extra documents after your initial email—missing proof of travel arrangements or visa eligibility results in automatic refusal.
  5. Rushing an Unprepared Part 2 Attempt: At £6,967 per full sitting—and with a strict four-attempt cap—treating an early Part 2 sitting as a "practice run" is one of the most expensive mistakes on the pathway.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What are the current GDC ORE exam fees for 2027?

(Official GDC Fact) Under the GDC's 2026/27 fee regulations for the UCL Consultants Ltd contract, the ORE application processing fee is £115, ORE Part 1 is £485, ORE Part 2 is £6,967, and a Medical Emergencies-only retake is £686. Because published contract sittings currently run through April 2027 and fees are subject to statutory review, always verify live figures on gdc-uk.org before paying.

2. Why did the ORE Part 2 fee increase to £6,967?

(Official GDC Fact) In May 2026, the GDC confirmed that Part 2 increased from £4,235 to £6,967 under its new delivery contract with UCL Consultants Ltd at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute. Since January 2025, ORE fees have been set on a full-cost-recovery basis, and VAT now applies to the ORE and is reflected in the fee structure.

3. How much does the ORE cost in total if I pass Part 1 and Part 2 on the first try?

Candidates budgeting for the written stage should pair the fee with ORE Part 1 Booking 2027, because the fee is paid when the seat is booked.

(DentAIstudy Calculation) Adding the £115 application processing fee, £485 Part 1 fee, and £6,967 Part 2 fee totals £7,567 in GDC ORE examination fees. This calculated baseline does not include UK ENIC, English testing, UK visas, London travel/accommodation, preparation materials, or post-ORE GDC registration and Annual Retention Fees.

4. Do candidates with priority booking pay a different fee?

(Official GDC Fact + Policy) No. While candidates nearing the five-year Part 2 time limit and candidates with refugee status receive priority booking access, all candidates pay the exact same statutory fees (£485 for Part 1 and £6,967 for Part 2).

5. Can I get a refund if I fail ORE Part 1 or Part 2, or if my UK visa is delayed?

(Official GDC Fact) No. Examination fees are not refunded if you fail an exam, and the GDC explicitly states that being unable to obtain a UK visa does not constitute an exceptional circumstance. Refunds are only considered if you withdraw before your name is submitted to the examination board (~5 weeks pre-exam) and the GDC finds a replacement candidate, or if you prove severe, unforeseen exceptional circumstances such as serious illness or bereavement.
