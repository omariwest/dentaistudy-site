ORE Part 1 Booking 2027: February/April Dates and Exam Seats

The General Dental Council has confirmed two ORE Part 1 sittings in early 2027: 2–3 February 2027 and 6–7 April 2027, with 600 places per sitting. The important complication is booking. The GDC says ORE sittings open around eight weeks in advance and that demand is extremely high, but as of 17 November 2026 it had not yet published the exact opening date or time for either 2027 Part 1 sitting.

This guide focuses on the booking decision: when the sittings happen, what you need before the window opens, how MyGDC booking works, what happens if a session fills, and how to avoid treating an estimate as an official booking date.

Quick Answers

  1. Confirmed Part 1 dates: 2–3 February 2027 and 6–7 April 2027.
  2. Capacity: the GDC has confirmed 600 places per sitting.
  3. Booking opening: the GDC says all ORE sittings open for booking about eight weeks in advance, but the exact February and April 2027 Part 1 opening timestamps were not yet published on 17 November 2026.
  4. Fee: the current Part 1 fee is £485, paid when you book.
  5. Booking system: candidates book through their GDC online account after successfully completing the ORE application process and being placed on the Part 1 candidate list.
  6. If the sitting is full: the GDC can reopen booking if withdrawals release places; candidates are notified by email and updates appear on the ORE booking-dates page.

Who can book ORE Part 1?

You can book Part 1 after the GDC has successfully completed your ORE application process and confirmed that you have been added to the ORE Part 1 candidate list.

That means the booking window is not the time to begin the application itself. The GDC notes that ORE application processing can take around 60 working days, so candidates who have not yet reached the candidate list should not assume they can apply shortly before a booking window and still compete for a seat.

Once you are on the candidate list:

  • keep access to your GDC online account working;
  • monitor the email address connected to your account;
  • check the official ORE booking-dates page;
  • have the full examination fee ready before the booking window opens.

The ORE also has an overall five-year rule: you must complete the ORE within five years of first attempting Part 1. The GDC allows up to four attempts at Part 1 and up to four attempts at Part 2.

How Part 1 booking works

1. Watch for the official opening notice

The GDC gives advance notice of ORE booking windows by email and publishes the booking information on its website.

For the February and April 2027 Part 1 sittings, the exam dates and capacities are confirmed, but the exact booking timestamps were not published at our last verification.

Do not rely on social-media screenshots or a date calculated by counting back eight weeks.

2. Log in through one device

The GDC's booking guidance says candidates should log in using one device at a time. Attempting to sign in from another device or another browser window can log you out.

The system may show an upcoming exam as “unavailable” before the opening time. If you are already logged in when the window opens, the GDC advises refreshing the browser.

3. Select the sitting and pay

The Part 1 fee is £485.

The GDC says the full examination fee must be paid at booking. Its booking guidance warns candidates not to select “book exam and pay” unless they genuinely intend to sit and can pay.

General bookings that remain unpaid are not processed. The live booking instructions should be followed exactly because the GDC can change operational details between sittings.

4. Save confirmation before arranging travel

Do not treat a successful login or a visible booking option as a confirmed seat. Complete payment and save the confirmation in your account before making non-refundable travel plans.

Why seats disappear quickly

ORE demand remains much higher than available short-term capacity. The GDC says ORE places have been taken very quickly when booking windows open and that it cannot guarantee a place for everyone trying to book.

The new contract has increased capacity. Part 1 now has 600 places per sitting, and the first contract year provides 2,400 Part 1 places in total. That is a material improvement, but it does not remove the need to be ready when booking opens.

What “fully booked” means

When all places have been taken, the booking system can display the session as closed or full. An error during a high-demand opening can also mean that the session has just filled.

If you cannot secure a place:

  • do not repeatedly create payment attempts you do not intend to complete;
  • monitor the booking-dates page;
  • keep checking your email;
  • be ready if the GDC announces a reopened window after withdrawals.

The GDC explicitly says it can reopen booking when seats become free through withdrawals.

Part 1 venue and exam format

Part 1 is computer-based and consists of two three-hour papers.

  • Paper A covers dental science and human disease.
  • Paper B covers clinical dentistry, including law, ethics, health and safety.

The GDC currently lists three London venues used for Part 1:

  • Teamco, 222 Gray's Inn Road, London;
  • Escalla, 8 Holmes Road, Kentish Town, London;
  • Tobacco Dock, Wapping Lane, London.

The exam provider allocates the venue and contacts candidates with the details. Do not assume that choosing a February or April sitting lets you choose one of those buildings yourself.

For format and preparation details, see ORE Part 1 Structure and Content.

Priority booking: who should expect it?

The GDC provides priority arrangements for some candidates, but the rules are not identical for everyone.

Candidates with refugee status may request priority access for up to two attempts at Part 1 and two attempts at Part 2, subject to the GDC's policy and verification.

Candidates approaching the five-year ORE time limit are also given priority access in circumstances described by the GDC, especially where remaining Part 2 opportunities are limited.

If you believe you qualify for priority access, do not assume the normal public window is your only route. Follow the specific email and instructions sent by the GDC.

Fees, withdrawal and refunds

The current Part 1 fee is £485.

ORE examination fees are not normally refundable. If you need to withdraw, review both the GDC withdrawal policy and refund policy before doing so. The GDC notes that medical circumstances can be relevant to refund decisions.

This is an area where candidates should avoid relying on old forum posts, because the provider, fee structure and booking system changed under the 2026 contract.

Candidate planning examples

Scenario 1: already on the Part 1 candidate list

You are eligible and want the February sitting. Your job is simple: make sure MyGDC works, watch for the official booking notice, have £485 available, and be online for the published opening time. Do not book travel until payment and booking confirmation are complete.

Scenario 2: application still being processed

You hope for February but are not yet on the Part 1 candidate list. The exam date itself does not make you eligible to book. Keep following your application status. If approval comes after the booking window has filled, April may become your next published Part 1 target.

Scenario 3: February is full

Do not assume April is your only remaining possibility immediately. The GDC can reopen February booking if withdrawals release places. Monitor official email and the booking-dates page while also preparing for the April window.

Scenario 4: you are close to the five-year limit

Count the time remaining from your first Part 1 attempt, not from your application date. If your remaining time is becoming critical, contact the GDC and follow any priority instructions sent to you. Do not build your plan around an unpublished later-2027 sitting.

Common booking mistakes

  • Treating “eight weeks” as an exact official date. It is a general GDC rule; the actual timestamp should come from the booking-dates page.
  • Using multiple devices. The GDC warns that this can log you out.
  • Waiting to find a payment method after selecting a seat. Be payment-ready before the window opens.
  • Assuming 600 places means easy availability. Demand remains very high.
  • Booking travel before confirmation. Your seat should be confirmed and paid first.
  • Ignoring email after a session fills. Reopened booking windows are announced when withdrawals free seats.
  • Using old fees. The current Part 1 fee under the new contract is £485.
