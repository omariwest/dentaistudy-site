ORE 2027 Changes: UCL Exam Provider, Capacity and New Booking

The General Dental Council (GDC) Overseas Registration Exam (ORE) entered a new operational era following the GDC's contract award to UCL Consultants Ltd (UCLC) in March 2026. For internationally qualified dentists preparing for ORE 2027 changes , the transition brings higher annual seat capacity, a dedicated London clinical venue for Part 2 at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute , a restructured VAT-inclusive fee schedule, online booking through MyGDC , and a planned new candidate portal under development.

Quick Answers: What Has Genuinely Changed for ORE 2027?

  • Who runs the ORE in 2027? (Active Change) UCL Consultants Ltd (UCLC) is the sole delivery provider for both ORE Part 1 and ORE Part 2 under a five-year contract signed with the GDC in March 2026 (Contract Year 1 runs from 31 May 2026 to 30 May 2027).
  • How much has seat capacity increased? (Active Change + Future Target) Contract Year 1 expands annual Part 1 capacity from 1,800 to 2,400 places (600 places per sitting, including 2–3 February 2027 and 6–7 April 2027) and annual Part 2 capacity from 720 to 944 places (200 places per sitting across 21–24 January 2027, 4–7 March 2027, and 15–18 April 2027). The GDC plans to scale Part 2 further to 1,500 places per year by Contract Year 3.
  • Where do ORE Part 1 and Part 2 take place in 2027? (Active Change) Part 1 is held at designated London computer-testing venues (Teamco on Gray's Inn Road, Escalla in Kentish Town, and Tobacco Dock in Wapping), while all Part 2 sittings take place at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, Rockefeller Building, London.
  • Why did ORE Part 2 increase to £6,967 while Part 1 dropped to £485? (Active Change) Under the General Dental Council (Dentists) (Fees) Regulations 2026, fees reflect full cost recovery plus VAT, which now applies to the ORE under the UCLC contract. Part 1 dropped 17% to £485, while Part 2 rose 65% to £6,967 (set with the intention of remaining broadly stable across the five-year contract).
  • Is the new candidate portal live yet for 2027 bookings? (Announced, Not Yet Implemented) Not yet. While the GDC announced in May 2026 that a new candidate portal and revised booking system are being developed for 2027 exams, active bookings—including the 24 November 2026 (14:30 UK time) booking opening for the 21–24 January 2027 Part 2 sitting—still run through MyGDC.
  • Have the four-attempt cap or five-year completion rule changed? (Unchanged) No. Candidates are still restricted to four attempts at Part 1, four attempts at Part 2, and a strict five-year window from first attempting Part 1 to passing Part 2.

Candidates affected by the five-year clock or refugee policy should use ORE Priority Booking 2027 rather than assuming the new contract removes those rules.

For internationally qualified dentists preparing for ORE 2027 changes, the transition brings higher annual seat capacity, a dedicated London clinical venue for Part 2 at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, a restructured VAT-inclusive fee schedule, online booking through MyGDC, and a planned new candidate portal under development.

At the same time, rumours across social media frequently blur what is already live with what is still in development—or falsely claim that core GDC eligibility and attempt limits have been relaxed. This guide separates changes already active, changes announced but not yet implemented, future multi-year capacity targets, and core statutory rules that have NOT changed. For sitting dates, fee breakdowns, and booking mechanics, see our ORE Exam Dates 2027, ORE Exam Fees 2027, and ORE Booking 2027 System Guide.

Status Classification: Active Changes vs. Pending Rollouts vs. Unchanged Rules

For the confirmed sitting dates, use ORE Exam Dates 2027. For the current fee structure, use ORE Exam Fees 2027.

To avoid acting on premature assumptions, distinguish clearly between what is operating today, what is scheduled for later rollout, and what remains legally fixed:

  1. Changes Already Active (Since May–August 2026):
  • Single Contract Provider (UCLC): UCL Consultants Ltd delivers both Part 1 and Part 2, ending the prior split-provider arrangement.
  • Higher Per-Sitting Capacities: 600 places per Part 1 sitting and 200 places per Part 2 sitting (from November 2026 through April 2027).
  • London Venue Consolidation: Part 2 consolidated at UCL Eastman Dental Institute (Rockefeller Building); Part 1 delivered across three central/east London computer-based testing centres.
  • Post-Part 1 Candidate Feedback: UCL Consultants provides structured candidate feedback approximately 21 working days after the GDC releases Part 1 results.
  • Revised Statutory Fees With VAT: £115 application fee, £485 Part 1 fee, £6,967 Part 2 fee, and £686 Medical Emergencies-only retake fee.
  • MyGDC Self-Service Booking & Priority Tiers: Candidates book directly via MyGDC (with priority access allocated ahead of the general queue for five-year-limit Part 2 candidates and verified refugee dental professionals).
  1. Changes Announced but Not Yet Implemented:
  • New Candidate Portal & Revised Booking System: Confirmed in May 2026 as under development with UCLC with the goal of launching for exams scheduled in 2027. As of November 18, 2026, the GDC has not published a launch date, migration instructions, or feature specifications for the replacement portal. The current live booking pages still direct candidates to the GDC online account, so continue using the live account instructions until the GDC announces a cutover.
  • Post-April 2027 Sitting Dates: The GDC has confirmed sittings through April 2027. Later 2027 sittings have not yet been published on the current Part 1/Part 2 pages.
  1. Future Multi-Year Capacity Targets:
  • Part 2 Expansion to 1,500 Places/Year: Alongside expanding Year 1 Part 2 places from 720 to 944, the GDC announced a multi-year target to reach 1,500 Part 2 places annually by Contract Year 3.
  1. Core Rules That Have NOT Changed:
  • Application & Eligibility Standards: UK ENIC statement of comparability, clinical experience references, and English language competence requirements remain unchanged.
  • Sequential Progression: You must pass Part 1 before being added to the Part 2 candidate list.
  • Four-Attempt Cap: Maximum four attempts at Part 1 and four attempts at Part 2.
  • Five-Year Completion Clock: You must still pass Part 2 within five years of your first Part 1 attempt.
  • Curriculum & Four-Component Part 2 Format: Both exams remain mapped to Preparing for practice, and Part 2 still comprises Dental Manikin (DM), OSCE, Diagnostic & Treatment Planning (DTP), and Medical Emergencies (ME).

Practical Implications for Candidates Preparing in 2027

1. Higher Capacity Improves Odds, Not Instant Availability

Expanding Part 1 to 600 seats per sitting and Part 2 to 200 seats per sitting measurably increases throughput across the year. However, because thousands of eligible candidates sit on the Part 1 and Part 2 candidate lists, sittings still fill rapidly once booking opens—often within 30 minutes. You still need exact UK-time readiness, a single logged-in device on MyGDC, and immediate card payment within the one-hour checkout window. Review our ORE Part 1 Booking 2027 and ORE Part 2 Booking 2027 guides for step-by-step checkout checklists.

2. Budgeting Around the £6,967 VAT-Inclusive Part 2 Fee

Because the £6,967 Part 2 fee reflects full cost recovery plus VAT—and because examination fees are not normally refundable once your name is submitted (~5 weeks pre-exam)—financial discipline matters more than ever:

  • Confirm your UK visitor visa pathway before booking a Part 2 place, as inability to obtain a UK visa is explicitly excluded from exceptional-circumstances refunds.
  • Prepare adequately before your first Part 2 attempt rather than treating an early sitting as a "trial run," since a full retake costs another £6,967 (whereas failing Medical Emergencies alone carries a £686 standalone retake fee).

3. Preparing for UCL Eastman Dental Institute Standards in Part 2

With all Part 2 sittings consolidated at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, Rockefeller Building, candidates benefit from consistent clinic infrastructure across every cohort. However, all four components (Dental Manikin, OSCE, Diagnostic & Treatment Planning, and Medical Emergencies) remain strictly mapped to GDC Preparing for practice outcomes—requiring clinical execution aligned with current UK standards (NICE, SDCEP, FGDP/CGDent, and GDC Standards for the Dental Team).

4. Navigating the MyGDC-to-New-Portal Transition Safely

The live seat workflow is covered in ORE Booking 2027; Part-specific booking pages are available for Part 1 and Part 2.

Until the GDC officially announces the launch date of the new candidate portal, treat MyGDC as your sole operational booking platform. Keep your MyGDC email address, password, and security details current, and watch official @gdc-uk.org announcements rather than unverified social-media posts claiming the new portal has already launched.

Common Mistakes: What Candidates Should NOT Assume in 2027

  1. Do NOT Assume Post-April 2027 Exam Dates Are Already Confirmed: Only Contract Year 1 sittings (running through April 2027) have been published as of November 18, 2026. Never build fixed travel or leave plans around unannounced summer or autumn 2027 dates.
  2. Do NOT Assume the New Candidate Portal Has Replaced MyGDC Yet: Active bookings (such as the 24 November 2026 opening for the 21–24 January 2027 Part 2 sitting) still take place in MyGDC.
  3. Do NOT Assume "200 Part 2 Places" Means Easy Last-Minute Booking: While 200 places per sitting is a major increase over 144, accumulated backlog demand means seats remain fiercely competitive on opening day.
  4. Do NOT Assume the Five-Year Limit or Four-Attempt Cap Was Reset: Transitioning to UCLC did not reset your five-year completion clock or grant extra attempts beyond four per part.
  5. Do NOT Assume Priority Booking Reduces Fees: Five-year-limit and refugee-status priority candidates receive earlier booking access, not reduced statutory fees.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. Who is the official ORE exam provider for 2027?

(Official GDC Fact) Following a contract signed in March 2026, UCL Consultants Ltd (UCLC) delivers both ORE Part 1 and ORE Part 2. Contract Year 1 runs from 31 May 2026 to 30 May 2027, with Part 1 held at London testing venues and Part 2 held at the UCL Eastman Dental Institute, Rockefeller Building.

2. How many ORE Part 1 and Part 2 places are available in 2027?

(Official GDC Fact) In Contract Year 1, the GDC confirmed 600 places per Part 1 sitting (including 2–3 February 2027 and 6–7 April 2027, part of 2,400 annual Year 1 places) and 200 places per Part 2 sitting across 21–24 January 2027, 4–7 March 2027, and 15–18 April 2027 (part of 944 annual Year 1 places). The GDC plans to expand Part 2 capacity further to 1,500 places per year by Contract Year 3.

3. Why did the ORE Part 2 fee increase to £6,967 under the new contract?

(Official GDC Fact) Under the General Dental Council (Dentists) (Fees) Regulations 2026, ORE fees are set on a full-cost-recovery basis and now include VAT, which applies to the ORE under the commercial delivery contract with UCL Consultants Ltd. Part 1 decreased to £485, while Part 2 rose to £6,967 with the aim of remaining broadly stable over the five-year contract term.

4. Is the new ORE candidate portal active for 2027 bookings?

(Official GDC Fact) As of November 18, 2026, candidates still book and pay for ORE sittings through their existing MyGDC account. While the GDC announced that a new candidate portal and revised booking system are being developed under the UCLC contract for 2027 exams, no launch date or feature details have been published yet.

5. Did the UCL Consultants contract change the five-year rule or four-attempt limit?

The underlying time-limit and attempt rules are explained in the existing ORE five-year deadline and attempts guide.

(Official GDC Fact) No. Candidates are still allowed a maximum of four attempts at ORE Part 1 and four attempts at ORE Part 2, and must still pass ORE Part 2 within five years of first attempting ORE Part 1.
