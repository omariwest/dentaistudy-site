ADC Initial Assessment 2027: Documents, Eligibility & Deadlines

The ADC initial assessment is the first stage for overseas-qualified dentists whose qualifications do not directly meet Australian registration requirements. It is not an exam. The Australian Dental Council reviews your identity and dental qualification to decide whether you can enter the written examination stage.

You can apply at any time, but candidates targeting a specific 2027 written sitting must work backward from the ADC's published initial-assessment deadlines. For the March 2027 written exam, the deadline was 11 October 2026. For the September 2027 written exam, the deadline is 11 April 2027.

Submitting by a deadline does not guarantee an exam place. Your initial assessment still needs to be completed successfully before the written-exam application window closes.

Quick Answers

  1. Basic eligibility: a minimum four-year full-time university dental degree or diploma from a recognised university.
  2. Current fee: $647 AUD for a new initial assessment.
  3. Approximate processing time: 8 weeks once the application can be assessed; incomplete documents can extend the timeline.
  4. Validity: a successful initial assessment is valid for 7 years.
  5. Renewal: current registration-only renewal fee is $35 AUD and must be completed before the existing assessment expires.
  6. 2027 deadlines: 11 October 2026 for the March 2027 written exam and 11 April 2027 for the September 2027 written exam.

What the Initial Assessment Does

The initial assessment determines whether your professional qualification is suitable to enter the ADC dental practitioner assessment process.

For general dentists, the pathway is:

Initial Assessment → Written Examination → Practical Examination

A successful initial assessment does not register you as a dentist in Australia and does not mean you have passed the ADC process. It makes you eligible to apply for the written examination while the assessment remains valid.

Eligibility

ADC states that a general dentist must hold a dental degree or diploma obtained after at least four years equivalent full-time academic study at a recognised higher-education provider.

If your qualification does not meet the required level or cannot be verified, the application may be unsuccessful.

The ADC also distinguishes a registration-focused initial assessment from a migration skills assessment. If your purpose is to complete the ADC pathway toward registration, choose the correct application type in ADC Connect.

Required Documents

The current ADC dentist initial-assessment page lists these core uploads:

  • current passport;
  • recent passport-style photograph;
  • evidence of legal name change, if required;
  • dental qualification, official certificate or testamur;
  • academic transcript;
  • internship certificate, if internship formed part of your qualification.

The current checklist also expects your ADC Connect profile details to match your legal identity and asks for clear, readable files.

Degree certificate

Use the final official qualification. ADC FAQs state that provisional degree documents are not accepted as a replacement for the completed certificate.

Academic transcript

Your transcript should show the subjects and marks/grades from the dental program. The current checklist identifies core dental-science and clinical subjects that should be represented in the training record.

Internship certificate

Provide it where the internship is a mandatory component of your qualification.

Passport and photograph

Your legal name in ADC Connect should match your passport. If names differ between educational records and current identification, upload the acceptable legal name-change evidence or the explanatory evidence requested by ADC.

English translations

Documents issued in another language require an official English translation meeting ADC translation rules. The original and translation should be supplied in the required format.

Letter or certificate of good standing

Where ADC requires current good-standing evidence, it must come directly from the relevant registering/licensing authority, not from the applicant. ADC's current FAQ says it can be emailed directly by the authority and that letters of good standing are time-limited.

Use the live ADC Connect checklist because document requirements can change.

The March 2027 deadline has already passed.

The ADC warns that meeting this deadline is not a guarantee of written-exam eligibility. Missing documents or additional verification can delay completion beyond the June application window.

Why Waiting Until 11 April Is Risky

ADC describes the normal initial assessment timeframe as approximately 8 weeks, but its FAQ recommends allowing more time when targeting a written exam.

The September written application opens on 8 June 2027. There is little buffer between 11 April and that window if your application is incomplete.

A sensible planning approach is to submit a complete application well before the deadline, especially if:

  • your university is slow to provide documents;
  • you need translations;
  • your names differ across documents;
  • good-standing verification must come from another country;
  • ADC has previously requested clarification.

That is planning advice, not an earlier official deadline.

Initial Assessment Outcomes

ADC can return three broad outcomes:

  1. Successful — you become eligible to apply for a written examination while the assessment is valid.
  2. Incomplete — additional information or documents are required.
  3. Unsuccessful — you are not eligible to proceed under that application.

Your outcome letter is made available through ADC Connect, and ADC emails you when it is ready.

Seven-Year Validity

A successful initial assessment is valid for seven years.

The expiry date is shown in ADC Connect.

This validity matters at every later stage. You need a valid initial assessment during relevant written/practical application periods and on the exam dates required by ADC.

If the initial assessment expires without an approved renewal, ADC states that you need to start the initial-assessment process again and previous successful exam results become void.

Renewal

The current registration-only renewal fee is $35 AUD.

You can apply to renew through ADC Connect before the existing assessment expires. ADC may require a new letter/certificate of good standing as part of renewal.

A renewed initial assessment receives a new seven-year validity period once approved.

Do not wait until after expiry. ADC states an expired initial assessment cannot simply be renewed; a new initial assessment application is required.

Interaction With Written-Exam Validity

The current ADC dentist written-exam page and June 2026 practical handbook state that a successful dentist written examination is valid for five years.

However, your initial assessment must also remain valid. ADC guidance says that if you allow the initial assessment to expire without renewal, previous valid written results can be lost.

So track both dates, not only the written-pass expiry.

Candidate Scenarios

Scenario 1: New applicant targeting September 2027

You have not started the initial assessment. The official submission deadline is 11 April 2027, but a complete application submitted earlier gives you more protection against document delays. Your goal is not merely to upload by April 11; it is to be assessed as eligible before the June written application closes.

Scenario 2: Already hold a valid initial assessment

You do not need another full initial assessment for the September written exam. Check the expiry date in ADC Connect and confirm it remains valid through the written exam date.

Scenario 3: Initial assessment expires in 2027

If the expiry approaches, submit the renewal before the expiry date. Do not assume that a pending renewal after expiry will preserve your existing written result.

Scenario 4: Your documents use different versions of your name

Resolve the discrepancy using accepted legal evidence or the explanatory documentation ADC requests. Do not upload inconsistent documents and hope they will be matched automatically.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a provisional degree certificate instead of the final qualification.
  • Waiting until the written-exam application window to begin initial assessment.
  • Treating the published deadline as a guaranteed approval date.
  • Forgetting official translations.
  • Sending a good-standing letter yourself when ADC requires it directly from the authority.
  • Letting the seven-year validity expire.
  • Confusing the registration initial assessment with a migration skills assessment.
