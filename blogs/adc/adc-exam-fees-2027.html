ADC Exam Fees 2027: Written, Practical & Assessment Costs

Budgeting accurately for the Australian Dental Council (ADC) assessment pathway requires looking beyond a single exam price tag. For dentists using the standard ADC assessment pathway toward general registration, the core fees are charged across three stages—initial assessment, the computer-based written examination, and the two-day practical examination in Melbourne—alongside potential administrative renewal fees, withdrawal penalties, and substantial non-ADC travel and preparation expenses.

Quick Answers: 2027 ADC Exam Fees for Dentists

  • How much is the ADC initial assessment fee for dentists? (Official ADC Fact) The mandatory Initial assessment fee is $647 AUD. If your initial assessment is nearing its seven-year expiry and you only need a registration-status renewal, the Initial assessment renewal – registration only fee is $35 AUD.
  • How much is the ADC written examination fee? (Official ADC Fact) The Written examination application fee for dentists is $2,122 AUD per sitting.
  • How much is the ADC practical examination fee? (Official ADC Fact) The Practical examination application fee for dentists is $4,775 AUD per sitting.
  • What is the minimum ADC-only total if I pass everything on the first try? (Calculation, Not an Official ADC Quoted Total) Adding the three mandatory stage fees together ($647 + $2,122 + $4,775) yields a minimum ADC-only baseline of $7,544 AUD, assuming no renewals, withdrawals, reviews, or repeat attempts are required.
  • Are ADC fees fixed permanently for all of 2027? (Official ADC Fact) On June 24, 2026, the ADC announced a 12-month freeze on examination and application fees. However, ADC fees are reviewed annually (typically around July) and are subject to change—always confirm the exact fee displayed on ADC's website and inside ADC Connect before paying.
  • Can I get a refund if I withdraw from a written or practical exam? (Official ADC Fact) Initial assessment fees are non-refundable once submitted. Written and practical examination fees carry 80%, 50%, or 0% refunds depending strictly on when you submit your withdrawal request in ADC Connect.

Official Review & Administrative Appeal Fees for Dentists

Under the ADC's Review and Appeals policy, candidates may apply via ADC Connect for an administrative review—and, if still dissatisfied following that review determination, escalate to an Independent Appeal Committee. Crucial limitation: Reviews and appeals apply only when alleging a breach or error in the administrative process. They do not involve re-scoring or re-evaluating your clinical or written exam performance, and detailed component marks or exam questions are never released.

Stage-by-Stage Breakdown of Mandatory ADC Fees

1. Initial Assessment ($647 AUD) vs. Renewal ($35 AUD)

Every candidate begins by submitting qualifications, transcripts, registration certificates, and professional references through ADC Connect for an Initial assessment ($647 AUD). For full document requirements, review our ADC Initial Assessment 2027 Guide.

  • An approved initial assessment stays valid for 7 years.
  • If you have not yet completed the practical exam as your seven-year expiry approaches, lodging an Initial assessment renewal – registration only ($35 AUD) before the expiry date extends your validity without repeating credential verification.
  • Cost trap: If you allow your initial assessment to expire even by a single day, it cannot be renewed—you must lodge a brand-new $647 AUD initial assessment, and any active written exam pass lapses along with it.

2. Written Examination Fee ($2,122 AUD)

For the current venue-selection flow and the September 2026 Pearson trial, see ADC Written Exam Booking 2027.

Once your initial assessment is approved, you pay $2,122 AUD per sitting during an open written application period (such as 8 – 15 June 2027 for the September 2027 sitting). The ADC announced a streamlined Pearson Professional Assessments workflow trial in September 2026 in which candidates select a location before completing payment and the selected seat is held for up to two hours. The standing dentist written-examination page still describes the older ATT workflow, so 2027 candidates must follow the live ADC Connect and Pearson instructions for their sitting. See our ADC Written Booking & Pearson VUE 2027 Guide for full payment mechanics.

3. Practical Examination Fee ($4,775 AUD)

For 2027 practical windows and seat rules, use ADC Practical Exam Dates 2027 and ADC Practical Waitlist 2027.

The practical examination is the single largest official fee on the pathway at $4,775 AUD. Payment is required in full during the 10-day practical application period (for example, 10 – 19 February 2027 for Period 2 or 16 – 25 June 2027 for Period 3) before the 48-hour-later date-selection window opens. Check ADC Practical Exam Dates 2027 and ADC Practical Waitlist 2027 to plan your application timing.

Withdrawal & Refund Rules: How Much You Lose If Plans Change

Under the ADC's Assessments and Examinations Withdrawal Procedure, examinations cannot be transferred or rescheduled to another sitting. If illness, visa delays, or inadequate preparation prevent you from attending, you must submit an official withdrawal request through ADC Connect and reapply (paying the full fee again) in a future window.

Approved refunds are processed back to the original credit card within 10 business days, and any requested supporting information must be supplied within three months.

When You May Need to Pay the Same ADC Fee Again

Even disciplined candidates occasionally end up paying a stage fee twice. The five most common triggers are:

  1. Unsuccessful Exam Attempt: There is no lifetime cap on written or practical attempts, but every re-sit requires paying the full $2,122 AUD written fee or $4,775 AUD practical fee again.
  2. Deferring to a Later Sitting: Because ADC bookings cannot be rolled forward to a future period, postponing an exam requires withdrawing (losing 20% to 100% of your fee) and paying the full exam fee again when the next window opens.
  3. Visa Delays or Exam-Day ID Issues: The ADC explicitly classifies inability to obtain an Australian visa, changed travel plans, or arriving without valid original primary photo ID as a candidate withdrawal/no-show—resulting in 0% refund if within the non-refundable window.
  4. Letting Your 7-Year Initial Assessment Expire: Failing to pay the $35 AUD renewal before expiry forces you to pay $647 AUD for a brand-new initial assessment and invalidates any active written exam pass, requiring another $2,122 AUD written exam.
  5. Written Exam Pass Expiry Before Completing the Practical Exam: Current ADC dentist-specific written and practical guidance states that a successful written examination pass is valid for 5 years from result notification (note that one general ADC FAQ page still displays 3 years—always check the exact expiry date recorded in your ADC Connect account). If you do not pass the practical exam before your written pass expires, you must pay $2,122 AUD to re-sit the written exam.

Out-of-Pocket Costs That Are NOT Paid to the ADC

When building your total budget for Australian dental registration, set aside a separate reserve for non-ADC expenses—which often equal or exceed the $7,544 AUD official assessment fees:

  • Credential Preparation & Primary-Source Verifications: University transcript fees, degree verification charges, professional good-standing certificates, certified true copies, notarization, and international couriers.
  • NAATI-Certified Translations: Required for any academic or registration document not originally issued in English.
  • English Language Proficiency Testing: Required later when applying for general registration with the Dental Board of Australia (AHPRA) if you do not meet exempt schooling criteria.
  • Written Exam Travel (If Local Seats Fill): While the written exam is offered at authorised Pearson VUE centres worldwide, strict workstation caps mean candidates in high-demand cities sometimes travel interstate or overseas to secure a seat.
  • Mandatory Melbourne Travel for the Practical Exam: Because all dentist practical exams take place exclusively at Level 6, 469 La Trobe Street, Melbourne, overseas and interstate candidates must budget for return airfares, Australian visitor/business visa fees, 4 to 7 nights of Melbourne accommodation (allowing buffer days for jet lag and mandatory registration check-in), meals, and airport transfers.
  • Preparation Materials & Simulation Practice: Optional hands-on typodont simulation courses, magnification loupes, practice teeth/burs, Australian clinical textbooks (Therapeutic Guidelines: Oral and Dental), and online question banks.
  • Post-ADC AHPRA Registration Fees: Once you receive your ADC certificate, applying for general dental registration with the Dental Board of Australia carries separate AHPRA application and annual registration fees.

Common Budgeting & Payment Mistakes Candidates Make

  1. Booking Non-Refundable Flights Before Receiving Session Confirmation: The ADC explicitly states it is not liable for travel or accommodation expenses if a session changes or you withdraw. Wait for your official session confirmation email before locking in airfares.
  2. Using a Card With Daily International Transaction Limits on Booking Day: When paying $2,122 AUD (written) or $4,775 AUD (practical)—especially under a two-hour seat reservation timer—a declined bank authorization can cause you to lose your reserved workstation.
  3. Forgetting Foreign Exchange Spreads & Bank Conversion Fees: Paying $7,544+ AUD across multiple transactions from a non-AUD bank account can add 2% to 4% in hidden FX spreads unless you use a low-FX multi-currency card.
  4. Assuming "Medical Certificate = 100% Refund": Under ADC rules, an approved medical withdrawal yields a discretionary refund of no more than 50%—never 100%.
  5. Assuming Fees Never Change: Even with the ADC's June 24, 2026 12-month fee freeze, always verify the live fee schedule on adc.org.au before every application period in 2027.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is the total official ADC fee to complete the dentist assessment pathway?

(Official ADC Facts + Calculation) The ADC charges per milestone rather than as a lump sum: $647 AUD for the initial assessment, $2,122 AUD for the written examination, and $4,775 AUD for the practical examination. Added together, the calculated minimum ADC-only cost for a candidate who passes every stage on the first attempt is $7,544 AUD, excluding travel, preparation, or AHPRA registration fees.

2. Are ADC exam fees charged in Australian Dollars (AUD), and are they subject to change?

(Official ADC Fact) Yes. All ADC assessment and examination fees are billed in Australian Dollars (AUD). Following the ADC's June 24, 2026 12-month fee freeze, fees remain subject to periodic review—candidates must verify current amounts on the ADC website prior to submitting any payment.

3. If I fail the written or practical exam, can I pay $1,008 AUD to have my exam re-marked?

(Official ADC Fact) No. While the Application for review fee is $1,008 AUD for either the written or practical examination, the ADC explicitly states that reviews audit administrative procedure only and do not re-score or re-assess your examination performance.

4. How long is my written exam pass valid before I have to pay for the practical exam?

(Official ADC Fact + Site Note) Current ADC dentist-specific written and practical guidance states that a passing result in the written examination is valid for 5 years from result notification (provided your 7-year initial assessment remains valid or is renewed prior to expiry). Because one general ADC FAQ page still displays 3 years, check the exact expiry date listed inside your ADC Connect dashboard.

5. What refund do I get if I cannot get an Australian visa in time for my practical exam?

(Official ADC Fact) Under ADC rules, inability to obtain a visa or changed travel arrangements is treated as a standard candidate withdrawal rather than an exceptional refund ground. You receive 80% only if you withdraw before your practical session is confirmed, 50% if you withdraw after confirmation at least six weeks before the exam, and 0% within six weeks of your confirmed exam date.
