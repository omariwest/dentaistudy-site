ADC Practical Exam Waitlist 2027: Priority and Eligibility Rules

The ADC practical examination does not work like a simple first-come calendar. Candidates must hold a valid initial assessment and written result, cannot sit consecutive practical examination periods, and may be invited from a waiting list when another candidate withdraws.

Quick Answers

Who is eligible for a 2027 practical place?

You need a valid initial assessment and valid written examination through the end of your practical exam date, and you must meet the period-specific eligibility rules.

Can you sit consecutive practical periods?

No. ADC states candidates cannot sit consecutive practical examination periods.

Did 2027 Period 1 use priority stages?

Yes. Stage 1 ran 14–18 October 2026 for candidates with eligibility expiring during Period 1; Stage 2 ran 19–23 October 2026 for other eligible candidates.

Is there a waitlist?

Yes. For Period 1, the waitlist became active after the application period closed on 23 October 2026. ADC's practical page also says each period has a waiting list when places become available.

Are Period 2 and Period 3 priority stages already confirmed?

No. Their application and date-selection windows are published, but the same staged priority rules have not yet been announced.

How much is the practical exam?

The current dentist practical examination fee is $4,775 AUD.

If you later sit the practical examination, ADC Practical Results 2027 explains result timing, retakes and the next registration step.

The ADC practical examination does not work like a simple first-come calendar. Candidates must hold a valid initial assessment and written result, cannot sit consecutive practical examination periods, and may be invited from a waiting list when another candidate withdraws.

For 2027 Period 1, the Australian Dental Council published a specific two-stage priority system before the October 2026 application window. The ADC has also confirmed that each practical examination period can use a waiting list when places become available at short notice. However, as of 18 November 2026, the ADC had not published equivalent Stage 1/Stage 2 rules for 2027 Periods 2 or 3. Do not automatically apply the Period 1 priority categories to later periods unless ADC publishes them.

Core Eligibility Rules

If your initial assessment is approaching expiry, review ADC Initial Assessment 2027; for the current five-year dentist written-validity rule, see the existing written validity guide.

ADC currently requires practical candidates to:

  • hold a valid initial assessment whose expiry extends beyond the practical examination date;
  • hold a valid written examination result whose expiry extends beyond the practical examination date;
  • meet the eligibility conditions for the advertised practical period;
  • not have sat, or be due to sit, in the immediately preceding practical examination period.

The current dentist guidance states that a written pass is valid for five years from the date you are notified of the successful result. Your initial assessment is generally valid for seven years and must also remain valid.

If your initial assessment expires and is not renewed before expiry, previous successful exam results can also become invalid. Check both expiry dates in ADC Connect before relying on a practical waitlist opportunity.

How 2027 Period 1 Priority Worked

The October 2026 Period 1 application was split into two stages.

Stage 1 — 14 to 18 October 2026

Stage 1 was for candidates who did not already have a Period 3 August–December 2026 practical place and had:

  • an initial assessment expiring during 2027 Period 1, January–May 2027; and/or
  • a written examination expiring before 31 May 2027.

This stage prioritized candidates whose eligibility would otherwise run out during the Period 1 window.

Stage 2 — 19 to 23 October 2026

Stage 2 was for candidates who did not have a Period 3 2026 practical booking and whose:

  • initial assessment expired after 31 May 2027; and/or
  • written examination expired after 31 May 2027.

ADC stated that Stage 2 applications were accepted first come, first served and could close early if available places were taken.

If the period closed early, ADC would create a waiting list.

What the Period 1 Waitlist Actually Means

The waitlist is not a guaranteed exam booking.

ADC says withdrawn seats may become available after the application period closes. Candidates can add their names to the Period 1 waiting list, and places are usually offered at short notice.

To be offered a place, a candidate must still satisfy the waitlist criteria:

  • valid initial assessment;
  • valid written examination;
  • no current practical booking;
  • no pending practical examination result;
  • no pending practical review or appeal;
  • ability to accept and attend within the offered timeframe.

ADC also gives priority to candidates who did not sit the practical examination in the previous period.

Being earlier on the list does not override eligibility. If the first candidate cannot meet the criteria for a released date, ADC may move to the next eligible person.

Short-Notice Invitations: Before You Say Yes

A waitlist invitation can be valuable, but it may create a difficult travel decision.

Before accepting, confirm:

  • the exact Melbourne examination dates;
  • your passport and Australian travel/visa position;
  • whether you can reach Melbourne in time;
  • accommodation availability;
  • whether your initial assessment and written result remain valid through the offered date;
  • whether you are genuinely prepared to sit.

ADC explicitly warns that waitlist opportunities can arise at short notice and that candidates should consider whether they have sufficient preparation and travel time before accepting. Special consideration is not created merely because you voluntarily accepted a short-notice place.

Periods 2 and 3: What Is Confirmed and What Is Not

The calendar is confirmed:

  • Period 2 application: 10–19 February 2027; date selection 23–25 February; exams May–August.
  • Period 3 application: 16–25 June 2027; date selection 29 June–1 July; exams August–December.

What is not yet confirmed is whether ADC will repeat the exact Period 1 Stage 1/Stage 2 priority model for either later period.

The safest approach is:

  1. verify eligibility in ADC Connect;
  2. watch ADC Candidate News before each application period;
  3. read any priority email sent directly to you;
  4. do not assume your Period 1 priority category carries forward automatically.

Date Selection Is Not Final Confirmation

Applying successfully is only the first step.

After the application window closes, the ADC opens a date-selection period. You select a preferred session through ADC Connect, but the ADC says your preferred date is not confirmed immediately.

ADC reviews the sessions after date selection closes and later emails your confirmed practical exam dates. You must be available during the broader period, not only your preferred date.

Do not buy non-refundable flights based only on an unconfirmed preferred session.

Current Fee and Withdrawal Risk

The current cost is also listed in ADC Exam Fees 2027, together with written, assessment and review/appeal charges.

The current general dentistry practical examination fee is $4,775 AUD.

ADC's current withdrawal procedure provides:

  • 80% refund after application but before the exam session is confirmed;
  • 50% refund after confirmation when at least six weeks remain;
  • no refund after confirmation when fewer than six weeks remain;
  • illness with supporting medical documentation may receive a discretionary refund of no more than 50%.

Because waitlist seats can be offered late, understand the refund position before accepting.

Candidate Scenarios

Scenario 1: Your written result is close to expiry

If your written validity ends during the practical period, an earlier priority stage may matter. Period 1 specifically prioritized candidates with expiring eligibility. For later periods, wait for ADC to publish the actual priority rules rather than assuming identical treatment.

Scenario 2: You sat the immediately previous period

ADC's general rule prevents candidates from sitting consecutive practical periods. Even if a waitlist place appears, you still have to meet the period's eligibility criteria.

Scenario 3: You are waitlisted but live outside Australia

Do not treat joining the list as a commitment to travel at any cost. If ADC contacts you, assess the exact date, visa, travel and preparation realities before accepting.

Scenario 4: You have a review or appeal pending

Period 1 waitlist criteria exclude candidates with a pending practical result, review or appeal. Resolve the status before expecting a new place.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming every 2027 period uses the same two-stage priority system.
  • Believing waitlist position guarantees a seat.
  • Forgetting the no-consecutive-period rule.
  • Letting the initial assessment expire while waiting.
  • Accepting a short-notice date before checking travel feasibility.
  • Treating preferred date selection as final confirmation.
  • Booking flights before ADC confirms the actual session.

FAQs

1. Does every ADC practical period have a waitlist?

The current dentist practical page says each period has a waiting list and eligible candidates may be invited when places become available. Specific waitlist mechanics can differ by period.

2. Who received Period 1 priority?

Stage 1 prioritized candidates whose initial assessment and/or written eligibility would expire during the January–May 2027 period. Stage 2 covered other eligible candidates.

3. Can I sit Period 2 if I sat Period 1?

ADC says candidates cannot sit consecutive practical examination periods.

4. Is the waitlist first come, first served?

Period 1 had eligibility criteria and priority rules. ADC can move to the next person if a candidate does not meet the criteria for the released place.

5. Are Period 2 and Period 3 priority stages published?

Not as of 18 November 2026. Their application and date-selection dates are published, but detailed priority stages had not been released.
