ADC Practical Eligibility 2027: Valid Written & Initial Assessment

Eligibility for the Australian Dental Council practical examination is not based only on whether you once passed the written exam. For a 2027 practical place, you need a valid initial assessment, a valid written examination result, and you must meet the rules for the specific practical examination period you are trying to enter.

The current ADC dentist guidance is especially important because older FAQ wording still conflicts with newer dentist-specific material. The current written-examination page, practical-examination page and June 2026 Practical Examination Handbook state that a successful dentist written examination is valid for five years. The initial assessment is valid for seven years. Candidates should still verify the exact expiry dates shown in ADC Connect.

Quick Answers

  • Initial assessment validity: 7 years.
  • Written examination validity: Current dentist-specific ADC guidance says 5 years from notification of the successful written result.
  • Must both still be valid on the practical exam date? Yes. ADC's 2027 Period 1 eligibility notice required both the initial assessment and written result to remain valid through the end of the examination date.
  • Can you sit consecutive practical periods? No. ADC says candidates cannot sit consecutive practical examination periods.
  • Is a practical place guaranteed once eligible? No. Capacity is limited, eligible candidates are invited into application periods, and each period can use a waiting list.
  • Current practical fee: $4,775 AUD.

The Three Core Eligibility Conditions

For general dentistry, treat practical eligibility as three separate checks.

1. Your initial assessment must still be valid

The ADC initial assessment is normally valid for seven years.

The expiry date is shown in ADC Connect. You can apply to renew the initial assessment before it expires. Once approved, the renewal receives a new seven-year validity period.

If the initial assessment expires before a renewal is completed, ADC says you must start the initial-assessment process again and previous successful examination results become void.

This makes initial-assessment expiry a serious planning issue even for a candidate who already has a valid written pass.

2. Your written result must still be valid

Current dentist-specific ADC guidance states that a successful written examination is valid for five years from the date you are notified of the successful result.

The June 2026 Practical Examination Handbook uses the same five-year rule.

One older ADC FAQ section still displays three years. Because the official site is internally inconsistent, use the newer/current dentist-specific guidance for planning but verify the actual expiry recorded in your ADC Connect account.

3. You must meet the period-specific participation rules

Being valid on paper does not guarantee that you may sit every practical period.

ADC currently says:

  • candidates cannot sit consecutive practical examination periods;
  • eligible candidates are invited to apply for specific periods;
  • each period can use a waiting list;
  • released places may be offered at short notice.

For 2027 Period 1, ADC explicitly required candidates to have both eligibility documents valid through the end of the practical exam date and not to have sat, or be due to sit, the immediately preceding practical period.

Period 1 Shows How Expiry Dates Affect Priority

ADC split the 2027 Period 1 application window into two stages.

Stage 1 prioritized eligible candidates whose initial assessment expired during the January–May 2027 period and/or whose written result expired before 31 May 2027.

Stage 2 opened for other eligible candidates whose relevant validity extended beyond 31 May 2027.

That does not mean the same Stage 1/Stage 2 structure will automatically apply to Periods 2 and 3. As of 17 November 2026, ADC had not published equivalent detailed priority stages for those periods.

The lesson is broader: expiry dates can affect when ADC gives you access to a practical application period, so monitor candidate emails and the live practical page rather than assuming every period follows identical rules.

How to Check Your Own Eligibility Before an Application Window

Use ADC Connect and confirm four things:

  1. Initial assessment expiry date — it must extend far enough for the targeted practical exam date.
  2. Written-result expiry date — under current dentist guidance, the five-year period must also extend through the practical exam date.
  3. Previous practical period — confirm the no-consecutive-period rule does not exclude you.
  4. ADC invitation/status — check whether you have been invited into the period or waitlist process.

Do this before paying for travel, accommodation or practical preparation.

Initial Assessment Renewal and Why Timing Matters

A renewal can be completed through ADC Connect before the initial assessment expires.

The current renewal fee for registration purposes is $35 AUD.

Do not wait until after expiry. ADC states that if the initial assessment expires without renewal:

  • a new initial assessment application is required;
  • previous successful exam results become void.

That can turn an otherwise valid written result into a lost result.

For the full administrative pathway, see ADC Initial Assessment 2027.

Written Validity and the Official 5-Year vs 3-Year Conflict

This is the point most likely to confuse candidates.

Current dentist-specific sources say five years:

  • ADC dentist written-examination page;
  • ADC dentist practical-examination page;
  • June 2026 Practical Examination Handbook.

An older FAQ section still says three years.

For 2027 planning, we use the newer/current dentist-specific five-year guidance. However, your own ADC Connect expiry date should control your personal decision-making. If the portal expiry conflicts with the current handbook, contact ADC before committing to a later practical period.

The No-Consecutive-Period Rule

ADC says candidates cannot sit consecutive practical examination periods because of limited capacity and fairness.

Example:

  • If you sit 2027 Period 1, do not assume you can immediately sit 2027 Period 2 if unsuccessful.
  • You may need to wait until a later eligible period.

This matters for candidates close to written or initial-assessment expiry. A failed practical attempt can consume more calendar time than the exam date itself because the next period may be unavailable under the consecutive-period rule.

Waiting Lists Do Not Override Eligibility

Each practical period can use a waiting list when places become available.

A short-notice invitation does not waive the normal eligibility rules. You still need:

  • valid initial assessment;
  • valid written result;
  • compliance with the period's participation rules;
  • ability to attend the offered Melbourne dates.

For detailed waitlist mechanics, see ADC Practical Exam Waitlist 2027.

Candidate Scenarios

Scenario 1: Written result expires during Period 2

If your written result expires in June 2027, a May practical date could be valid while an August date would not be. Do not assume that being eligible at the February application window guarantees eligibility for every date inside the May–August exam period.

Scenario 2: Initial assessment expires before the practical date

Renew before the expiry. If the initial assessment lapses, ADC says previous successful exam results become void.

Scenario 3: You sat the immediately previous practical period

Even with valid documents, the no-consecutive-period rule can block the next period.

Scenario 4: You receive a short-notice waitlist invitation

Check both expiry dates against the exact offered exam date before accepting. A waitlist seat is not useful if one of your required eligibilities expires first.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating written-exam validity as the only eligibility date that matters.
  • Forgetting the initial assessment has its own seven-year expiry.
  • Using the older three-year FAQ rule without checking the current dentist-specific guidance.
  • Assuming eligibility on application day is enough even if a document expires before the exam date.
  • Ignoring the no-consecutive-period rule.
  • Assuming a waitlist invitation overrides validity requirements.
  • Letting the initial assessment expire while waiting for a practical place.

FAQs

1. How long is the ADC initial assessment valid?

Seven years. Renewal is available before expiry.

2. How long is the dentist written exam valid?

Current dentist-specific ADC guidance says five years from notification of the successful result. One older FAQ still says three years, so verify your ADC Connect expiry.

3. Can I take practical exams in consecutive periods?

No. Current ADC dentist guidance says candidates cannot sit consecutive practical examination periods.

4. Do my documents need to be valid on the actual practical exam date?

For 2027 Period 1, ADC explicitly required both the initial assessment and written result to remain valid through the end of the examination date. Use the same conservative check for any later period and follow the period-specific notice.

5. Does passing the practical exam expire?

The current ADC practical page lists a successful practical result as not expiring.
