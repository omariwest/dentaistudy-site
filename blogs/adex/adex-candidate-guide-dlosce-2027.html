ADEX 2027 Candidate Guide: DLOSCE, Exam Components and Eligibility

The American Board of Dental Examiners (ADEX)—administering examinations under the unified CDCA-WREB-CITA structure—does not publish a document titled "2027 Candidate Guide." Instead, ADEX organizes its examination rules on an academic-year manual season. Candidates sitting the ADEX Dental Examination in early and mid-2027 are governed by the August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027 documentation suite published on adextesting.org (specifically the Candidate Guide: General Policies & Information , the Dental Clinical Examination Candidate Manual , and the Dental Candidate Manual: Computer-Based DLOSCE ).

Quick Answers

  • Which ADEX manual season governs 2027 exams? All ADEX Dental sittings from August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027 are governed by the August 2026 release (Version 20260727). Manuals for August 1, 2027 onward have not yet been issued.
  • What are the current components of the ADEX Dental Examination series? Six required components: one computer-based examination (DLOSCE) and five simulated-patient (manikin) clinical hand-skill components (Periodontal, Endodontic, Prosthodontic, Anterior Restorative, and Posterior Restorative).
  • How did the August 1, 2026 transition change the exam? Two major changes took effect for the August 1, 2026 season: (1) the ADA's DLOSCE became an integrated component of the ADEX Dental series, replacing the legacy DSE OSCE for new registrants, and (2) the simulated-patient Periodontal Examination became mandatory for all ADEX Dental candidates.
  • Who is eligible to sit the ADEX Dental Examination? Current students in their final year (or authorized CIF cohort) of a CODA/CDAC-accredited dental program, graduates holding a DDS/DMD from a CODA/CDAC-accredited program, or internationally trained dentists holding a valid state board authorization letter specifying ADEX Dental eligibility.
  • How long do candidates have to pass the entire series? Under the ADEX 18-Month Rule, you must pass all components of the series within 18 months of your initial attempt at any component, with a maximum of three consecutive attempts per component before the entire series must be repeated.
  • Does ADEX grant a dental license? No. ADEX is a test developer and administrator, not a licensing board. Each U.S. state dental board sets its own acceptance rules, score validity windows, and additional requirements (such as California's transition timeline for the DLOSCE).

The American Board of Dental Examiners (ADEX)—administering examinations under the unified CDCA-WREB-CITA structure—does not publish a document titled "2027 Candidate Guide." Instead, ADEX organizes its examination rules on an academic-year manual season. Candidates sitting the ADEX Dental Examination in early and mid-2027 are governed by the August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027 documentation suite published on adextesting.org (specifically the Candidate Guide: General Policies & Information, the Dental Clinical Examination Candidate Manual, and the Dental Candidate Manual: Computer-Based DLOSCE).

If you plan to challenge any component of the ADEX Dental Examination after July 31, 2027, you must recheck adextesting.org for the August 1, 2027 – July 31, 2028 Candidate Guide and manuals before registering or testing, as ADEX updates administrative policies, scoring rubrics, and procedural criteria annually for each August 1 season.

Governing Manual Period: August 1, 2026 – July 31, 2027

For location-specific sittings, registration deadlines and outside-candidate access, use ADEX Exam Dates 2027.

A frequent error among candidates preparing for 2027 is searching for a standalone "2027 ADEX Handbook" or studying from pre-August 2026 PDFs. ADEX divides its operational year from August 1 to July 31:

  • Current Active Season (August 1, 2026 – July 31, 2027): Governed by the August 2026 document suite on adextesting.org/adex-dental-forms-2026-aug/. This suite separates general administrative regulations from exam-specific technical criteria:
  1. Candidate Guide: General Policies & Information (eligibility, ID rules, 18-month rule, 3-time fail policy, accommodations, appeals, and conduct).
  2. Dental Clinical Examination Candidate Manual (plus individual procedure criteria sheets, scoring rubrics, and candidate worksheets).
  3. Dental Candidate Manual: Computer-Based DLOSCE (Prometric testing rules, two-day scheduling window, item formats, and scoring).
  • Testing After July 31, 2027: ADEX has not yet published manuals, criteria sheets, or fee schedules for the August 1, 2027 – July 31, 2028 season. Any candidate scheduling clinical or computer-based components on or after August 1, 2027 must verify the newly published season manuals on adextesting.org prior to sitting.

Overall ADEX Dental Examination Components

Registration for the ADEX Dental Examination series ($2,995 base series fee) encompasses six required components split across two testing environments:

  1. Computer-Based Component (1 exam at Prometric Test Centers):
  • Dental Licensure Objective Structured Clinical Examination (DLOSCE) — developed by the American Dental Association (ADA) and integrated into the ADEX series.
  1. Simulated-Patient Manikin Clinical Components (5 hand-skill exams at host dental schools/centers):
  • Periodontal Examination (required for all candidates effective August 1, 2026)
  • Endodontic Examination (anterior and posterior manikin procedures graded together as one component)
  • Prosthodontic Examination (crown preparations graded together as one component)
  • Anterior Restorative Examination (Class III composite preparation and restoration)
  • Posterior Restorative Examination (Class II composite preparation and restoration)

ADEX offers clinical administrations in two structural formats depending on host school scheduling:

  • Traditional Format: All clinical components are scheduled over a consolidated multi-day window (typically 2 days), available to both eligible students and outside candidates at open sites.
  • Curriculum Integrated Format (CIF): Designed exclusively for currently enrolled dental students at participating schools, splitting clinical modules across the final academic year.

For a deeper look at how individual clinical procedures are evaluated and where critical errors occur, review ADEX Scoring & Critical Fail Points and ADEX Restorative Errors & Fail Points.

The DLOSCE and the August 1, 2026 Transition From DSE OSCE

The existing ADEX OSCE overview provides broader computer-based exam context, while this page focuses on the current DLOSCE transition and season rules.

One of the biggest structural shifts in the 2026–2027 season is the replacement of ADEX's former Diagnostic Skills Examination OSCE (DSE OSCE) with the ADA's Dental Licensure Objective Structured Clinical Examination (DLOSCE).

Timeline of the Official Transition

  • April 14, 2026 Agreement: The ADA and ADEX announced an agreement incorporating the DLOSCE into the ADEX Dental Examination series no later than August 1, 2026.
  • June 2026 Registration Switch: Registration for the integrated ADEX Dental Examination series opened with the DLOSCE included as the computer-based component, with Prometric appointment scheduling beginning August 2026.
  • August 1, 2026 Cutoff: Standalone DLOSCE registrations for new candidates ended after August 1, 2026 (except in conjunction with the ADEX Dental Examination).
  • October 9, 2026 Cessation of Standalone DLOSCE: All remaining standalone DLOSCE administrations ceased after October 9, 2026.
  • December 31, 2026 Legacy DSE OSCE Cutoff: Candidates who had previously registered for the legacy DSE OSCE prior to the transition were required to complete their DSE OSCE attempt no later than December 31, 2026. From January 1, 2027 onward, all computer-based initial attempts and retakes within the ADEX Dental series are exclusively the DLOSCE.

How the DLOSCE Is Administered and Scored (2026–2027 Manual)

According to the Dental Candidate Manual: Computer-Based DLOSCE (Version 20260727):

  • Test Center & Appointment Structure: Administered at secure Prometric Test Centers. Total appointment seat time is approximately 6 hours and 45 minutes, including a 25-minute introduction/tutorial, multiple timed clinical sections, and three optional scheduled breaks.
  • Two-Day Completion Window: The DLOSCE is delivered across two testing appointments (Day 1 and Day 2) that must be scheduled and completed within 14 calendar days of each other as a single examination attempt. Failing to attend either scheduled appointment requires restarting the entire DLOSCE attempt.
  • Interactive Stimulus Materials: Questions utilize high-resolution clinical photographs, radiographs, laboratory values, prescription forms, patient charts ("Patient Boxes"), and interactive 3D models that candidates rotate and inspect on screen.
  • Question Formats & Partial Credit:
  1. Single Correct Answer: Standard multiple-choice items worth 1 point for the single best answer and 0 points otherwise.
  2. Multiple Correct Answers: Items where more than one option is correct. Candidates earn full credit (1 point) by selecting all correct answers and zero incorrect answers, or partial credit (e.g., 0.5 points) by selecting a subset of correct answers without selecting any incorrect answers. Selecting any incorrect option yields zero credit for the question.

Candidate Eligibility Requirements

ADEX divides ADEX Dental Examination eligibility into three distinct categories in the Candidate Guide: General Policies & Information (updated July 23, 2026):

1. Current Dental Students (CODA / CDAC-Accredited Programs)

  • Must be currently enrolled in good standing in a dental education program accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation (CODA) (United States) or the Commission on Dental Accreditation of Canada (CDAC).
  • Eligibility is verified directly through your dental school's dean or designated school administrator via your Candidate Profile (Candidates.ADEXtesting.org) based on your anticipated graduation date.

2. Dental School Graduates (CODA / CDAC-Accredited Programs)

  • Must hold a DDS or DMD degree from a CODA- or CDAC-accredited dental school.
  • Must upload an official final transcript or diploma confirming degree conferral into your online Candidate Profile for verification.

3. Internationally Trained Dentists (Non-CODA / Non-CDAC Programs)

  • Graduates of dental schools not accredited by CODA or CDAC cannot self-certify eligibility.
  • You must obtain and upload an official State Board Authorization Letter from the U.S. state dental board where you are seeking licensure, explicitly authorizing you to take the ADEX Dental Examination.
  • Scope and Expiry of Authorization: Eligibility is restricted strictly to the specific exam types named in that state board letter, and authorization letters are valid for up to 18 months from the letter date (subject to state-specific exclusions). Contact your target state board directly to ask how to qualify for an exam authorization letter (many states require completion of an advanced standing CODA program or accredited residency first).

Candidate Profile, Verification, and Site Authorization Rules

Before you can schedule either the DLOSCE at Prometric or a clinical sitting at a host dental school, you must complete profile verification on Candidates.ADEXtesting.org:

Profile Verification Requirements (Allow 2–3 Business Days)

  1. Exact Legal Name Match: Your profile first and last names must match your valid, unexpired, signature-bearing U.S. government-issued primary photo ID (driver's license, military ID, passport, or passport card). Do not use accent marks or tildes. Name-change updates require official legal documentation (marriage certificate, divorce decree, court order, naturalization certificate) and take up to 10 business days to process.
  2. Head-and-Shoulders Photo: Upload a clear selfie or head-and-shoulders portrait (never a photo of your passport or driver's license).
  3. Qualifying Education / Authorization Upload: School verification (for current students), diploma/final transcript (for CODA/CDAC graduates), or state board authorization letter (for internationally trained graduates).
  4. 60-Day Post-Graduation Revert Rule: If you created your profile as a current student, your account status automatically reverts from Verified to Pending 60 days after your listed graduation date until you upload your final diploma or proof of graduation.

Outside-Candidate Rules for Clinical Sites

Once your profile is verified, clinical registration is still governed by host-school capacity and access rules on adextesting.org/exam-calendars/ (see our detailed breakdown in ADEX Exam Dates 2027):

  • Outside Candidates: YES: Open to non-students (graduates and students from other institutions) as well as host-school students, allocated strictly first-come, first-served until outside seats reach zero.
  • Outside Candidates: NO: Restricted solely to currently enrolled students of that host dental school; outside candidates will not see these sittings as selectable options in their portal.
  • Schedule Release Lock-In: Personal clinical schedules and Q&A times are posted 2–3 weeks before the exam date; once released, no schedule changes are permitted.

High-Level Retake Rules: The 18-Month Rule and 3-Time Fail Policy

Every component of the ADEX Dental Examination series is governed by two interlocking progression rules in the Candidate Guide:

  1. The 18-Month Rule: You must achieve passing scores on all six components (DLOSCE plus all five clinical modules) within 18 months of the date of your very first attempt at any component in the series. If you fail to pass every component before the 18-month clock expires, all previously passed scores expire and you must restart the entire series from scratch.
  2. The 3-Time Fail Policy: You are permitted up to three consecutive unsuccessful attempts at any single component (note that for transition candidates, combined unsuccessful attempts across the legacy DSE OSCE and DLOSCE count toward the 3-time limit). Upon a third consecutive failure of any component, your series resets and you must repeat the entire series.
  3. Mandatory Waiting Periods:
  • After any single failed attempt (DLOSCE or clinical): You must wait a minimum of 10 calendar days from the failure notification/exam date before registering to retake that component.
  • After a third consecutive DLOSCE failure: You must wait a minimum of 6 months from the date of your third unsuccessful DLOSCE attempt before becoming eligible to reattempt the DLOSCE as part of a new series (this 6-month wait cannot be waived).
  1. Retake Fees (2026–2027 Schedule):
  • Clinical 2nd Attempt: $0 clinical exam fee (covered by the initial $2,995 series package), but you must pay the $150 typodont fee per procedure and any host-site Facility Fee.
  • Clinical 3rd Attempt: $1,495 for Perio, Endo, and/or Prosth; $1,495 for Anterior Restorative and/or Posterior Restorative; plus applicable typodont and site facility fees.
  • DLOSCE 2nd or 3rd Attempt: $695 per retake attempt.

Why Licensing Jurisdiction Rules Matter: California's DLOSCE Transition as an Example

The ADEX exam overview explains why passing ADEX and obtaining a state dental license are separate steps.

ADEX develops and administers examinations—it does not license dentists. Every state dental board determines independently:

  • Whether it accepts the ADEX Dental Examination at all (for example, ADEX's Acceptance Map updated September 9, 2026 shows acceptance across almost all U.S. jurisdictions except Delaware and New York for initial licensure).
  • Which specific components are required, how long scores remain valid, and whether extra state exams (such as state jurisprudence/law and ethics exams) or remediation rules apply.

Jurisdiction-Specific Example: Dental Board of California (dbc.ca.gov)

California illustrates why candidates must verify state board rules separately rather than assuming national uniformity:

  • California's DLOSCE Cutoff Date: On its official Licensure by ADEX page (dbc.ca.gov/applicants/licensure_by_adex.shtml), the Dental Board of California specifies that the DLOSCE replaced the DSE OSCE as of August 1, 2026, and notes that candidates registered for ADEX after June 1, 2026 take the DLOSCE instead.
  • California's 5-Year Score Validity Window: California requires applicants applying under Business and Professions Code (BPC) § 1632(c)(2) to submit proof of having passed the required ADEX sections (computer-based OSCE/DLOSCE, manikin Prosthodontic, manikin Endodontic, live-patient or CompeDont manikin Anterior/Posterior Restorative, and live-patient or CompeDont manikin Periodontal) within five (5) years preceding the date of the license application—distinct from ADEX's internal 18-month series completion window.
  • California's Statutory 3-Fail Remediation Rule (BPC § 1633): Under California Business and Professions Code § 1633, an applicant who fails any portion(s) of the ADEX examination three (3) times must complete 50 hours of remedial education at an acceptable dental school in the failed subject(s) and submit a signed Certification of Successful Completion of Remedial Education Requirements for Re-Examination Eligibility form before re-examining for California licensure.
  • Additional California Requirements: California also requires proof of passing the INBDE/NBDE, fingerprints (Live Scan or hard cards), a $400 initial application fee, and passing the California Law and Ethics Examination ($125 fee).

(Important: These statutory rules apply specifically to California licensure and are NOT nationwide rules. Other state dental boards impose their own distinct score-expiry windows, remediation policies, and jurisprudence requirements.)

Checklist for Candidates Testing After July 31, 2027

Because the current ADEX manual suite expires on July 31, 2027, candidates planning to take any part of the exam on or after August 1, 2027 must verify the following updates on adextesting.org before registering:

  1. Download the 2027–2028 Candidate Guide and Manuals: Confirm whether any procedural tolerances, typodont tooth numbers, preparation dimensions, or scoring rubrics changed for the August 1, 2027 – July 31, 2028 season.
  2. Verify Current 2027–2028 Fee Schedules: Confirm whether the $2,995 base series fee, $150 typodont fee, $695 DLOSCE retake fee, or $345 late/transfer fee has been adjusted for the new contract year.
  3. Recheck State Board Acceptance and Component Rules: Confirm on your target state dental board's website that no legislative or regulatory updates changed ADEX component acceptance or score validity rules for 2027–2028 graduates.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is there an official ADEX 2027 Candidate Guide PDF I should download?

No separate document is titled "2027 Candidate Guide." For all exams taken between August 1, 2026 and July 31, 2027, you must download the August 2026 edition of the Candidate Guide: General Policies & Information, the Dental Clinical Examination Candidate Manual, and the Dental Candidate Manual: Computer-Based DLOSCE from adextesting.org/adex-dental-forms-2026-aug/.

2. Is the Periodontal manikin exam optional in 2027?

For one of the now-required clinical areas, use ADEX Periodontal Assessment; for emergency decision-making practice, see ADEX Emergencies OSCE.

No. Effective August 1, 2026, the simulated-patient Periodontal Examination became a mandatory component of the ADEX Dental Examination series for all candidates, alongside Endodontics, Prosthodontics, Anterior Restorative, Posterior Restorative, and the computer-based DLOSCE.

3. Can an internationally trained dentist register directly for the ADEX Dental Exam in 2027?

Only after obtaining an official State Board Authorization Letter from a U.S. state dental board that explicitly authorizes you to challenge the ADEX Dental Examination. You must upload that letter to your Candidates.ADEXtesting.org profile for verification before registering, and the letter is valid for up to 18 months from its issue date.

4. How does scoring work on multiple-correct-answer questions in the DLOSCE?

On DLOSCE items with multiple correct answers, selecting all correct options and zero incorrect options earns full credit (1 point), while selecting only a subset of correct options without any incorrect options earns partial credit (0.5 points). However, selecting even one incorrect option results in zero credit for that item—so you should never guess blindly on options you know are wrong.

5. What happens if I pass five components of the ADEX series in 2027 but run out of time on the 18-month clock for the sixth?

Under the ADEX 18-Month Rule, all six components of the ADEX Dental Examination series must be passed within 18 months of your very first attempt at any component. If the 18-month period expires before you pass all six components, all prior component passes lapse and you must re-register and retake the entire examination series.
