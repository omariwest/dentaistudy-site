ADEX Exam Dates 2027: CDCA-WREB-CITA Sites and Registration

There is no single national examination date or fixed annual sitting calendar for the ADEX Dental Examination. Instead, the American Board of Dental Examiners (ADEX)—which unified with CDCA-WREB-CITA—administers clinical dental examinations across dozens of host dental schools and testing facilities on a location-specific, real-time calendar . Every host site sets its own testing dates, registration cutoff, facility fee, and rules on whether non-students ("Outside Candidates") may register.

Quick Answers

  • Is there one national ADEX exam date in 2027? No. Clinical ADEX dental exams are scheduled site-by-site across participating dental schools and testing centers in the United States, Canada, Puerto Rico, and Jamaica.
  • How is the 2026–2027 ADEX Dental season defined? The current ADEX Dental Examination manual cycle runs from August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027. Effective August 1, 2026, Periodontal became a mandatory clinical component alongside Endodontics, Prosthodontics, Anterior Restorative, and Posterior Restorative, and the ADA's computer-based DLOSCE became an integrated part of the ADEX Dental series.
  • Can graduated dentists or students from other schools book any 2027 site? No. You may only register at locations marked Outside Candidates: YES that still have open outside seats remaining. Sites marked Outside Candidates: NO restrict registration strictly to students currently enrolled at that host institution.
  • What does TBA mean on the ADEX calendar? "To Be Announced." It indicates a host school is planning an exam administration, but the exact dates have not yet been finalized.
  • When are personal exam schedules released? Individual clinical schedules and Q&A session details are posted to your online profile approximately 2–3 weeks prior to the clinical exam. Once posted, schedule changes are not permitted.
  • What happens if a host site changes its exam date? You do not need to reapply. ADEX automatically moves your existing registration to the rescheduled date.

There is no single national examination date or fixed annual sitting calendar for the ADEX Dental Examination. Instead, the American Board of Dental Examiners (ADEX)—which unified with CDCA-WREB-CITA—administers clinical dental examinations across dozens of host dental schools and testing facilities on a location-specific, real-time calendar. Every host site sets its own testing dates, registration cutoff, facility fee, and rules on whether non-students ("Outside Candidates") may register.

Important Dynamic Calendar Notice: The official ADEX Exam Calendar (adextesting.org/exam-calendars/) updates continuously in real time as host schools finalize dates and seats are booked. Any individual site listing can reach zero capacity before its published registration deadline, so you must recheck the live calendar and your personal Candidate Profile immediately before submitting registration and payment.

No separate "2027-only" policy handbook exists yet for the full 2027 calendar year. Early and mid-2027 clinical sittings are governed by the August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027 examination season documents (published July 27, 2026 / August 2026 on adextesting.org), while sittings scheduled on or after August 1, 2027 will fall under the subsequent 2027–2028 manual cycle once released.

How the 2026–2027 ADEX Examination Season Is Defined

For the full current component and eligibility rules behind this season, use ADEX 2027 Candidate Guide & DLOSCE. For common scoring failure points, see ADEX Scoring & Fail Points.

Understanding which rulebook governs your 2027 sitting prevents costly preparation mistakes. Unlike exams tied to a January–December calendar year, the ADEX Dental Examination operates on an academic-year manual cycle:

  • Current Governing Season: August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027. All clinical criteria sheets, scoring rubrics, forms, and administrative rules posted under the August 2026 release apply to every ADEX Dental examination taken within this 12-month window—including all winter, spring, and early-summer 2027 sittings.
  • Major Structural Updates Effective August 1, 2026:
  1. Mandatory Periodontal Component: Effective August 1, 2026, the simulated-patient Periodontal examination became a required component of the ADEX Dental Examination series alongside Endodontics, Fixed Prosthodontics, Anterior Restorative, and Posterior Restorative.
  2. DLOSCE Integration: Under the joint agreement between the American Dental Association (ADA) and ADEX, the computer-based Dental Licensure Objective Structured Clinical Examination (DLOSCE) was incorporated into the ADEX Dental Examination series by August 1, 2026. Standalone DLOSCE registrations for new candidates ended after August 1, 2026, and all standalone administrations ceased after October 9, 2026.
  • What Is Not Yet Published for Late 2027: Updated Candidate Manuals and criteria sheets for the August 1, 2027 – July 31, 2028 season have not yet been published, and many late-2027 host school dates have not yet been added to the live calendar.

For a complete overview of all five clinical procedures and the computerized component, see our ADEX Dental Exam Complete Guide and ADEX Candidate Guide & DLOSCE 2027.

How the Live ADEX Site-Specific Calendar Works

The official ADEX Dental Clinical Exam Calendar (adextesting.org/exam-calendars/ and adextesting.org/adex-dental/) is a live database rather than a static PDF timetable. Because host dental schools determine when their clinic floors and simulation labs are available, dozens of separate exams occur across North America and the Caribbean every month—particularly between January and May.

Reading Each Column on the Official Calendar

When you open the ADEX Dental Examinations calendar and filter by location, date, or exam type, each row displays five critical pieces of data:

  1. Host Institution & Location: The specific dental school or clinical testing facility (city, state/province, and country) plus a link to its Site Info Sheet when uploaded. The Site Info Sheet details local equipment, handpiece connections, infection control protocols, and any site-specific Facility Fee.
  2. Exam Date: The exact multi-day or single-day window scheduled at that facility, or TBA if dates are still pending confirmation.
  3. Registration Deadline: The final date to submit standard online registration for that specific sitting. Each site has its own cutoff date—varies by host site; use the exact registration deadline shown on the live calendar.
  4. Exam Types Offered at That Sitting: A single listing may offer the full clinical battery (Restorative, Prosth, Endo, Perio) or only selected modules (for example, Prosth, Endo, Perio only, or Restorative only). If you need all five hand-skill procedures in one trip, verify that both Restorative and Prosth/Endo/Perio appear on that sitting's entry.
  5. Outside Candidate Status & Real-Time Seat Counter ("i" Icon): Displays whether non-students may apply (Outside Candidates: Yes vs. Outside Candidates: No) and, when you click the information "i" icon, shows the exact number of outside candidate seats still open for each module.

Why We Do Not Republish a Static 2027 Site List

ADEX explicitly describes its clinical Exam Calendar as real-time. Host dates, registration status and outside-candidate capacity can change before a published deadline, so a copied school-by-school table can become stale quickly.

Use the live ADEX calendar and the information icon for the actual site/date decision, then confirm that the required modules and outside-candidate access are still available before paying.

Outside Candidates: YES vs. NO (and Why Listings Do Not Guarantee Seats)

Clinical preparation should also match the current published criteria. Useful evergreen guides include ADEX Restorative Errors & Fail Points and ADEX Periodontal Assessment.

For graduated dentists, internationally trained dentists completing advanced standing or licensure pathways, and dental students who need to test away from their home school, the Outside Candidates designation is the single most important filter on the calendar.

What Outside Candidates: YES vs. NO Means

  • Outside Candidates: YES: Registration is open to both external ("outside") candidates and students of record at the host school. A designated pool of operatories/stations is allocated to non-students.
  • Outside Candidates: NO: Registration is restricted exclusively to students currently enrolled at that specific institution (or, where noted by ADEX, alumni/students of record of that program). Outside candidates cannot book these dates, and those sittings will not appear as selectable registration options inside an outside candidate's portal.

Four Reasons a Calendar Listing Does Not Guarantee a Seat

A frequent source of confusion is seeing an upcoming 2027 exam listed on the public calendar and assuming a seat is available until the registration deadline. In practice, four factors control whether you can actually register:

  1. Zero Outside Seats Remaining Before the Deadline: Registration operates on a strict first-come, first-served basis. A school can be marked Outside Candidates: Yes with a future deadline, yet clicking the "i" icon reveals 0 outside candidate seats available. ADEX does not maintain waitlists; your only option is to monitor the "i" icon for cancellations or select another open site.
  2. Module-Specific Seat Caps: A site offering both Restorative and Prosth, Endo, Perio tracks outside seats separately for each group. One section may still have seats open while the other has reached zero.
  3. Unverified or Pending Candidate Profile: Even when outside seats are open, the "Register for an Exam" button inside your profile only displays exams for which your account is verified and eligible. If your profile status is still Pending, you cannot lock in a seat.
  4. Past Registration Deadline: Once a site's registration deadline passes, it disappears from standard self-service portal registration (late entry requires contacting ADEX directly and paying an additional fee if space and materials allow).

Step-by-Step Registration Through Your ADEX Candidate Profile

All ADEX registration takes place online through Candidates.ADEXtesting.org. Host dental schools cannot register you and can only answer questions about their physical facility, equipment, materials, or Site Info Sheet.

Step 1: Create and Verify Your Candidate Profile Early

Do not wait until a 2027 site opens to create your account. ADEX states that profile verification generally takes 2–3 business days after you upload required items:

  • Legal Name Accuracy: Your profile name must exactly match the legal first and last names on your valid, unexpired, signature-bearing U.S. government-issued photo ID (avoid accent marks or tildes).
  • Email Best Practices: Use a personal email address you check permanently. ADEX advises avoiding school email addresses (which may expire after graduation) and AOL addresses, and recommends adding @ADEXtesting.org to your safe-sender list so Gmail or institutional firewalls do not block confirmation emails.
  • Two Required Verification Uploads:
  1. Photo: A clear "selfie" or professional head-and-shoulders image (do not upload a photo of a passport or driver's license).
  2. Qualifying Document or School Verification: Current students select their school and anticipated graduation date; graduates upload their diploma or qualifying proof of graduation. (Note: If you registered as a student, your profile automatically reverts to Pending 60 days after your graduation date until you upload proof of graduation.)

Step 2: Check Live Calendar Availability and Site Info Sheets

Before clicking register, review the live calendar on adextesting.org/exam-calendars/, click the "i" icon to confirm outside seats are greater than zero for every module you need, and read the host school's Site Info Sheet so you know what instruments or handpieces you must supply.

Step 3: Register and Pay in Full via Your Profile

Log into Candidates.ADEXtesting.org and click "Register for an Exam":

  • Only exams for which you are eligible and authorized will display.
  • Payment Rules: Payment in full is required at the time of registration using Visa or MasterCard only.
  • Current 2026–2027 Series Fee: $2,995 for the full ADEX Dental Examination series (which includes clinical Periodontal, Endodontic, Prosthodontic, Anterior Restorative, and Posterior Restorative exams, the computer-based DLOSCE, and one complimentary clinical retake per clinical procedure, though you remain responsible for the $150 typodont fee per procedure and any site-specific Facility Fee).

Step 4: Understand Deadlines, Late Registration, Transfers, and Withdrawals

Each clinical site has an individual Registration Deadline posted on the calendar. If you miss that date or need to switch locations:

  • Registering After the Deadline: Because seats fill first-come, first-served, late registration is only possible if capacity and typodont materials remain. You must submit a request through Contact Us with your full name, desired location(s), exam dates, and required sessions. Approved late registrations incur a $345 Extended Registration & Transfer Fee on top of standard exam and facility fees.
  • Transferring Exam Sites: Site transfers are never guaranteed and depend on seat availability, timing, and materials. Submitting a transfer request after a site's Clinical Registration Deadline incurs the $345 Extended Registration & Transfer Fee.
  • Withdrawals and Refunds:
  • Withdrawal prior to the Clinical Registration Deadline (with no component attempted) = Full refund.
  • Withdrawal after the Clinical Registration Deadline (with no component attempted) = Refund minus the $345 Extended Registration & Transfer Fee.
  • Once any component of the examination series (clinical or computer-based) has been attempted, no refund is issued for remaining untaken components.
  • Unused registration credits expire two years from the original payment date.

TBA Dates, Scheduling Requests, Schedule Posting, and Date Changes

Once you understand how seats are booked, four operational rules govern what happens between registration and exam day:

1. What TBA Means on the Calendar

When a host school entry shows TBA in the Exam Date column, the institution is planning to host an ADEX examination, but the exact dates have not yet been finalized between the school and ADEX. Candidates cannot sit an unscheduled exam; once the date is confirmed, ADEX updates the live Exam Calendar with the exact dates and registration deadline.

2. How to Submit a Clinical Scheduling Request

Multi-day clinical administrations split candidates across morning/afternoon sessions or across Day 1 and Day 2. If you have an unavoidable conflict on one specific day of a multi-day sitting:

  • When to Submit: After completing your registration, you must submit your request prior to the site's registration deadline.
  • How to Submit: Log into your Candidate Profile dashboard, select Contact Us, choose the Scheduling Request category, and specify both the exact exam day requested and the reason (such as religious observance, residency interview, or wedding).
  • No Guarantee: ADEX states it will do its best to honor timely requests, but accommodation cannot be guaranteed.

3. When Candidate Schedules Are Normally Posted

ADEX posts individualized candidate schedules and Q&A session information directly to your online profile approximately 2–3 weeks prior to the clinical examination. Once schedules are released, schedule changes are no longer permitted. Do not book non-refundable return flights assuming you will finish on the morning of Day 1 before your schedule is officially published.

4. What Happens If a Site's Examination Date Changes

If a host school and ADEX reschedule an examination date after you have registered, you do not need to reapply. Under official ADEX FAQ policy, your registration is automatically moved to the new date.

How DLOSCE Integration and the 18-Month Rule Affect 2027 Candidates

For the computer-based clinical-reasoning side of the series, use the existing ADEX OSCE overview together with the current DLOSCE manual.

While this guide focuses on clinical site dates, every 2027 ADEX Dental candidate must coordinate their clinical calendar with the computer-based DLOSCE (Dental Licensure Objective Structured Clinical Examination):

  • Separate Scheduling at Prometric: Unlike the simulated-patient hand-skill exams (which happen at host dental schools), the DLOSCE is administered year-round at secure Prometric Test Centers after you register for the ADEX Dental series.
  • Two-Day Prometric Window: The DLOSCE requires two testing appointments (Day 1 and Day 2) that must be scheduled and completed within 14 calendar days of one another as a single attempt.
  • The Strict 18-Month Completion Rule: Under ADEX policy, you must pass all components of the ADEX Dental Examination series (the DLOSCE plus all five clinical hand-skill components) within 18 months of your initial attempt at any component in the series. If you fail to pass every component within 18 months—or if you fail the same component three consecutive times—you must repeat the entire examination series.

(Note: Candidates who registered under the legacy DSE OSCE pathway prior to the August 2026 transition were subject to ADEX's December 31, 2026 initial-attempt cutoff; new candidates in 2027 take the integrated DLOSCE pathway.)

For full details on DLOSCE structure and retake waiting periods, read our ADEX Candidate Guide & DLOSCE 2027.

Why You Must Verify Your Target State Licensing Jurisdiction First

Do not confuse ADEX testing with state licensure. The ADEX exam overview explains the broader role of the examination, while your target board controls licensure acceptance.

ADEX (American Board of Dental Examiners) is a test developer and examination administrator—not a state dental licensing board. Passing the ADEX Dental Examination does not automatically grant a dental license, nor does ADEX decide which exam components a given state requires.

Before locking in a 2027 exam site and paying $2,995 plus facility fees, verify two facts directly with the state dental board where you plan to practice:

  1. Jurisdiction Acceptance: According to ADEX's official Acceptance Map (updated September 9, 2026), almost all U.S. jurisdictions accept the ADEX Dental Examination for initial licensure, with the notable exceptions of Delaware and New York (depicted in grey as not accepting ADEX Dental for initial licensure).
  2. State-Specific Time Limits and Score Validity: Some state boards impose strict limits on how long exam scores remain valid between passing ADEX and submitting a license application, or require specific supporting credentials (such as CODA-accredited graduation or postgraduate residency completion). Always verify current rules directly on your target state board's official website.

Practical Scenarios for 2027 ADEX Candidates

Scenario 1: Graduated Dentist Seeking a Complete Spring 2027 Sitting (Outside Candidates: YES)

  • Situation: Dr. M graduated from dental school in 2025 and needs to complete all five clinical procedures (Restorative, Prosth, Endo, Perio) in a single trip in early 2027.
  • Planning Strategy: Dr. M creates a Candidate Profile at least three weeks ahead of target registration deadlines, uploads a valid head-and-shoulders photo and dental diploma, and waits 2–3 business days for Verified status. On adextesting.org/exam-calendars/, Dr. M filters out all Outside Candidates: No sites and checks the "i" icon on Outside Candidates: Yes locations offering both Restorative and Prosth, Endo, Perio (such as Cleveland Dental Institute in Ashtabula, OH on January 9–10, 2027, while seats remain). Because individual schedules are released 2–3 weeks before the exam, Dr. M books flexible travel covering the full exam window.

Scenario 2: Candidate Needing Only a Retake in One Module (e.g., Restorative Only)

  • Situation: Dr. K passed DLOSCE and Prosth, Endo, Perio in late 2026 but failed Posterior Restorative. Under the 18-month rule, Dr. K must pass Restorative before mid-2028 and wants a 2027 retake site.
  • Planning Strategy: Dr. K does not need a site offering all four modules—any Outside Candidates: Yes site offering Restorative with open outside seats qualifies. Because this is Dr. K's second clinical attempt in Restorative, the clinical exam fee is covered under the series' complimentary retake policy, though Dr. K must still pay the $150 typodont fee and the new host site's Facility Fee at registration.

Scenario 3: Candidate With a Residency Interview or Religious Observance Conflict

  • Situation: Dr. R registers for a two-day Saturday–Sunday clinical sitting in March 2027, and later realizes Saturday conflicts with a religious observance.
  • Planning Strategy: Provided the site's Registration Deadline has not yet passed, Dr. R immediately logs into Candidates.ADEXtesting.org, clicks Contact Us -> Scheduling Request, and requests Sunday placement with the reason clearly stated. If Dr. R waited until after the registration deadline or until schedules were released 2–3 weeks before the exam, no schedule modification would be permitted.

Common Mistakes That Delay 2027 ADEX Registration

  1. Assuming Every Listed 2027 School Accepts Outside Candidates: Many high-volume dental school sittings in January–April 2027 are internal exams (Outside Candidates: No) exclusively for enrolled D4/D3 students. Always check the Outside Candidate column and "i" icon first.
  2. Waiting Until the Registration Deadline to Apply: Popular Outside Candidates: Yes sites frequently hit 0 outside candidate seats available weeks before the published Registration Deadline.
  3. Creating a Profile on Registration Day: Profile verification takes 2–3 business days. If you upload your selfie and diploma on the day the last outside seat is open, someone with an already-verified profile will take the seat first.
  4. Booking Non-Refundable Travel Before the 2–3 Week Schedule Release: You will not know your exact clinic rotation hours until ADEX posts your schedule 2–3 weeks prior to the exam.
  5. Overlooking the Site Info Sheet: Facility fees, handpiece couplings (4-hole vs. 5-hole/6-pin fiber optic), ultrasonic scaler connections, and local infection-control rules vary by host dental school and are only published in each site's Site Info Sheet.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Why doesn't a 2027 exam I see on the public ADEX calendar show up when I log into my profile?

ADEX lists four primary reasons in its official FAQs: (1) the site's registration deadline has already passed, (2) the exam site has reached full capacity (0 outside candidate seats available), (3) the host school does not permit outside candidates (Outside Candidates: No), or (4) your Candidate Profile is still in Pending status awaiting document verification.

2. Is there a waitlist if an outside-candidate exam site is full?

No. ADEX explicitly states that it does not offer waitlists for exams. However, if a registered candidate withdraws, seat counts update in real time—so checking the "i" icon periodically on adextesting.org/exam-calendars/ can reveal newly reopened outside seats.

3. Can I register for a 2027 clinical sitting after the site's Registration Deadline has passed?

Only if space and typodont materials remain available, and only by contacting ADEX directly via Contact Us with your name, desired location, exam dates, and required modules. Approved late registrations incur a $345 Extended Registration & Transfer Fee.

4. Should I take the computer-based DLOSCE before or after my 2027 clinical exam date?

ADEX does not mandate a strict sequence between the computer-based DLOSCE and the clinical modules, provided you complete all components within 18 months of your first attempt at any component in the series. Many candidates schedule the DLOSCE at a Prometric center before their clinical dates so they can focus exclusively on typodont hand skills in the final weeks before travel.

5. What happens if I miss my clinical check-in or arrive without valid identification?

Under the ADEX Candidate Guide, candidates who arrive late must still complete clinic setup and procedures within the remaining allotted time or are recorded as a "No Show," forfeiting both the exam attempt and all registration fees. You must present valid, unexpired physical identification matching your profile name (digital photos or expired IDs are not accepted).
