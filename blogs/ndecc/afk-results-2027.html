AFK Results 2027: Score Reports, Passing & Next Steps

The National Dental Examining Board of Canada reports the Assessment of Fundamental Knowledge (AFK) using a test-equated, re-scaled passing standard of 75. A score of 75 or greater is reported as Pass. A result below 75 is reported as Fail, and unsuccessful candidates receive their test-equated, re-scaled score.

The current NDEB AFK page does not publish a fixed 2027 result-release date or a guaranteed number of weeks for every sitting. Candidates should therefore avoid planning around unofficial countdowns and instead monitor NDEBConnect and NDEB communications after the examination.

Quick Answers

  • Passing standard: A test-equated, re-scaled score of 75 or greater.
  • Is 75 equal to 75% correct? No. NDEB explicitly says the rescaled score is not a percentage-correct score.
  • Do failed candidates receive a score? Yes. Candidates who fail receive their test-equated, re-scaled score.
  • Do candidates receive a subject-by-subject breakdown? No. The current AFK page says no further breakdown is available.
  • Maximum AFK attempts: Three.
  • What happens after passing? You become eligible to register for the Assessment of Clinical Judgement (ACJ).
  • Can a failed candidate request score verification? Yes. NDEB allows verification of scores for failed candidates through NDEBConnect; the current fee is $200 CAD.

How AFK Scoring Works

The AFK contains 200 single-answer multiple-choice questions delivered in two parts.

NDEB uses test equating so scores from different exam versions remain comparable.

The important result rule is:

  • 75 or greater: Pass.
  • Below 75: Fail.

The number 75 is a rescaled score, not a raw percentage.

NDEB specifically explains that a candidate with a score of 74 may be one or several questions away from the passing standard. You cannot convert a 74 into “one question short” or assume that 75 means exactly 75% of questions correct.

The exam is also not graded on a curve. The passing standard is set against the level of competence required, not against how many other candidates pass.

What Appears on a Failed AFK Result

Current NDEB guidance says an unsuccessful AFK candidate receives:

  • a Fail outcome;
  • the test-equated, re-scaled score.

No further breakdown is available.

That means you should not expect an official report identifying separate scores for pharmacology, pathology, restorative dentistry, periodontics or other subject areas.

Your remediation plan therefore needs to combine the official score with your own preparation history, practice performance and known weak areas.

When Will 2027 AFK Results Be Released?

NDEB's current AFK page does not provide a fixed result-release date for the February or August 2027 sittings.

Do not convert historical release dates into a guaranteed 2027 timetable.

The safe approach is:

  1. monitor your NDEBConnect profile;
  2. watch the email account attached to your NDEB profile;
  3. rely on NDEB's official notice when the result is released;
  4. do not treat social-media result posts as an official deadline.

If NDEB publishes a sitting-specific release notice later, that notice should replace any earlier estimate.

Passing the February AFK can make a candidate eligible for ACJ, but do not assume that every February AFK candidate will automatically secure a May ACJ seat. Registration availability, result timing and the ACJ registration rules all matter.

What Happens After You Pass AFK

Passing AFK unlocks eligibility for the Assessment of Clinical Judgement (ACJ).

The current ACJ registration process is:

  1. register and pay in NDEBConnect while registration is open;
  2. after the deadline, receive scheduling instructions;
  3. schedule the electronic examination through Prometric when applicable.

Since September 2026, NDEB also uses a two-registration-period model for AFK, ACJ and Virtual OSCE:

  • an initial registration period;
  • a second period around 6–8 weeks before the exam if seats become available because of withdrawals or added capacity.

Passing AFK does not guarantee that a specific ACJ location or sitting will still have a seat.

See ACJ Exam Dates 2027 and ACJ Registration 2027.

What Happens If You Fail AFK

You can repeat the AFK, but the maximum is three attempts.

Do not interpret the three-attempt rule as permission to rush into the next available sitting without fixing the reason for the unsuccessful result.

A failed result should trigger three decisions:

  • whether to request score verification;
  • whether your preparation strategy needs a major change;
  • which future AFK sitting gives enough time to prepare while registration is still available.

Score Verification

NDEB provides score verification to candidates who have failed.

For an electronic AFK, verification involves:

  • manually comparing your recorded responses with the answer key;
  • manually recalculating the score to confirm the final grade.

It is a verification of calculation and recording, not a new academic evaluation of whether a different answer should have been accepted.

For paper-based AFK administrations, the Scantron answer sheet is manually compared with the answer key and the score is recalculated.

The current NDEB fee page lists $200 CAD for Verification of Score for AFK, ACJ or NDECC.

You request it through:

NDEBConnect → Service Requests → New Service Request → Verification of Score

NDEB's FAQ specifies that score verification is available to examinees who failed the examination.

A Score of 74 Does Not Mean One Question Short

This misconception is common enough that NDEB addresses it directly.

The AFK uses equating and rescaling. Question sets can differ in difficulty, and the standardized score is designed to preserve a consistent passing standard.

Therefore:

  • 75 is not 75%;
  • 74 does not map to a known raw number of correct answers;
  • comparing recalled question counts with another candidate does not tell you whether your score should have passed.

Candidate Scenarios

Scenario 1: Pass in February 2027

You become eligible for ACJ. Check the live ACJ registration status immediately rather than assuming a May place is guaranteed.

Scenario 2: Fail with a score of 74

Do not interpret 74 as “one question away.” Decide whether a $200 score verification is worthwhile, then build a remediation plan before using another of your three attempts.

Scenario 3: Fail on the first attempt

You still have up to two further AFK attempts under the current maximum. Use the next sitting only if your preparation and registration timing make sense.

Scenario 4: You cannot see detailed subject scores

That is expected. The current AFK page says no further result breakdown is provided beyond the failed candidate's test-equated, re-scaled score.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating 75 as 75% correct.
  • Assuming a score of 74 means exactly one missed question.
  • Expecting a subject-by-subject official score report.
  • Assuming a fixed 2027 result date that NDEB has not published.
  • Believing a score verification is a re-marking of clinical judgement.
  • Forgetting the maximum of three AFK attempts.
  • Waiting too long after a pass to check ACJ registration availability.

FAQs

1. What score passes the AFK?

A test-equated, re-scaled score of 75 or greater.

2. Do passing candidates receive a numerical score?

The current AFK page reports a Pass at 75 or greater. Failed candidates receive their test-equated, re-scaled score.

3. When are 2027 AFK results released?

NDEB does not currently publish a fixed 2027 release date on the AFK page. Monitor NDEBConnect and official NDEB communications.

4. How many times can I take AFK?

A maximum of three attempts.

5. Can I request a manual check of a failed result?

Yes. NDEB offers score verification for failed candidates. The current fee is $200 CAD.
