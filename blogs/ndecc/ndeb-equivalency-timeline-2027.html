NDEB Equivalency Timeline 2027: Planning AFK, ACJ and NDECC

Mapping out an NDEB equivalency timeline 2027 requires more than copying exam dates onto a calendar. Under National Dental Examining Board of Canada (NDEB) rules, every stage of the Equivalency Process is gated by sequential prerequisites: credential verification must finish before you can register for the Assessment of Fundamental Knowledge (AFK), AFK results must post before you can book the Assessment of Clinical Judgement (ACJ), ACJ results must post before you enter the National Dental Clinical Examination (NDECC) queue, and NDECC evaluation sessions determine when you can advance to the Virtual OSCE.

Because registration windows for the AFK and ACJ close months before exam day—and often fill within minutes—a single missed registration timestamp can shift your entire pathway by six to twelve months. This planning guide explains how the stages interact in 2027, compares NDEB's two-window AFK/ACJ registration rules with the NDECC's separate three-stage booking system, highlights the May 2027 five-year NDECC enforcement cutoff, and walks through realistic candidate timelines. For full sitting-by-sitting tables, refer to our NDEB Exam Calendar 2027 and NDECC Equivalency Process Overview.

Quick Answers: Planning Your 2027 NDEB Equivalency Pathway

  • How long does initial NDEB credential verification currently take? (Official NDEB Fact) NDEB's current credential verification processing time is approximately 10 weeks once all required documents are received in the proper format. Missing or improperly certified documents reset or delay this queue.
  • Can an applicant starting in late 2026 finish the entire Equivalency Process in 2027? (Planning Reality) Completing credential verification, passing the AFK, passing the ACJ, sitting the NDECC, and receiving NDECC evaluation results within a single 12-month calendar year is virtually impossible under published 2027 registration deadlines. There is no single official completion time. The NDEB says timing varies with registration availability and the number of attempts; its own sample pathways span multiple examination cycles.
  • How many attempts are allowed for the AFK and ACJ? (Official NDEB Fact) Candidates may attempt the AFK a maximum of three times and the ACJ a maximum of three times. Both exams require a test-equated, re-scaled score of 75 or greater to pass.
  • Does passing the AFK or ACJ automatically guarantee a seat in the next exam? (Official NDEB Fact) No. Passing an exam unlocks eligibility in NDEBConnect, but you must still secure an open seat during an active registration period before capacity fills.
  • How long do candidates have to pass the NDECC, and what changes in May 2027? (Official NDEB Fact) Candidates have five years to pass both components of the NDECC. Effective in May 2027, candidates who have not demonstrated minimal competence on both NDECC components after five years will cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process and will not be able to reapply.

Core Mechanics That Control Your 2027 Schedule

1. Why Passing One Exam Does Not Guarantee a Seat in the Next

Every NDEB exam requires an active pass on the preceding assessment before NDEBConnect unlocks registration. Two structural delays prevent seamless back-to-back testing:

  • Result-Release Lag vs. First-Window Openings: First-window registration for the next exam opens months before the preceding exam is even held. For example, First Registration for the May 4, 2027 ACJ opens on December 8, 2026—two months before the February 5, 2027 AFK takes place.
  • Capacity Caps: Exam seats at Prometric centres (for AFK, ACJ, and Virtual OSCE) and at the Ottawa Test Centre (for NDECC) are strictly capped. Even when your eligibility unlocks on time, you can only register when an active registration window is open and seats remain available.

2. AFK and ACJ Two-Registration-Period Policy (Effective September 1, 2026)

Under NDEB's registration policy effective September 1, 2026, both the AFK and ACJ follow a standardized two-window model:

  • First Registration Period: Opens on the scheduled date at 10:00 ET and closes immediately as soon as all exam locations fill. (North American sites open first; Australia/New Zealand sites open one week later with regional transfer lock-ins.)
  • Second Registration Period: Opens approximately 6 to 8 weeks before the exam to release seats freed by withdrawals or expanded capacity. Seat availability in this second window is never guaranteed, and candidates cannot transfer between registration periods.

For step-by-step booking instructions, see our AFK Registration 2027 and ACJ Registration 2027 guides.

3. NDECC's Separate Three-Stage Registration System

Unlike the AFK and ACJ, the NDECC is delivered exclusively at NDEB's Ottawa facility and uses a three-stage priority registration flow:

  • Stage 1 — First-Attempt Offer After Passing ACJ: Once you pass the ACJ, NDEB offers you registration for your first NDECC attempt. If you accept, you bypass public seat competition for that initial sitting.
  • Stage 2 — 9:00 a.m. ET Priority Window (Canadian Citizens & Permanent Residents): Candidates who declined or missed their initial first-attempt offer—or who are registering to retake one or both NDECC components—and who hold approved proof of Canadian citizenship or permanent residency in NDEBConnect can register starting at 9:00 a.m. ET on the scheduled registration opening date.
  • Stage 3 — 10:00 a.m. ET General Registration Window: All other eligible NDECC candidates log in to register starting at 10:00 a.m. ET on the scheduled registration date.

During NDECC booking, the session dates you choose are tentative until registration closes and NDEB emails your confirmed Ottawa dates. For complete rules, read NDECC Registration 2027.

4. The Five-Year NDECC Clock & May 2027 Enforcement Consequences

NDEB enforces a strict five-year window from passing the ACJ (when you become eligible for the NDECC) to successfully passing both the Clinical Skills and Situational Judgement components of the NDECC:

  • May 2027 Candidacy Termination Rule: Effective May 2027, candidates who have not demonstrated minimal competence on both components of the NDECC after five years will cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process and will not be able to reapply to the Equivalency Process.
  • Narrow Extension Exceptions: Time extensions beyond five years require formal application and documentation proving either exceptional medical circumstances or an inability to register for the NDECC at least three times within five years.
  • May 1, 2027 Legacy ACS Re-Application Cutoff: Separately, NDEB states that effective May 1, 2027, examinees who previously failed the legacy Assessment of Clinical Skills (ACS) three times will no longer be eligible to reapply to the Equivalency Process.

Four Realistic Candidate Timeline Scenarios for 2027

Scenario 1: Candidate Beginning Application in Late 2026

  • November – December 2026: Create your NDEBConnect profile, submit certified academic transcripts, degree certificates, and identity documents.
  • February – March 2027: Assuming all documents pass initial screening and two-stage verification without deficiency notices, credential verification finishes (~10 weeks from receipt of your complete file)—too late for the February 2027 AFK (whose final registration deadline is January 4, 2027), but right on time for the March 2, 2027 (10:00 ET) First Registration opening of the August 13, 2027 AFK.
  • August 13, 2027: Sit the AFK in Canada (or an international centre if later announced).
  • October 2027: Receive AFK results (~8 weeks post-exam)—positioning you to register in December 2027 for a Spring 2028 ACJ and enter the NDECC in late 2028 or 2029.

Scenario 2: February 2027 AFK Candidate

  • February 5 or 6, 2027: Sit the AFK (Canada on Feb 5; Australia/New Zealand on Feb 6).
  • Late March – Early April 2027: Receive AFK pass (~8 weeks post-exam).
  • May 2027 vs. November 2027 ACJ Choice:
  • Optimistic path: If your AFK pass posts before April 5, 2027 at 21:00 ET and seats remain open in the May ACJ's Second Registration Period (~6–8 weeks pre-exam), you could enter the May 4/5, 2027 ACJ.
  • Dependable baseline path: Because second-window ACJ seats are never guaranteed, plan around June 8, 2027 at 10:00 ET to secure a first-window seat for the November 2, 2027 ACJ, followed by your first NDECC sitting in 2028.

Scenario 3: May 2027 ACJ Candidate

  • May 4 or 5, 2027: Sit the ACJ (Canada/U.S. on May 4; Australia/New Zealand on May 5).
  • Late June – July 2027: Receive ACJ pass (~8 weeks post-exam).
  • Second-Half 2027 or Early 2028 NDECC: Upon passing the ACJ, NDEB extends a first-attempt registration offer for an upcoming NDECC session. Because the 30 March – 3 June 2027 NDECC session closes registration on February 1, 2027, a May 2027 ACJ passer enters either a second-half 2027 NDECC session (dates not yet published as of November 17, 2026) or an early 2028 NDECC session, with results arriving ~8 weeks after the assigned evaluation session.

Scenario 4: Candidate Approaching the NDECC Five-Year Limit

  • Immediate Audit (November 2026): Check the exact date of your first ACJ pass in NDEBConnect. If your five-year window expires in May 2027 or later in 2027, you face mandatory termination from the Equivalency Process under the May 2027 enforcement rule if either component remains unpassed.
  • Action Plan: Target the 30 March – 3 June 2027 NDECC window (registration opens January 19, 2027 at 10:00 ET, or 9:00 ET for verified Canadian citizens/permanent residents). Remember that NDECC grading occurs across four annual evaluation sessions (~8 weeks post-evaluation)—leaving minimal time for another retake within 2027 if one component fails.

What Is Impossible or Unrealistic Within One Calendar Year

Setting realistic expectations prevents rushed exam attempts that burn one of your three lifetime AFK or ACJ tries:

  1. Starting From Scratch in 2027 and Finishing NDECC in 2027 (Impossible): Even if you submit documents on January 2, 2027, the ~10-week credential verification process finishes in mid-March 2027—missing the February AFK. Sitting the August 13, 2027 AFK produces results in October 2027, making the November 2, 2027 ACJ (registration deadline October 4, 2027) mathematically unreachable.
  2. Sitting February AFK, May ACJ, and NDECC All Inside 2027 (Highly Unrealistic): Even if a February 2027 AFK candidate catches a rare second-window cancellation for the May 4, 2027 ACJ, ACJ results released after the official ACJ result period leave limited room to complete an unannounced fall NDECC sitting and receive post-evaluation results before December 31, 2027.
  3. Treating Second-Window Registration as a Guaranteed Backup (Unrealistic): Relying on the 6-to-8-week pre-exam Second Registration Period for AFK or ACJ means gambling on other candidates withdrawing. Always treat First Registration opening timestamps as non-negotiable deadlines.

What to Do When a Later 2027 Session Is Not Yet Published

As of November 17, 2026, NDEB's Examination Dates and Locations schedule has not yet published second-half 2027 NDECC session windows, international locations for the August 2027 AFK, or international locations for the November 2027 ACJ. When your target milestone is unannounced:

  • Anchor Study Backward From Historical Seasonal Windows: NDEB historically delivers NDECC blocks across spring, summer/fall, and late-autumn windows in Ottawa. Build your clinical practice and Situational Judgement study plan around a seasonal target window rather than waiting for an announcement to start studying.
  • Keep Your NDEBConnect Profile "Registration-Ready" Months Early: Upload approved proof of Canadian citizenship or permanent residency via NDEBConnect Service Requests well in advance if eligible (to unlock the 9:00 a.m. ET NDECC priority tier), verify your handedness (left/right) and exam language (English/French), and keep a high-limit credit card ready.
  • Monitor Official NDEB Channels Weekly: Check ndeb-bned.ca/examination-dates-and-locations/ and ndeb-bned.ca/news/ weekly—NDEB posts new session announcements and extra-seat notices (such as the December 22, 2026 at 10:00 ET additional seat release for the February 2027 AFK) directly on those pages.

Common Timeline Planning Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Submitting Unverified or Improperly Translated Documents: Any missing seal or incorrect notarization pauses the ~10-week credential verification clock and can cost you an entire six-month AFK cycle.
  2. Wasting an AFK or ACJ Attempt Just to "Try Out" the Exam: With a strict three-attempt lifetime cap on both the AFK and ACJ, sitting unprepared permanently reduces your margin for error.
  3. Forgetting to Upload Citizenship/PR Proof Before NDECC Registration: Submitting proof of Canadian citizenship or permanent residency on registration morning is too late—it must already be approved in NDEBConnect to access the 9:00 a.m. ET NDECC registration tier.
  4. Misjudging the Five-Year NDECC Clock: Candidates who pass the ACJ and delay their first NDECC attempt by two or three years risk running out of evaluation cycles before the May 2027 five-year termination rule cuts off their candidacy.
  5. Booking Non-Refundable Ottawa Travel Before NDECC Date Confirmation: The dates you pick during NDECC registration are tentative; wait for NDEB's post-registration confirmation email before purchasing locked flights.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How long does the entire NDEB Equivalency Process usually take?

(Official NDEB Fact + Planning Reality) NDEB notes that total completion time varies by candidate depending on exam registration availability and how many attempts each stage requires. The NDEB does not publish a single typical duration. Credential verification is currently about 10 weeks when documents are complete, and the remaining timeline depends on registration availability and whether each examination is passed on the first attempt.

2. How many times can I take the AFK and ACJ if I do not pass on my first try?

(Official NDEB Fact) You may take the AFK a maximum of three times and the ACJ a maximum of three times. Both assessments require a test-equated, re-scaled score of 75 or higher to pass.

3. When does the five-year NDECC time limit start, and what happens in May 2027?

(Official NDEB Fact) Candidates have five years to pass both components of the NDECC. Effective May 2027, candidates who fail to demonstrate minimal competence on both NDECC components after five years will cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process and will not be able to reapply.

4. How is NDECC registration different from AFK and ACJ registration?

(Official NDEB Fact) AFK and ACJ use a two-registration-period system (First Registration opens at 10:00 ET until full; Second Registration opens ~6–8 weeks pre-exam) via NDEBConnect and Prometric. NDECC uses a three-stage system for Ottawa testing: first-attempt ACJ passers receive an initial registration offer, approved Canadian citizens/permanent residents register at 9:00 a.m. ET, and general candidates register at 10:00 a.m. ET.

5. Do I have to apply separately for the Certification Process after passing the NDECC?

(Official NDEB Fact) No. Once you pass both components of the NDECC and complete the Equivalency Process, you can register directly in NDEBConnect for an upcoming Virtual OSCE sitting without filing a separate Certification Process application.
