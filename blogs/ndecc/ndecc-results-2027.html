NDECC Results 2027: Where to Check, Timing & Next Steps

Waiting for NDECC results 2027 is one of the most misunderstood stages of the National Dental Examining Board of Canada (NDEB) Equivalency Process. Unlike computer-based multiple-choice exams—where results are calculated from a single test date—the National Dental Clinical Examination (NDECC) is graded in batch evaluation sessions . As a result, your waiting time is measured from your cohort's scheduled evaluation session rather than the specific days you sat in Ottawa.

Quick Answers: NDECC Results 2027

Where do I check my NDECC results?

(Official NDEB Fact) Official results are posted exclusively inside your secure online NDEBConnect profile. You will receive an automated email notification when your results are available in your account; NDEB never provides results over the phone or by email.

How long after my exam date are NDECC results released?

(Official NDEB Fact) Results are not released a fixed number of weeks after your individual test date. Instead, NDEB typically releases results approximately 8 weeks after the scheduled evaluation session assigned to your testing block.

How many NDECC evaluation sessions take place per year?

(Official NDEB Fact) NDEB currently plans four evaluation sessions per year, and the evaluation period linked to your session is displayed on the NDECC calendar when you register.

What happens if I pass one component and fail the other?

(Official NDEB Fact) You do not repeat the entire examination. You only repeat the single failed component—either the Clinical Skills Component ($3,250 CAD) or the Situational Judgement Component ($3,250 CAD). If you fail both components, you must repeat the full NDECC ($6,500 CAD).

Can I request a regrade of my typodont or OSCE stations?

(Official NDEB Fact) No. NDEB offers a Verification of Score ($200 CAD) within one month (30 days) of results release to verify grade calculations and data entry, but it does not involve a physical re-evaluation of your typodont or Situational Judgement stations.

What is the next step after passing both NDECC components?

(Official NDEB Fact) Passing both components completes the Equivalency Process. You become immediately eligible to register for the Virtual OSCE ($1,750 CAD) in the Certification Process without submitting a separate Certification Process application.

How NDECC Result Timing Works: Evaluation Sessions vs. Exam Dates

For the testing windows that feed these evaluation sessions, see NDECC Exam Dates 2027 and NDECC Registration 2027.

A frequent source of confusion among candidates is assuming that two candidates who take the NDECC in the same quarter will wait the exact same number of weeks after their personal test dates. Under NDEB's operational model, that assumption is incorrect.

Official NDEB Facts on Evaluation Sessions

  1. Multi-Week Testing Blocks Feed Into Scheduled Evaluation Sessions: NDECC sittings at the Ottawa test centre run across multi-week delivery windows (for example, 4 January – 11 March 2027 and 30 March – 3 June 2027). When you register on the NDECC calendar, NDEB informs you of the evaluation period assigned to your session.
  2. Four Evaluation Sessions Annually: NDEB plans four evaluation sessions per year to grade accumulated Clinical Skills typodonts and Situational Judgement responses.
  3. The 8-Week Post-Evaluation Benchmark: NDEB states that results are typically released about 8 weeks after the evaluation session concludes—not 8 weeks after your individual examination date.
  4. No Pre-Published Exact 2027 Release Dates: NDEB does not publish fixed calendar release timestamps in advance for individual candidates; exact 2027 result release dates remain unconfirmed until NDEB uploads the batch to NDEBConnect and triggers email alerts.

Why Marking Takes Time (Official NDEB Explanation)

In its official guidance explaining NDECC processing timelines, NDEB highlights two core reasons why grading requires several weeks after an evaluation session:

  • Nationwide Examiner Mobilization & Calibration: NDEB relies on a diverse panel of practising dental professionals from across Canada who travel to Ottawa for multi-day evaluation sessions. Before grading begins, examiners complete mandatory training and calibration exercises to ensure uniform application of the NDECC Protocol.
  • Independent Dual-Examiner Grading & Psychometric Verification: Every Clinical Skills requirement is graded independently by two separate calibrated examiners. Whenever those two examiners disagree on a criterion, a third facilitator examiner is brought in to review the work until two examiners reach agreement. Following the evaluation session, NDEB conducts multi-step quality assurance, data-entry audits, and standard-setting checks across both components before releasing results.

Where & How to Check Your NDECC Results

Official NDEB Facts

  • NDEBConnect Online Profile: When grading and quality checks are finalized, NDEB posts your official component outcomes inside your NDEBConnect account.
  • Email Notification: NDEB sends an automated email to the address registered on your NDEBConnect profile letting you know your results are ready to view.
  • No Phone or Advance Disclosures: NDEB staff cannot release grades over the phone, confirm pass/fail status by email prior to portal publication, or disclose partial marks while an evaluation session is underway.

Understanding Your Two Component Results

The NDECC evaluates entry-to-practice competence across two independent components completed over two days:

  1. Clinical Skills Component: Assesses hands-on restorative, prosthodontic, and endodontic procedures on typodonts against current Canadian clinical standards outlined in the NDECC Protocol. Each procedure is evaluated against defined criteria and critical/unacceptable error thresholds.
  2. Situational Judgement Component: Assesses communication, clinical problem-solving, ethics, and patient-management judgment across 10 Situational Judgement stations (five written-answer and five conversation stations) aligned with Canadian practice standards.

Your NDEBConnect result report issues a separate outcome for each component, determining your exact next steps.

Next Steps Based on Your NDECC Result Outcome

Outcome 1: You Passed Both Components (Equivalency Process Complete)

  • Official NDEB Fact: Passing both the Clinical Skills and Situational Judgement components completes the NDEB Equivalency Process. NDEB recognizes your dental education and clinical competence as equivalent to that of a graduate from an accredited dental program.
  • Immediate Progression to the Virtual OSCE (Official NDEB Fact): NDEB explicitly confirms that candidates who have successfully completed the Equivalency Process are not required to create a separate Certification Process application. Once your NDECC pass posts in NDEBConnect, you become eligible to register directly for an upcoming Virtual OSCE sitting ($1,750 CAD fee; maximum three attempts permitted) whenever registration is open and spaces are available.
  • 2027 Virtual OSCE Target Dates (Official NDEB Facts):
  • March 1, 2027 (Canada/US & Australia/Ireland; registration closes January 18, 2027 at 21:00 ET)
  • May 20, 2027 (Canada/US; registration opens February 15, 2027 at 10:00 ET; closes April 20, 2027 at 21:00 ET)
  • November 22, 2027 (Canada; registration opens May 25, 2027 at 10:00 ET; closes October 26, 2027 at 21:00 ET)
  • DentAIstudy Planning Advice: Check NDECC After Passing & Provincial Licensing and NDEB Exam Calendar 2027 to align your Virtual OSCE sitting with provincial Dental Regulatory Authority (DRA) language and jurisprudence requirements.

Outcome 2: You Passed One Component and Failed the Other

  • Official NDEB Fact: Under NDEB's modular NDECC rules, if you pass one component and fail the other, you only need to repeat the failed component—your passed component remains credited toward NDECC completion within your five-year window.
  • Component Retake Fees (Official NDEB Fact): Re-registering for a single component costs $3,250 CAD (either Clinical Skills Component: $3,250 CAD or Situational Judgement Component: $3,250 CAD), half the $6,500 CAD full-exam fee.
  • DentAIstudy Planning Advice: Review your NDEBConnect performance breakdown immediately to identify whether clinical error categories (such as pulp exposure, adjacent tooth damage, or margin discrepancies) or situational communication domains caused the shortfall, and follow our NDECC Failed Component Retake Strategy before the next registration opening.

Outcome 3: You Failed Both Components

  • Official NDEB Fact: If you receive a failing outcome on both Clinical Skills and Situational Judgement, you must re-register for and repeat the full NDECC ($6,500 CAD) during an open registration period when seats are available.
  • DentAIstudy Planning Advice: Do not rush into the very next opening if fundamental hand-skill speed or Canadian clinical communication habits need structured remediation—plan your retake date around your remaining time under the five-year NDECC completion rule.

Component Retake Rules & the May 2027 Five-Year Rule

For the 60-month rule and May 2027 non-reapplication change, review the existing NDECC five-year deadline guide.

Before scheduling any NDECC retake in 2027, pay close attention to NDEB's strict five-year completion rule:

  • Five-Year NDECC Completion Rule (Official NDEB Fact): Candidates have five years to successfully pass both components of the NDECC.
  • May 2027 Enforcement Milestone (Official NDEB Fact): NDEB has announced that effective in May 2027, candidates who have not demonstrated minimal competence on both components of the NDECC after five years will cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process and will not be able to reapply to the Equivalency Process.
  • Time Extension Criteria (Official NDEB Fact): Requests for a time extension beyond five years are considered only for candidates who experienced exceptional medical circumstances or who were unable to register for the NDECC a minimum of three times in five years (subject to formal application and supporting documentation).
  • First-Attempt Priority vs. Retake Booking (Official NDEB Fact): First-attempt candidates who just passed the ACJ receive an initial registration offer; retake candidates register through NDEBConnect when public NDECC registration windows open (with approved Canadian citizens and permanent residents eligible at 9:00 a.m. ET and general candidates at 10:00 a.m. ET on scheduled registration dates).

How to Submit a Verification of Score (Official NDEB Procedure)

  1. Log in to your NDEBConnect account within one month (30 days) of your NDECC result release.
  2. Navigate to Service Requests and select New Service Request.
  3. Follow the prompts for Verification of Score and pay the $200 CAD fee.
  4. Keep in mind NDEB's explicit advisory: all failing results are already verified prior to release, so a Verification of Score is unlikely to change a grade unless a clerical calculation error occurred.

Practical Candidate Scenarios & What NOT to Infer From Peers

Scenario 1: Comparing Your Wait Time to Another Candidate's Exam Date

  • Why Peer Comparisons Mislead You: Suppose Candidate A sits the NDECC during the first week of the 4 January – 11 March 2027 window, while Candidate B sits during the final week of that same window. If both candidates' work is assigned to the same post-March evaluation session, Candidate A may wait roughly 16–18 weeks from their personal exam date to receive results (~8 weeks to the evaluation session + ~8 weeks post-evaluation), whereas Candidate B waits roughly 8–9 weeks from their personal exam date.
  • What NOT to Infer: Never assume your results are delayed—or that you failed—simply because another candidate received results fewer weeks after their test date than you have waited since yours. Always anchor your expectation to your assigned evaluation session, not your individual test date.

Scenario 2: One Peer Gets an Email Minutes Before You

  • What NOT to Infer: When NDEB publishes an evaluation batch in NDEBConnect, automated email notifications roll out across candidate accounts. Receiving your email 15 minutes after a study partner—or seeing a social-media post before your inbox refreshes—has zero correlation with pass or fail outcomes. Log directly into NDEBConnect rather than relying solely on email delivery speed.

Scenario 3: Approaching the May 2027 Five-Year NDECC Cutoff

  • Official Fact + Planning Advice: If you first passed the ACJ nearly five years ago and still need one component to pass, audit your exact five-year start date (the date you first passed the ACJ) in NDEBConnect immediately. Because the five-year termination rule takes effect in May 2027, candidates near the five-year mark must prioritize securing a seat in the 4 January – 11 March 2027 or 30 March – 3 June 2027 sessions.

Common Mistakes Candidates Make While Waiting for NDECC Results

  1. Counting 8 Weeks From Their Exam Date Instead of the Evaluation Session: The 8-week benchmark runs from the evaluation session, not from the day you walked out of the Ottawa test centre.
  2. Assuming Failed Components Require Re-Sitting the Full $6,500 CAD Exam: Passing one component banks that pass within your five-year window; you only pay $3,250 CAD to repeat the single failed component.
  3. Paying $200 CAD for a Score Verification Expecting a Typodont Regrade: Verification of Score checks score math only—it never re-examines your preparations or OSCE station rubrics.
  4. Waiting Until Results Day to Research Virtual OSCE Registration Deadlines: Virtual OSCE registration windows close weeks ahead of exam day (for instance, April 20, 2027 for the May 20, 2027 sitting). Checking deadlines early ensures you do not miss the next available Virtual OSCE cohort once your NDECC pass posts.
  5. Ignoring the May 2027 Five-Year Candidacy Cutoff: Failing both components—or leaving one component unpassed—beyond five years ends Equivalency Process candidacy starting May 2027.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How long after my NDECC exam will I receive my 2027 results?

(Official NDEB Fact) NDECC results are not released a fixed number of weeks after your personal exam date. Instead, NDEB typically releases results about 8 weeks after the scheduled evaluation session linked to your exam block. You will receive an email notification as soon as your results appear in NDEBConnect.

2. Where do I check my NDECC results when they are released?

(Official NDEB Fact) Log in to your secure NDEBConnect online profile. NDEB does not provide examination results over the telephone or directly inside emails.

3. If I fail only the Clinical Skills or Situational Judgement component, do I have to retake both?

(Official NDEB Fact) No. If you pass one component and fail the other, you only repeat the single failed component ($3,250 CAD). You only repeat the full NDECC ($6,500 CAD) if you fail both components.

4. Can I ask NDEB to re-evaluate my typodont or OSCE stations if I fail?

(Official NDEB Fact) No. NDEB does not permit appeals or physical re-evaluations of NDECC grading. You may submit a Verification of Score ($200 CAD) in NDEBConnect within one month (30 days) of results release, which audits grade calculations and data entry only.

5. What happens immediately after I pass both components of the NDECC?

(Official NDEB Fact) Passing both components completes the NDEB Equivalency Process. You do not need to file a separate Certification Process application—you can register directly in NDEBConnect for an upcoming Virtual OSCE ($1,750 CAD) sitting while registration is open and spaces remain available.
