NDECC Protocol 2027: Candidate Rules, Preparation & Grading

Every candidate preparing for the National Dental Clinical Examination (NDECC) relies on one authoritative rulebook published by the National Dental Examining Board of Canada (NDEB): the NDECC Protocol . Before buying loupes, practising typodont preparations, or rehearsing Situational Judgement cases for a 2027 sitting in Ottawa, you must verify which protocol edition is currently in force and how its evaluation rules, clinic restrictions, and confidentiality policies govern exam day.

Quick Answers: NDECC Protocol Rules for 2027 Candidates

Is there a separate "2027 NDECC Protocol" yet?

(Official NDEB Fact) No. As of November 17, 2026, the latest published protocol on NDEB's Exam Resources page is the 2026 NDECC Protocol (effective June 15, 2026). That edition governs current preparation unless NDEB publishes a replacement.

What does the NDECC Protocol cover?

(Official NDEB Fact) It defines the purpose and structure of the NDECC, the examination blueprint across both components, the criterion-referenced grading and evaluation framework, clinic equipment and infection-control rules, candidate conduct standards, and examination confidentiality obligations.

What are the two components of the NDECC?

(Official NDEB Fact) The examination consists of two independent components completed over two days at the NDEB Test Centre in Ottawa: the Clinical Skills Component and the Situational Judgement Component.

How is the NDECC graded?

(Official NDEB Fact) The NDECC uses criterion-referenced standard setting rather than norm-referenced curves. Clinical Skills work is evaluated in scheduled evaluation sessions by calibrated Canadian examiners using independent dual-examiner grading (with a third facilitator examiner resolving any disagreement), and outcomes are reported as Pass or Fail per component.

Can I bring my own dental instruments or handpieces to Ottawa?

(Official NDEB Fact) No—NDEB provides standardized dental units, handpieces, instruments, typodonts, and consumables at the Ottawa Test Centre, restricting personal items strictly to approved exceptions listed in the current NDECC Equipment & Materials documentation (such as approved loupes/eyewear).

What happens if I share or discuss exam tasks after leaving the test centre?

(Official NDEB Fact) All NDECC station scenarios, clinical tasks, and evaluation checklists are strictly confidential intellectual property of NDEB. Breaching exam confidentiality or test-centre conduct rules can result in examination invalidation and disciplinary action under NDEB By-Laws and Policies.

Purpose of the NDECC Protocol & Examination Structure

Purpose of the Protocol

The NDECC Protocol serves as the official operational and academic contract between NDEB and examination candidates. Its primary function is to ensure that every candidate tested at the NDEB Test Centre in Ottawa is evaluated under uniform clinical conditions, transparent competency expectations, and standardized grading rules aligned with entry-to-practice general dentistry in Canada.

Two-Day, Two-Component Examination Structure

Under the current protocol framework, the NDECC assesses whether internationally educated dentists demonstrate clinical competence equivalent to graduates of accredited Canadian dental programs. The assessment is divided into two separate components administered across two examination days in Ottawa:

  1. Clinical Skills Component: Evaluates hands-on technical execution on simulated teeth/typodonts inside standardized clinical operatories.
  2. Situational Judgement Component: Evaluates patient-centred communication, clinical problem-solving, ethical decision-making, and Canadian practice judgment across structured simulated scenarios.

Because the two components are graded independently, candidates who pass both components complete the Equivalency Process, whereas candidates who pass one component and fail the other retain credit for the passed component and repeat only the failed component within NDEB's five-year completion rule.

Clinical Skills Component: Scope & Evaluation Rules

The hands-on requirements are covered separately in NDECC Clinical Skills 2027, while the older clinical procedures guide provides additional evergreen context.

The Clinical Skills Component tests psychomotor precision, restorative and endodontic execution, prosthodontic preparation principles, and tissue/adjacent-structure protection under timed clinical conditions. For a deeper procedural breakdown, see our NDECC Clinical Skills Component Procedures guide.

  • Standardized Typodont & Operatory Simulation: Candidates perform designated clinical procedures on mounted typodonts inside NDEB's Ottawa simulation clinics using NDEB-supplied handpieces, burs, instruments, and restorative materials.
  • Criterion-Based Procedure Assessment: Rather than subjective holistic impressions, each clinical task is broken down into defined anatomical, mechanical, and biological criteria specified in the protocol (such as preparation design, caries/defect management, restoration contour/contacts, or endodontic access/obturation standards).
  • Critical & Unacceptable Errors: The protocol identifies specific critical errors—such as iatrogenic damage to adjacent teeth, pulp chamber violations outside task parameters, or irreversible procedural mistakes—that carry decisive grading weight regardless of how well other steps were executed.
  • Infection Control & Clinic Safety: Candidates are required to maintain proper personal protective equipment (PPE), barrier protocols, sharps handling, and ergonomic clinic hygiene throughout every session.

Situational Judgement Component: Scope & Reference Alignment

For the current 10-station structure and passing logic, see NDECC Situational Judgement 2027.

The Situational Judgement Component evaluates how you communicate, gather clinical information, obtain informed consent, manage patient concerns, and apply Canadian ethical and clinical standards. The current protocol specifies 10 stations—five written-answer and five conversation stations—covering five requirements: Patient-Centered Care, Professionalism, Communication and Collaboration, Practice and Information Management, and Health Promotion. For structured station strategies, see our NDECC Situational Judgement Component Explained guide.

  • 10 Situational Judgement stations: Under NDEB's current NDECC structure, candidates rotate through 10 stations: five written-answer stations and five conversation stations designed to mirror interactions with patients, parents/guardians, or dental team members.
  • Core Assessed Domains: Examiners assess clinical reasoning, structured history-taking, diagnostic synthesis, treatment-option explanation, risk/benefit communication, cultural sensitivity, and adherence to Canadian regulatory and ethical duties.
  • Alignment With the Situational Judgement Reference List: NDEB publishes an official Situational Judgement Reference List on its Exam Resources page pointing candidates to Canadian clinical practice guidelines, infection-control standards, medical emergency protocols, and dental ethics/jurisprudence principles that underpin station expectations.

Examination Blueprint & Grading Framework

The existing protocol, blueprint and grading guide gives broader background, while NDECC Results 2027 explains what Pass/Fail outcomes mean after evaluation.

Understanding how NDEB constructs and grades the NDECC prevents wasted study effort and unrealistic score assumptions. For a dedicated look at competency domains and rubric logic, consult our NDECC Protocol Blueprint & Grading Criteria guide.

The Examination Blueprint

  • Competency-Mapped Sampling: The NDECC blueprint defines the entry-to-practice dental competencies sampled across both components, ensuring balanced coverage of restorative dentistry, prosthodontics, endodontics, periodontics, oral surgery/diagnostics, patient communication, and professionalism.
  • No Guaranteed Fixed Case Combination: While the blueprint defines the overarching competency domains and procedural categories, NDEB varies specific teeth, clinical scenarios, and task combinations across testing blocks to protect examination security.

Criterion-Referenced Grading & Evaluation Sessions

  • Criterion-Referenced Standard Setting: Candidates are judged against an established entry-to-practice competence standard—not ranked against peers testing on the same day. There is no quota limiting how many candidates can pass in a given week.
  • Batch Evaluation Sessions: Clinical Skills typodonts are graded during scheduled evaluation sessions (NDEB plans four evaluation sessions annually) by calibrated Canadian dentists who complete mandatory standardization training before scoring begins.
  • Independent Dual-Examiner Verification: Every Clinical Skills requirement is scored independently by two calibrated examiners. If the two examiners disagree on a criterion, a third facilitator examiner reviews the work until two examiners reach consensus.
  • Pass / Fail Reporting: The protocol provides an overall NDECC Pass/Fail result, Pass/Fail for each component, and Pass/Fail for each requirement.

Candidate Conduct, Equipment Rules & Confidentiality Restrictions

Strict adherence to NDEB's operational rules is mandatory from check-in until final departure from the Ottawa Test Centre. Violations can lead to immediate dismissal, grade invalidation, or long-term candidacy sanctions under NDEB By-Laws and Policies.

1. Check-In, Identification & Test-Centre Conduct

  • Government-Issued Photo ID: Candidates must present valid, unexpired original government-issued photo identification matching their NDEBConnect registration profile character-for-character.
  • Prohibited Items: Mobile phones, smartwatches, wearable electronics, cameras, recording devices, personal notes, reference books, and unauthorized dental instruments are strictly barred from clinical and briefing zones.
  • Punctuality & Station Instructions: Late arrival after scheduled check-in deadlines results in denied entry without refund. Candidates must follow invigilator start/stop announcements immediately—continuing to work on a typodont or speak in a station after time is called constitutes an examination irregularity.

2. Equipment, Materials & Companion Orientation Guides

  • Standardized Clinic Supply: NDEB's Equipment and Materials list details the exact dental units, electric/air handpieces, bur blocks, matrix systems, restorative materials, and protective gear supplied at every workstation.
  • Permitted Personal Items: NDEB provides the necessary clinical equipment, instruments and supplies. Bring only items expressly permitted by the current protocol, Practical Guide or an approved accommodation; staff may inspect accessories at check-in.
  • Using the Practical Guide & Orientation Resources: Before travelling to Ottawa, candidates should review NDEB's Practical Guide and Candidate Orientation Resources so they know how typodonts are mounted/removed, how replacement teeth or materials are requested during a session, and how station warning bells operate.

3. Confidentiality & Intellectual Property Restrictions

  • Binding Non-Disclosure Obligation: All NDECC station prompts, patient scripts, typodont task sheets, grading criteria, and clinic configurations are confidential intellectual property owned by NDEB.
  • Strict Prohibition on Sharing or Reconstruction: Candidates are prohibited from photographing, recording, writing down for removal, selling, posting online, or discussing specific exam stations, tooth numbers, or clinical tasks with peers, study groups, or commercial prep courses—both during multi-day exam blocks and after completing the exam.

Pre-Exam Verification Checklist for 2027 NDECC Candidates

Use this checklist in the final month—and again 48 to 72 hours—before your scheduled Ottawa examination dates:

  1. Verify Active Protocol Edition: Visit the official NDEB Exam Resources page to confirm whether the 2026 NDECC Protocol (effective June 15, 2026) remains the current edition or if NDEB has posted a newer revision.
  2. Audit Companion Documents: Re-read the current NDECC Practical Guide, Equipment & Materials List, and Situational Judgement Reference List to verify there are no last-minute changes to supplied burs, restorative materials, or PPE rules.
  3. Inspect Permitted Personal Items: Check that your magnification loupes, protective eyewear, and clinic attire/footwear comply fully with NDEB clinic dress and safety requirements.
  4. Confirm Identification & NDEBConnect Details: Verify that your government-issued photo ID is unexpired and matches your NDEBConnect name exactly, and save your test-centre check-in time and Ottawa address offline.
  5. Review Orientation Walkthroughs: Re-watch NDEB's official candidate orientation materials so clinic layout, typodont check-in/out steps, and Situational Judgement station bells feel routine on exam morning.

Common Mistakes Candidates Make With the NDECC Protocol

  1. Studying From Pre-June 2026 Summaries or Unverified Recall Notes: Relying on outdated third-party notes instead of reading the 2026 NDECC Protocol (effective June 15, 2026) directly from NDEB risks preparing for superseded clinical specifications.
  2. Assuming a "2027 Protocol" Exists Without Checking NDEB's Site: Candidates sometimes waste time searching for a "2027 NDECC Protocol" PDF when NDEB's active governing document is the June 15, 2026 edition.
  3. Overlooking the Practical Guide & Equipment List: Mastering tooth preparations at home means little if you lose valuable minutes in Ottawa struggling with unfamiliar NDEB-supplied matrix systems, handpiece couplings, or typodont mounting rules explained in the Practical Guide.
  4. Treating Situational Judgement as a Pure Oral Quiz: In the 10 Situational Judgement stations, examiners evaluate how you communicate, validate patient concerns, and structure shared decision-making—not just whether you recite textbook facts.
  5. Discussing Station Details Between Exam Days or Online: Sharing specific station scenarios or task details breaches NDEB's confidentiality agreement and can jeopardize your candidacy.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. Has NDEB published a separate 2027 NDECC Protocol?

(Official NDEB Fact) No. As of November 17, 2026, the latest protocol published on NDEB's Exam Resources page is the 2026 NDECC Protocol (effective June 15, 2026). Candidates preparing for 2027 sittings should study from this edition and recheck NDEB's Exam Resources page prior to their exam date in case NDEB issues an update.

2. What are the two components covered in the NDECC Protocol?

(Official NDEB Fact) The NDECC consists of two independent components taken over two days in Ottawa: the Clinical Skills Component (simulated dental procedures on typodonts) and the Situational Judgement Component (10 Situational Judgement stations assessing communication, clinical reasoning, and professionalism).

3. How does NDEB grade the Clinical Skills Component?

(Official NDEB Fact) Clinical Skills work is evaluated during scheduled evaluation sessions by calibrated Canadian dentists using independent dual-examiner grading. Whenever two examiners disagree on a grading criterion, a third facilitator examiner reviews the work until two examiners reach agreement.

4. Does the NDECC Protocol publish numerical passing percentages?

(Official NDEB Fact) No. The NDECC uses criterion-referenced standard setting and reports outcomes as Pass or Fail per component in NDEBConnect rather than numerical percentage scores.

5. What should I check on NDEB's website right before my 2027 exam date?

(DentAIstudy Planning Advice based on Official NDEB Resources) Always recheck the official NDEB Exam Resources page and your NDEBConnect messages in the final two weeks before your Ottawa sitting to confirm the active protocol edition, equipment and PPE rules, and check-in instructions.
