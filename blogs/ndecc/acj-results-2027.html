ACJ Results 2027: Score Reports and Retake Decisions

The Assessment of Clinical Judgement (ACJ) is the second examination in Canada's NDEB Equivalency Process. A test-equated, re-scaled score of 75 or greater is reported as Pass. A result below 75 is reported as Fail, and unsuccessful candidates receive their test-equated, re-scaled score.

The current NDEB ACJ page does not publish a fixed 2027 result-release date. Candidates should therefore monitor NDEBConnect and official NDEB communication rather than treating historical release patterns or social-media posts as a guaranteed timetable.

A passing ACJ result is especially important because it opens the NDECC stage and starts the 60-month NDECC completion clock under current NDEB by-laws.

Quick Answers

  • ACJ passing standard: 75 or greater on the test-equated, re-scaled score.
  • Is 75 equal to 75% correct? No.
  • Do failed candidates receive a score? Yes.
  • Is there a detailed section breakdown? No. Current NDEB guidance says no further breakdown is available.
  • Maximum ACJ attempts: Three.
  • What happens after passing? You become eligible for NDECC.
  • When does the NDECC five-year clock start? Current NDEB policy uses 60 months from the date you first pass the ACJ.
  • Can a failed result be verified? Yes. Current score-verification fee is $200 CAD.

How the ACJ Result Is Scored

The ACJ assesses diagnosis, treatment planning, clinical decision-making and radiographic interpretation.

NDEB currently describes the examination as a 5.5-hour electronic assessment containing approximately 120–150 single-answer and multi-answer questions.

Results use a test-equated, re-scaled score:

  • 75 or greater: Pass.
  • Below 75: Fail.

The standard is not curved against the other candidates.

NDEB also explains that 75 is not the same as answering 75% of questions correctly. Equating accounts for the relative difficulty of exam forms.

What an Unsuccessful Candidate Receives

Current ACJ guidance says candidates who fail receive:

  • a Fail outcome;
  • the test-equated, re-scaled score.

No further breakdown is available.

That means the official result does not provide separate marks for:

  • radiographic interpretation;
  • diagnosis;
  • treatment planning;
  • individual clinical disciplines.

Use your result together with your own preparation data rather than waiting for a detailed official domain report that NDEB does not provide.

What Happens After You Pass ACJ

Passing ACJ moves you to the NDECC stage.

Current NDEB guidance says that once you pass ACJ, you will be offered registration for your first NDECC attempt. If you do not accept that offer, you remain eligible to register when a later NDECC registration window opens.

The NDECC is delivered at the NDEB test centre in Ottawa throughout the year.

See NDECC Registration 2027 and NDECC Exam Dates 2027.

Passing ACJ Starts the 60-Month NDECC Clock

This is the biggest planning consequence of an ACJ pass.

Current NDEB by-laws state that an Equivalency Candidate who fails to pass NDECC within 60 months of the date they first pass ACJ can cease to be an Equivalency Candidate.

NDEB's 2026 policy update also confirms that, effective in May 2027, candidates who cannot demonstrate minimal competence on both NDECC components after five years will cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process and cannot simply reapply under the old pathway.

An extension process exists only for specified circumstances, including certain exceptional medical circumstances or inability to register for NDECC at least three times during the five-year period.

This means an ACJ pass should be treated as the start of active NDECC planning, not as a stage you can leave indefinitely.

What Happens If You Fail ACJ

You can take ACJ a maximum of three times.

Before using another attempt, decide:

  • whether to request score verification;
  • whether your clinical judgement preparation needs a major change;
  • which future ACJ registration period gives you enough preparation time;
  • whether the next available region/location is practical.

Do not interpret the three-attempt maximum as a reason to rush into a retake.

Score Verification

NDEB offers score verification to failed candidates.

For electronic examinations such as ACJ, verification includes:

  • manually comparing your recorded responses with the answer key;
  • manually recalculating the score to confirm the final grade.

It is not a new expert review of whether your diagnosis or treatment choice should have been awarded credit.

The current NDEB fee page lists $200 CAD for Verification of Score for AFK, ACJ or NDECC.

Requests are made through NDEBConnect under Service Requests.

Why a Score of 74 Is Not “One Question Short”

NDEB explicitly warns against that interpretation.

The rescaled score does not reveal the raw number of questions you answered correctly.

A candidate receiving 74 could be one or several questions away from the passing standard because the examination uses equating and rescaling.

Similarly, 75 does not mean 75% correct.

This matters when deciding whether to retake quickly. A score near 75 should not become an excuse to repeat the same preparation unchanged.

2027 Registration Context After a Fail

Current 2027 ACJ scheduling includes:

  • May 4, 2027 in the United States/Canada;
  • May 5, 2027 in Australia/New Zealand;
  • November 2, 2027 in Canada.

NDEB's registration process changed in September 2026 to include a second registration period when seats become available because of withdrawals or increased capacity.

A failed candidate should therefore check the live ACJ page and exam calendar rather than assuming registration remains continuously open.

Candidate Scenarios

Scenario 1: You pass ACJ in 2027

Start NDECC planning immediately. The 60-month period is measured from the date you first pass ACJ, and NDECC seat availability can affect how quickly you progress.

Scenario 2: You fail with a score of 74

Do not call it “one question short.” Consider the $200 verification if you have a genuine reason to confirm the calculation, then change your preparation before using another of the three attempts.

Scenario 3: You fail your second ACJ attempt

Only one ACJ attempt remains under the current maximum. A deliberate remediation cycle is more important than chasing the next date.

Scenario 4: You pass but cannot immediately secure NDECC

Monitor NDECC registration and keep your profile current. The five-year clock still runs, although NDEB has a limited extension process for candidates who meet the published criteria.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating 75 as 75% correct.
  • Assuming 74 means exactly one question below passing.
  • Expecting a detailed subject-by-subject score report.
  • Assuming a fixed 2027 result date that NDEB has not announced.
  • Believing score verification is a re-evaluation of clinical decisions.
  • Forgetting ACJ has a three-attempt maximum.
  • Passing ACJ and then ignoring the start of the NDECC 60-month clock.

FAQs

A test-equated, re-scaled score of 75 or greater.

2. Do failed candidates receive their score?

Yes. Current NDEB guidance says failed candidates receive their test-equated, re-scaled score, with no further breakdown.

3. When are 2027 ACJ results released?

4. How many times can I take the ACJ?

A maximum of three attempts.

5. What happens immediately after passing ACJ?
