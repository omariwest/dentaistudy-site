NDECC Clinical Skills 2027: Procedures and Candidate Checklist

The NDECC Clinical Skills component is the hands-on part of Canada's National Dental Examining Board Equivalency Process. It is a full-day simulated-patient examination at the NDECC Test Centre in Ottawa and, under the latest published NDECC Protocol, requires candidates to complete seven clinical requirements plus infection-control and safety procedures .

Quick Answers

How many Clinical Skills requirements are there?

Seven.

How long is the Clinical Skills component?

A full examination day.

Where is it held?

At the NDECC Test Centre in Ottawa.

Are instruments and materials supplied?

NDEB says all necessary equipment, instruments and supplies are provided.

Can the seven requirements be completed in any order?

Yes, under the current protocol.

How is the component graded?

Each requirement is Pass/Fail using competent, minimally competent and not competent performance criteria; you must pass all clinical requirements.

What happens if only Clinical Skills is failed?

You repeat the failed Clinical Skills component, not the Situational Judgement component that you already passed.

The NDECC Clinical Skills component is the hands-on part of Canada's National Dental Examining Board Equivalency Process. It is a full-day simulated-patient examination at the NDECC Test Centre in Ottawa and, under the latest published NDECC Protocol, requires candidates to complete seven clinical requirements plus infection-control and safety procedures.

As of 17 November 2026, the latest official protocol is still the 2026 NDECC Protocol effective June 15, 2026. NDEB has not published a separate 2027 protocol. Candidates preparing for a 2027 sitting should use the current protocol and Practical Guide now, then recheck the Exam Resources page before the examination in case NDEB replaces or updates either document.

Current Protocol Status for 2027

Do not search for a document called “2027 NDECC Clinical Skills Protocol” and assume an unofficial copy is current.

NDEB's Exam Resources page currently lists:

2026 NDECC Protocol — Effective June 15, 2026

That protocol governs the current structure and grading rules. NDEB also maintains a separate NDECC Practical Guide, which explains the Ottawa test centre, facilities, instruments, materials and supplies. The Practical Guide is updated when new operational information becomes available.

Your safest preparation order is:

  1. current NDECC Protocol;
  2. current NDECC Practical Guide;
  3. current orientation videos;
  4. current NDECC Tips;
  5. any NDEB notice specific to your confirmed sitting.

See NDECC Protocol 2027 for the broader two-component rules.

The Seven Clinical Skills Requirements

For the full governing rule set, use NDECC Protocol 2027. The existing Clinical Skills procedures guide provides additional evergreen context.

The current protocol lists seven requirements:

Clinical requirementWhat the candidate is asked to demonstrate
Class II amalgam preparationBasic operative preparation consistent with current Canadian standards
Class II amalgam restorationPlacement and finishing of an amalgam restoration
Class II composite resin restorationPosterior composite restorative technique
Class IV composite resin restorationAnterior composite restorative technique
Endodontic access cavity preparationSafe and appropriate access preparation
Crown preparationTooth preparation for a crown
Provisional crown restorationFabrication of the provisional on your own crown preparation

The examination is designed around basic skills that dentists entering practice in Canada are expected to perform competently.

This article intentionally does not reproduce NDEB's detailed preparation dimensions, scoring rubrics or proprietary examination criteria. Use the current protocol directly for the official grading boundaries.

Simulated Patient and Clinical Standard

The seven requirements are performed on a simulated patient/manikin in a clinical setting.

NDEB instructs candidates to perform the work as though they were treating actual patients and to use accepted clinical-care standards and judgement.

The current protocol also makes one exam-specific distinction: although dental-dam placement is part of normal standard of care in relevant clinical situations, candidates are not required to use a dental dam to perform the NDECC Clinical Skills requirements under the current examination protocol.

Do not generalize that exam-specific rule into normal patient care.

Can You Choose the Order?

Yes.

The current protocol says the clinical requirements can be performed in any order.

That gives you some control over time management, but the best order is not universal. A sensible plan considers:

  • which tasks require the most concentration;
  • which materials or procedures create waiting/finishing steps;
  • when you are most precise during the day;
  • the mandatory break;
  • infection-control transitions between activities.

Your strategy should remain simple enough that you do not waste examination time constantly changing plans.

Full-Day Schedule and Breaks

The Clinical Skills component is a full day.

The current protocol includes:

  • an orientation before clinical work begins;
  • a mandatory 30-minute break;
  • optional unscheduled breaks if needed.

Unscheduled breaks do not add examination time.

Candidates must remain inside the Test Centre during the examination. Leaving the Test Centre means you cannot return to complete the examination.

Your Clinical Skills day may occur on Day 1 or Day 2 of the overall NDECC schedule, so keep both days available until your confirmed examination schedule tells you which component occurs first.

Equipment, Instruments and Supplies

NDEB states that it provides the necessary clinical equipment, instruments, materials and supplies.

The current Practical Guide is the correct source for the exact test-centre setup.

NDEB also advises candidates to report equipment or material problems to an invigilator immediately.

Current equipment guidance says a time extension is given when an equipment-related problem creates a cumulative delay of five minutes or more. It also states that no time extension is given simply for tightening or repositioning teeth.

Do not attempt to solve a test-centre equipment problem quietly while losing time. Notify the invigilator when the problem occurs.

What You Can Bring

The current protocol tightly limits personal items in the Clinical Skills area.

Permitted clinical items include things such as:

  • eye protection;
  • magnification;
  • a headlight with batteries or charger;
  • approved personal items;
  • lunch, which remains in the examinee lounge.

Do not bring your own clinical instrument kit on the assumption that it will be permitted.

Watches, mobile phones, smart watches, smart glasses and other transmitting devices are prohibited under the current exam-day rules.

Always recheck the latest Practical Guide and protocol before travelling because NDEB can modify administration-specific rules.

Infection Control and Safety Can Fail the Component

Clinical Skills is only one half of NDECC; the other component is explained in NDECC Situational Judgement 2027.

Clinical technique is only part of the assessment.

Infection control and safety are evaluated throughout the Clinical Skills component.

Under the current protocol:

  • up to two infection-control and safety errors are permitted;
  • three or more errors result in a failing grade;
  • one critical infection-control or safety error also results in a failing grade.

The protocol gives examples such as failure to wear required personal protective equipment, unsafe objects left in the simulated oral cavity, or a clear lack of understanding of infection-control concepts.

The lesson is straightforward: infection control is not a minor checklist at the side of the exam. It is part of the pass standard.

How Clinical Skills Grading Works

NDEB describes three performance categories:

  • Competent
  • Minimally Competent
  • Not Competent

Competent and minimally competent performance lead to a passing result for a requirement.

A performance classified as Not Competent fails that clinical requirement.

To pass the Clinical Skills component, the current protocol requires a passing grade for all clinical requirements.

This is stricter than a system where one weak procedure can be averaged out by stronger procedures.

NDEB's consultation review specifically reaffirmed that candidates must demonstrate at least minimally competent performance across all seven basic procedures on the same examination day.

What Happens If You Fail One Clinical Requirement?

If Clinical Skills is the only unsuccessful component, the existing failed-component retake guide explains the component-only retake logic, while NDECC Results 2027 covers result timing.

If one or more of the seven requirements receives a failing grade, you fail the Clinical Skills component.

You do not retake only the individual preparation or restoration that failed. NDEB requires you to repeat the entire Clinical Skills component.

However, NDECC has two separate components.

If you passed Situational Judgement and failed Clinical Skills, current NDEB rules require you to repeat only Clinical Skills—not the entire two-component NDECC.

If you fail both components, you repeat the full NDECC.

See NDECC Failed Component Retake Strategy.

The 60-Month NDECC Window Still Matters

For calendar pressure, use NDECC Registration 2027 and the existing five-year deadline guide.

NDECC does not use the old simple three-attempt cap for this clinical examination.

The current protocol states that NDECC may be taken as needed within the 60-month period measured from the date you first pass ACJ.

NDEB has also announced that, effective in May 2027, candidates who cannot demonstrate minimal competence on both NDECC components after the applicable five-year period can cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process, subject to the published extension criteria.

That means a failed Clinical Skills component is not only a technical setback. It also consumes part of your five-year NDECC window.

Candidate Checklist Before the Clinical Skills Day

Before travel, pair this checklist with the existing Ottawa Test Centre logistics guide.

Several weeks before

  • Download the current NDECC Protocol again.
  • Download the current Practical Guide again.
  • Review the Clinical Skills orientation resources.
  • Confirm your exact Ottawa exam schedule.
  • Confirm your government-issued ID matches your NDEB profile.
  • Review the FDI two-digit tooth-numbering system used by NDEB.

Preparation phase

  • Practise the seven official categories, not recalled “secret” examination content.
  • Train infection-control habits until they are automatic.
  • Practise time management across a full simulated day.
  • Practise switching between procedures without losing organization.
  • Use the official grading criteria to understand the difference between minimally competent and not competent performance.

Day before

  • Confirm permitted personal items.
  • Charge any permitted headlight equipment.
  • Prepare your lunch according to test-centre rules.
  • Avoid bringing prohibited devices or an unnecessary instrument kit.
  • Review transportation to the Ottawa test centre.

During the exam

  • Treat the manikin as an actual patient.
  • Follow infection-control rules throughout the day.
  • Report equipment problems immediately.
  • Do not leave the Test Centre.
  • Stop working when instructed.

For logistics, see NDECC Ottawa Test Centre Logistics.

Common Mistakes

  • Studying from an old ACS checklist instead of the current NDECC protocol.
  • Assuming one failed procedure can be averaged out.
  • Ignoring infection control because the dentistry itself looks more important.
  • Memorizing third-party “exact exam dimensions” instead of the current NDEB criteria.
  • Bringing unauthorized personal equipment.
  • Failing to report an equipment malfunction immediately.
  • Practising only individual tasks and never completing a timed full-day simulation.
  • Forgetting that the current protocol, not an old preparation course, controls the exam.

FAQs

1. How many procedures are in NDECC Clinical Skills?

Seven clinical requirements plus infection-control and safety assessment.

2. Do I have to perform the procedures in a fixed order?

No. The current protocol says the requirements can be performed in any order.

3. Does NDEB provide the instruments?

Yes. NDEB states that all necessary equipment, instruments and supplies are provided. Use the current Practical Guide for the exact setup.

4. Can I fail one procedure and still pass Clinical Skills?

No. You need a passing grade for all clinical requirements.

5. If I fail Clinical Skills but pass Situational Judgement, do I repeat both?

No. Current NDEB rules require you to repeat only the failed component.
