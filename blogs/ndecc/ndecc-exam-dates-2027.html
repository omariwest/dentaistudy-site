NDECC Exam Dates 2027: Calendar, Ottawa Sessions & Registration

On 17 November 2026, NDEB had confirmed two NDECC evaluation periods for 2027 , both in Ottawa: 4 January – 11 March 2027 (registration already closed) and 30 March – 3 June 2027 (registration opens 19 January 2027). NDEB had published no later 2027 periods.

Quick Answers

  1. Confirmed periods: 4 Jan – 11 Mar 2027 (registration closed 20 Oct 2026) and 30 Mar – 3 Jun 2027 (registration opens 19 Jan 2027 at 10:00 ET and closes 1 Feb 2027 at 21:00 ET).
  2. Nothing later is published. Do not plan around a later 2027 session until NDEB lists it.
  3. An evaluation period is not your exam date. You select a preferred date at registration. NDEB says it is tentative and is confirmed when registration closes.
  4. You register after passing the ACJ. NDEB opens NDECC registration in three stages, starting with candidates who passed the most recent ACJ.
  5. Five-year rule: effective May 2027, candidates who cannot demonstrate minimal competence on both components after five years cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process and cannot reapply to it.
  6. Withdrawing costs money. The fee is 50% before the registration deadline, 100% after it or for a no-show, and 25% for approved serious medical or unforeseen exceptional circumstances.

Ottawa location and what "evaluation period" means

For what the two-day assessment actually contains, see the current NDECC Protocol 2027 guide, then review the dedicated Clinical Skills and Situational Judgement pages.

NDEB states that the NDECC is administered at the NDEB Office in Ottawa throughout the year. NDEB's published office address is 340 Albert Street, 12th Floor, Ottawa, Ontario, Canada K1R 7Y6. Facility details, instruments and materials are in the NDECC Practical Guide (effective 30 July 2026), linked from NDEB's Exam Resources page.

Evaluation period vs your individual appointment

The date range on NDEB's dates page is the evaluation period: the window during which NDECC sittings are delivered. Your own two-day attendance falls inside it. In this article we call that your appointment. NDEB does not use that word on the pages reviewed.

The NDECC page describes the process this way:

  • At registration you see the available sessions in the exam window and select a preferred date.
  • That date is tentative, and you must be flexible.
  • The exact date is confirmed when registration closes, and you receive a confirmation email.

So a candidate "in the 30 March – 3 June period" does not yet know their exam days when they register. NDEB's NDECC calendar shows sessions and lets you click a date to see its registration window and evaluation period. The calendar content did not load for our automated check, so use it in your browser.

NDECC format: the two components

The NDECC is the final step in the Equivalency Process. It has two components. For this article we abbreviate them as CSC (clinical skills component) and SJC (situational judgement component). NDEB's own pages spell them out.

  • CSC: assesses clinical skills and techniques relevant to current Canadian standards.
  • SJC: assesses the judgement needed to solve problems in work-related situations.

The NDECC Protocol (effective 15 June 2026) describes the NDECC as a two-day examination. It says the SJC is half a day and may be scheduled in the morning or the afternoon. The Protocol also holds the format, grading criteria and blueprint, so read it before registering. Our separate guide covers CSC procedures.

Results are reported by component. NDEB's FAQ says that if you fail both components you must repeat the full NDECC. A 2022 NDEB by-law update says a candidate who fails only one component retakes only that component. That update is older, so check the current by-laws before planning around a partial retake.

Eligibility after the ACJ and registration stages

If you have not reached NDECC yet, the ACJ 2027 dates and NDEB Equivalency Timeline 2027 show how the earlier stage feeds into these Ottawa periods.

You reach the NDECC through the Equivalency Process. NDEB states that once you pass the ACJ you are offered registration for your first NDECC attempt. If you do not accept, you become eligible to register when registration opens.

NDEB's Examination and Appeal Policies and Procedures (effective 15 July 2026) set out three registration stages:

StageWho can register
Stage 1Candidates who have passed the most recent ACJ
Stage 2Candidates with approved proof of Canadian citizenship or permanent residency
Stage 3All candidates with NDECC eligibility

An eligible candidate who does not register in Stage 1 may register in Stage 2 (if a citizen or permanent resident) or in Stage 3 for a subsequent NDECC attempt. A candidate with an exceptional medical circumstance when Stage 1 opens may ask the Executive Committee to postpone their registration priority, with health care documentation.

NDEB's pages do not publish the exact opening time for each stage beyond the 9:00 and 10:00 a.m. times above. They also do not say when ACJ results become available relative to an NDECC registration date. Check your account and the ACJ timing in our ACJ exam dates 2027 article.

Seat availability and the registration queue

NDEB's 13 August 2026 announcement introduced a second registration period for the AFK, ACJ and Virtual OSCE. It does not mention the NDECC, so do not assume the same model applies to the NDECC.

NDEB's policy does describe how NDECC seats are obtained: candidates enter a registration queue and are then moved to seat selection. This matters for extensions. When a candidate says they were unable to register three or more times, NDEB staff review the registration logs. They check that the candidate entered the queue in each registration window and was not moved to seat selection.

Other official details:

  • If a health-related issue or personal circumstance before the exam will prevent you from demonstrating your ability, NDEB says you must withdraw. If you do not attend, you do not lose an attempt.
  • For a reduced-fee withdrawal for exceptional circumstances, the policy requires you to withdraw in NDEBConnect before the exam starts. You must then email a refund request to NDEB within seven days, with supporting or health care documentation.

The five-year rule and the May 2027 change

What NDEB has published

  • NDECC page: candidates have five years to pass both components. Effective in May 2027, candidates who cannot demonstrate minimal competence on both components after five years "cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process."
  • NDEB notice, 27 January 2026: NDEB confirmed that, effective May 2027, such candidates cease to be candidates and "will not be able to reapply to the EP." In the same notice NDEB reported that, after consultation, it made no changes to examination validity and added no new application criteria.
  • Policies and Procedures (effective 15 July 2026): the five years are counted as 60 months from the date a candidate first passes the ACJ.

The consequence

The published consequence is removal from the Equivalency Process without the ability to reapply to it. This article does not describe any way to restart the process, because NDEB's guidance does not offer one.

What the pages reviewed do not say

  • The exact day in May 2027 on which the rule takes effect.
  • How candidates whose 60 months end before or around that time are treated.

If your ACJ pass is more than about four years old, ask NDEB in writing how the rule applies to you.

Extension requests

NDEB's policy lets a candidate apply to the Executive Committee to extend the 60-month timeframe. The candidate must show one or both of the following during those 60 months:

  • an exceptional medical circumstance that impeded their ability to register or participate in the NDECC three times; and/or
  • inability to register for the NDECC three or more times.

The decision is final, and an extension is not automatic. The request form is on NDEB's NDECC page.

Candidate planning scenarios

These are illustrations of how the published rules interact, not NDEB guidance.

Scenario 1: you passed the ACJ and want the March–June period. Be logged in at the opening time on 19 January 2027, converted to your time zone. If you qualify for the 9:00 a.m. citizenship or permanent residency slot, note that its time zone is not stated on the NDECC page. Expect your confirmed date only after the 1 February deadline. Do not buy flights before NDEB's confirmation email.

Scenario 2: you passed the ACJ in late 2026. Your 60-month period starts from the date you first pass the ACJ, so it runs to about late 2031 (our arithmetic; confirm the exact date with NDEB). That is a long runway, but it covers multiple registration windows, and any window can fill.

Scenario 3: your ACJ pass dates from mid-2022. Your five years end around mid-2027, close to the May 2027 enforcement. Contact NDEB now and keep records of every registration attempt. The January–March period is already closed, so the March–June period is the only confirmed period still open for registration.

Scenario 4: you could not get a seat. Enter the registration queue in every window and keep screenshots or logs. NDEB reviews registration logs for extension requests. Three failed attempts to register is one of the two criteria.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the evaluation period as your exam date. Your date is tentative until registration closes.
  • Planning around unpublished sessions. Only the two periods above are confirmed.
  • Misreading ET. The 1 February 2027 deadline falls on 2 February in much of Asia, Australia and New Zealand.
  • Assuming the AFK/ACJ/OSCE second-registration model covers the NDECC. NDEB's announcement does not name it.
  • Assuming removal under the five-year rule can be reversed by reapplying. NDEB states it cannot.
  • Withdrawing late. After the registration deadline the fee is 100%.
  • Neglecting your profile. NDEB asks you to keep your examination language and left/right handedness up to date. Language change requests go by email to NDEB before the exam. Accounts with no examination activity for 24 months are set to inactive, and reactivation requires a fee.
  • Leaving accommodations late. For needs known when you apply, submit the request immediately after registering for your first exam. For a new or changing need, submit it no later than eight weeks before the exam date.

FAQs

1. What are the NDECC exam dates in 2027? NDEB lists two evaluation periods: 4 January – 11 March 2027 and 30 March – 3 June 2027, both in Canada, at the NDEB Office in Ottawa. No later 2027 period was published on 17 November 2026.

2. Can I still register for the January–March 2027 period? No. Its registration deadline was 20 October 2026 at 21:00 ET, and NDEB shows it as closed. The March–June period opens for registration on 19 January 2027 at 10:00 ET and closes on 1 February 2027 at 21:00 ET.

3. Is the evaluation period my exam date? No. It is the window in which sessions are delivered. You select a preferred date, which NDEB calls tentative, and NDEB confirms the exact date when registration closes.

4. What happens if I do not pass both components within five years? Effective May 2027, NDEB states that such candidates cease to be candidates in the Equivalency Process and cannot reapply to it. The five years run from first passing the ACJ. An extension request is possible only on the narrow grounds above, and the Executive Committee's decision is final.

5. What does withdrawing cost? It is 50% of the fee before the registration deadline and 100% after it or for failure to appear. For approved serious medical or unanticipated exceptional circumstances it is 25%. Fees are in Canadian dollars and are subject to change.
