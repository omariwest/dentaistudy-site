NDECC Registration 2027: Eligibility, Ottawa Booking & Documents

NDECC registration is different from AFK and ACJ registration. The National Dental Examining Board of Canada uses a three-stage NDECC registration system , the exam is delivered at the NDEB test centre in Ottawa, and the date you initially select is only a preferred tentative date until registration closes.

Quick Answers

  1. Who can register? NDECC candidates have passed the ACJ and hold NDECC eligibility.
  2. How does priority work? Registration opens in three stages: most recent ACJ passers, candidates with approved Canadian citizenship/permanent-residency proof, then all eligible candidates.
  3. Where is NDECC held? At the NDEB Office/Test Centre in Ottawa.
  4. Current 2027 open window: 30 March–3 June 2027; published registration opens 19 January 2027 at 10:00 ET and closes 1 February 2027 at 21:00 ET.
  5. Fee: currently CAD 6,500 for both components, or CAD 3,250 for one component where only a component is being retaken.
  6. Five-year rule: candidates have 60 months from first passing the ACJ to pass the NDECC; from May 2027, candidates who exceed the limit cease to be Equivalency Process candidates unless an applicable extension is granted.

NDECC registration is different from AFK and ACJ registration. The National Dental Examining Board of Canada uses a three-stage NDECC registration system, the exam is delivered at the NDEB test centre in Ottawa, and the date you initially select is only a preferred tentative date until registration closes.

For 2027, the currently published NDECC periods are 4 January–11 March 2027, which is already closed, and 30 March–3 June 2027, whose published registration opening is 19 January 2027 at 10:00 ET with a 1 February 2027 at 21:00 ET deadline.

This guide focuses on eligibility, registration priority, account information, fees, accommodations and what to document if you repeatedly cannot secure a seat.

Before attending, review the NDECC Protocol 2027 guide and the existing Ottawa Test Centre logistics guide.

Eligibility after the ACJ

If you have just completed the previous stage, ACJ Results 2027 explains the pass/retake decision and when the 60-month NDECC clock begins.

The NDECC is the final examination stage of the NDEB Equivalency Process.

Once you pass the Assessment of Clinical Judgement (ACJ), the NDEB says you will be offered registration for your first NDECC attempt. If you do not accept that offer, you remain eligible to register when a later NDECC registration opens, subject to the stage that applies to you and available seats.

You do not start a new Equivalency Process application simply because you are moving from ACJ to NDECC. Your NDEBConnect profile and candidate status carry forward.

The three NDECC registration stages

The existing NDECC tiered registration guide provides background on the priority system, while this page applies the current 2027 calendar.

The current NDEB policies define three stages.

StageCandidates
Stage 1Candidates who passed the most recent ACJ
Stage 2Candidates with approved proof of Canadian citizenship or permanent residency
Stage 3All candidates with NDECC eligibility

Stage 1: most recent ACJ passers

This gives newly eligible candidates an initial registration opportunity.

If an exceptional medical circumstance affects you at the time you would receive Stage 1 priority, NDEB policy allows a request to the Executive Committee to postpone that registration priority, subject to the required documentation and decision.

Stage 2: approved citizenship or permanent-residency proof

Canadian citizenship or permanent residency is not required to participate in the Equivalency Process or take NDEB exams.

However, approved proof can affect NDECC registration priority. Existing candidates can submit proof through a service request in NDEBConnect.

If you previously supplied acceptable evidence, the NDEB says you can request that it be considered rather than assuming you must submit an entirely new credential package.

Stage 3: all remaining eligible candidates

Stage 3 opens NDECC registration to all candidates who hold NDECC eligibility, subject to available capacity.

This is why an international candidate without Canadian status can still register and sit the NDECC.

Registration time: 9:00 vs 10:00

The NDECC page says candidates should log in to their NDEB online profile at 10:00 a.m. on the registration date to register and pay.

It also says candidates with approved proof of Canadian citizenship or permanent residency can register at 9:00 a.m. on the scheduled registration date.

The public 2027 calendar lists the 30 March–3 June period as opening on 19 January 2027 at 10:00 ET. Candidates who qualify for the earlier residency/citizenship stage should follow their NDEBConnect instructions and current NDEB notice for the 9:00 access.

Step-by-step NDECC registration

1. Confirm your candidate status before opening day

Log into NDEBConnect before the registration date and verify that your ACJ pass and NDECC eligibility are correctly reflected.

Do not wait until the opening minute to discover that your account is inactive or that a profile issue needs staff review.

NDEB policy says accounts with 24 months of no examination activity can be set to inactive and may require reactivation and a fee.

2. Check the information that affects your exam

The NDEB specifically tells NDECC candidates to keep these profile items current:

  • examination language;
  • left- or right-handedness.

Language needs special attention. NDEB guidance says an NDECC language-change request must be submitted before registration opens. Once NDECC registration has started, a language-change request cannot be considered.

3. Confirm whether Canadian-status proof is approved

If you want to rely on Stage 2 priority, do not assume uploading a document at the last moment means it is approved.

Check NDEBConnect and use the official service-request process ahead of the registration date.

4. Register and pay

The current NDECC fee is:

  • CAD 6,500 for the full NDECC;
  • CAD 3,250 for the Clinical Skills Component alone;
  • CAD 3,250 for the Situational Judgement Component alone.

The single-component fee is relevant to eligible retake situations. If you fail only one component, current NDEB guidance says you retake the failed component rather than automatically repeating both.

5. Select a preferred exam date

Available sessions inside the evaluation period are shown during registration.

Choose your preferred date, but keep travel plans flexible. The NDEB states that the date is tentative until registration closes.

6. Wait for exact confirmation

After the registration window closes, the NDEB confirms the exact date and sends a confirmation email.

Do not buy a non-refundable flight based only on your tentative selection.

What documents and profile items should be ready?

At the NDECC stage, most candidates are not re-submitting the original degree-verification package. Instead, the practical registration checklist is about your current candidate record.

Have these issues settled before the window opens:

  • active NDEBConnect access;
  • recorded ACJ pass and NDECC eligibility;
  • current language preference;
  • correct left/right handedness;
  • approved Canadian citizenship/permanent-residency proof if you want the applicable priority stage;
  • current accommodation documentation if required;
  • a payment method able to cover the applicable exam fee;
  • current contact details so you receive confirmation emails.

If your legal name or identity details have changed, follow NDEB's formal process rather than trying to solve it during seat selection.

Test accommodations

Candidates can request accommodations for a documented disability, medical diagnosis or religious reason.

The NDEB requires the request form and supporting documentation through NDEBConnect.

For needs known when entering the process, the request should be submitted promptly following registration for the first examination. For a new or changing need, current policy says submit as soon as possible and no later than eight weeks before the affected examination date.

A new accommodation request is required for each registration.

Some accommodations that may exist for written examinations are not available for the NDECC Clinical Skills Component because they would change the nature of the assessment or cannot be delivered safely in that setting.

Do not wait until the week of the exam to raise a known accommodation need.

Fees and withdrawal

Current NDEB fees list:

ItemCurrent fee
Full NDECCCAD 6,500
Clinical Skills ComponentCAD 3,250
Situational Judgement ComponentCAD 3,250

All fees are subject to change.

The NDEB withdrawal page currently states:

  • withdrawal before the registration deadline: 50% withdrawal fee;
  • withdrawal after the registration deadline or failure to appear: 100% withdrawal fee;
  • approved serious medical or unanticipated exceptional circumstance: 25% withdrawal fee under the applicable policy.

If an exceptional circumstance occurs, follow the NDEBConnect withdrawal and documentation deadlines exactly.

What if you cannot secure an NDECC seat?

This matters because repeated inability to register can become relevant to an extension request.

Current NDEB policy says a candidate may request an extension of the 60-month NDECC period if, during those 60 months, they experienced:

  • an exceptional medical circumstance that impeded registration or participation three times; and/or
  • inability to register for the NDECC three or more times.

For an inability-to-register request, the NDEB says staff review the registration logs to verify that the candidate entered the registration queue during each window and was not moved to seat selection.

That means your behaviour during registration matters. If your five-year clock is becoming important, enter each legitimate registration window and keep your own records, but understand that the NDEB will rely on its system logs.

The five-year rule

Candidates near the limit should also read the existing NDECC five-year rule because registration failures can matter when assessing a later extension request.

NDEB policies measure the NDECC timeframe as 60 months from the date you first pass the ACJ.

Effective in May 2027, a candidate who has not demonstrated minimal competence on both NDECC components within that timeframe will cease to be an Equivalency Process candidate.

The NDEB has also stated that candidates removed under this rule cannot simply reapply to the Equivalency Process.

An extension exists only under the limited policy grounds described above and is decided by the Executive Committee. It is not automatic.

See NDECC Five-Year Rule: May 2027 Deadline for a dedicated explanation.

Candidate planning examples

Scenario 1: you just passed the most recent ACJ

You may receive Stage 1 priority. Check your NDEBConnect profile and registration communication. If you intend to sit the March–June 2027 evaluation period, prepare before 19 January rather than trying to fix account details at opening time.

Scenario 2: you are a permanent resident but did not pass the most recent ACJ

If your proof is already approved, Stage 2 may give you earlier access than the general eligible group. Confirm that the status is actually approved in your account.

Scenario 3: international candidate without Canadian status

You can still participate. Canadian citizenship or permanent residency is not required for NDECC eligibility. You register during the stage available to all eligible candidates, subject to capacity.

Scenario 4: you repeatedly enter the queue but cannot reach seat selection

Keep trying during legitimate registration windows. If you later approach the 60-month limit, the NDEB can review its logs when considering an extension request based on inability to register three or more times.

Common registration mistakes

  • Assuming citizenship is required. It is not; it affects one priority stage.
  • Treating the preferred date as final. NDEB confirms the exact date only after registration closes.
  • Changing NDECC language too late. Finalise the language preference before registration opens.
  • Ignoring handedness in the profile. NDEB specifically asks candidates to keep it current.
  • Assuming AFK/ACJ two-window rules apply to NDECC. NDECC has its own three-stage process.
  • Waiting too long on accommodations. New or changing needs generally need to be submitted no later than eight weeks before the exam.
  • Failing to enter registration windows while close to the five-year limit. Registration logs matter for extension requests based on inability to secure a seat.
  • Believing you can simply reapply after the May 2027 five-year rule removes your candidacy. Current NDEB guidance says you cannot.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. When does NDECC registration open for the March–June 2027 period?

The public NDEB calendar lists 19 January 2027 at 10:00 ET, with a 1 February 2027 at 21:00 ET deadline. Candidates with approved Canadian citizenship/permanent-residency proof should follow the NDECC instructions for the applicable earlier stage.

2. Do I need Canadian citizenship or permanent residency?

No. It is not required to apply to the Equivalency Process or sit its exams. Approved proof can give you Stage 2 NDECC registration priority.

3. Is the date I select during registration final?

No. The NDEB describes it as tentative. The exact date is confirmed after registration closes.

4. How much is NDECC in 2027?

The currently published fee is CAD 6,500 for both components or CAD 3,250 for an individual component where applicable. Fees are subject to change.

5. What if I repeatedly cannot register?

Current policy allows a request to extend Equivalency Candidate Status in specific circumstances, including inability to register for NDECC three or more times during the 60-month period. The NDEB reviews its registration logs.
