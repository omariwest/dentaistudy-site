NDEB 2027 Exam Calendar: Compare AFK, ACJ, NDECC & OSCE

NDEB publishes dates exam by exam, and the dates for different exams sit on different pages. This guide puts everything NDEB had published for 2027 into one calendar, shows how the exams connect, and sends you to the detailed article for each stage.

Quick Answers

AFK 2027:

5 Feb (Canada), 6 Feb (Australia and New Zealand), 13 Aug (Canada). Official.

ACJ 2027:

4 May (United States and Canada), 5 May (Australia and New Zealand), 2 Nov (Canada). Official.

NDECC 2027:

two evaluation periods at the NDEB Office in Ottawa: 4 Jan – 11 Mar (registration closed) and 30 Mar – 3 Jun. Official.

Virtual OSCE 2027:

1 Mar (United States and Canada; Australia and Ireland), 20 May (United States and Canada) and 22 Nov (Canada). Official.

The order is AFK → ACJ → NDECC, then the Virtual OSCE.

Each Equivalency Process exam opens the next, as set out below.

Key points:

  • Australia and New Zealand have one AFK and one ACJ date. No August AFK or November ACJ was listed for them. Not listed does not mean not offered, but do not plan around an unpublished sitting.
  • Delivery formats (official): NDEB says the AFK is paper-based across Canada and electronic at Prometric centres in Canada and select international locations. It says the ACJ is administered at Prometric centres within Canada and select international locations. The NDECC is administered at the NDEB Office in Ottawa throughout the year. The Virtual OSCE is administered at locations across Canada and some in the USA, Ireland and Australia. NDEB's pages do not map a format to each individual sitting, so check your registration.
  • New Zealand licensure: NDEB's Equivalency Process page says participants who complete the Equivalency Process can apply for licensure as a dentist in New Zealand under an agreement with the New Zealand Dental Council.

How the exams connect

NDEB's pages describe two stages. The Equivalency Process contains the AFK, ACJ and NDECC. The Certification Process contains the Virtual OSCE, followed by NDEB Certification and provincial licensure. NDEB states that completing the Equivalency Process allows you to move on to the Certification Process.

How results affect the next exam:

  • NDEB says that once you pass the AFK you are eligible to register for the ACJ, and that a passing AFK grade is required before continuing to the other Equivalency Process exams.
  • Once you pass the ACJ, NDEB says you are offered registration for your first NDECC attempt.
  • The five-year rule: candidates have five years to pass both NDECC components. Effective May 2027, candidates who cannot demonstrate minimal competence on both components after five years cease to be Equivalency Process candidates and "will not be able to reapply to the EP." NDEB's policy counts the five years from first passing the ACJ.
  • Virtual OSCE eligibility: NDEB's registration page says you need an approved application. It does not say which application applies to someone completing the Equivalency Process, so confirm that in NDEBConnect. That page was last modified in August 2025. NDEB's policies also set a 60-month limit for passing the Virtual OSCE, measured from graduation or from completing the Equivalency Process.

A passing grade on one exam does not secure a seat at the next. You still have to register while a window is open and seats remain.

Registration rules since 1 September 2026

NDEB's 13 August 2026 announcement changed registration for the AFK, ACJ and Virtual OSCE. It took effect on 1 September 2026.

  • Two registration periods. The first opens at a set date and time and closes when all locations are full. The second opens about 6–8 weeks before the exam, so candidates can register or transfer if seats become available through withdrawals or increased capacity.
  • Transfers between exam locations are allowed only when registration is open. You cannot transfer between registration periods.
  • Withdrawal fees: 50% of the exam fee before the registration deadline and 100% after it. The reduced fee for approved medical or other unforeseen exceptional circumstances is unchanged. NDEB's withdrawal page lists 25% for those circumstances and includes the NDECC.

The announcement does not name the NDECC. NDECC registration works differently, with a three-stage opening and a queue (see our NDECC dates article).

When later dates have not been announced

NDEB had published nothing after the dates above on 17 November 2026. That includes any NDECC period after 3 June 2027 and any 2028 sitting.

Advice:

  • Plan in two layers. Build a firm plan around published dates and a provisional one for the gap. Do not buy non-refundable travel or leave until NDEB's confirmation.
  • Re-check the dates page before every registration opening. NDEB lists opening dates there ahead of time, and the page changed as recently as 13 November 2026.
  • Keep NDEBConnect active. NDEB sets accounts to inactive after 24 months with no exam activity.
  • Protect the NDECC clock. If the ACJ pass is the start of your five years, an unpublished NDECC period is a risk. Enter the registration queue in every NDECC window and keep records.
  • Ask NDEB in writing when your situation depends on an unpublished date, such as an NDECC period after June 2027.

Candidate planning scenarios

These timelines combine official dates with our calculations. They are illustrations, not NDEB guidance.

Scenario 1: AFK in February, ACJ in May. You sit the AFK on 5 February 2027. The ACJ registration deadline is 5 April, 59 days later (calculation). NDEB's pages reviewed do not give an AFK result-release date, so confirm it before assuming May is possible. If your result does not arrive in time, the next published ACJ is 2 November 2027 (Canada).

Scenario 2: passed the ACJ in November 2026. NDECC registration opens in stages, starting with candidates who passed the most recent ACJ. The 30 March – 3 June 2027 period opens for registration on 19 January 2027, so that is the only NDECC period currently open to you. Whether your ACJ result arrives before then is unconfirmed.

Scenario 3: passing the ACJ in May 2027. The published NDECC periods end on 3 June 2027, and registration for the 30 March period closes on 1 February, before the May ACJ. Your NDECC period was unpublished on 17 November 2026. Your five years run to about May 2032 (calculation; NDEB sets the exact date).

Scenario 4: Australia/New Zealand route. AFK on 6 February and ACJ on 5 May are both listed for your region. The NDECC is in Ottawa, so factor travel into the NDECC stage. The Virtual OSCE listings for Australia and Ireland currently show only 1 March 2027, while New Zealand has none, and other 2027 listings are for the United States and Canada or Canada alone.

Scenario 5: completed the Equivalency Process. The published Virtual OSCE dates are 1 March (United States, Canada, Australia and Ireland), 20 May (United States and Canada) and 22 November 2027 (Canada). The 1 March registration deadline is 18 January 2027, and NDEB's registration page says results are released within 8 weeks of the exam.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the NDECC evaluation period as your exam date. Your date is confirmed after registration closes.
  • Assuming other regions share Canada's dates. The AFK and ACJ run one day apart in Australia and New Zealand, and Ireland appears only for the Virtual OSCE.
  • Planning around unpublished sessions, including a second AFK or ACJ in a region that has only one listed.
  • Reading deadlines in local time. They are in ET, and the date often changes after conversion.
  • Assuming a passing result guarantees the next seat. Registration windows still apply.
  • Applying the second-registration rules to the NDECC. NDEB's announcement names only the AFK, ACJ and Virtual OSCE.
  • Assuming the five-year NDECC rule can be undone by reapplying. NDEB states that candidates removed under it cannot reapply.
  • Using older pages as current. NDEB's Virtual OSCE registration page was last modified in August 2025, before the registration changes.

FAQs

1. What are the NDEB exam dates in 2027? NDEB lists the AFK on 5 February, 6 February and 13 August; the ACJ on 4 May, 5 May and 2 November; two NDECC periods (4 January – 11 March and 30 March – 3 June); and the Virtual OSCE on 1 March (two listings), 20 May and 22 November. Details and deadlines are in the master calendar above.

2. In what order do I take the exams? AFK, then ACJ, then NDECC to complete the Equivalency Process, then the Virtual OSCE in the Certification Process. NDEB says that passing the AFK makes you eligible to register for the ACJ, and passing the ACJ gets you offered NDECC registration.

3. Are the dates the same in Australia, New Zealand and Ireland? No. Australia and New Zealand sit the AFK on 6 February and the ACJ on 5 May. Australia and Ireland have a Virtual OSCE listing on 1 March 2027. The NDECC is held only in Ottawa.

4. What if the date I need has not been announced? Do not assume one. NDEB had published nothing beyond the dates above. Check NDEB's dates page regularly, keep your account active and ask NDEB in writing if your timeline depends on an unpublished date.

5. What changed in exam registration? From 1 September 2026, the AFK, ACJ and Virtual OSCE have a second registration period about 6–8 weeks before the exam. Transfers are allowed only while registration is open, and withdrawal fees are 50% before the deadline and 100% after it.
