INBDE Exam Dates 2027: How Prometric Scheduling Actually Works

There is no single national list of INBDE exam dates for 2027. The Integrated National Board Dental Examination is not administered like an exam with two or three fixed annual sittings. Once the Joint Commission on National Dental Examinations (JCNDE) approves your application and confirms your eligibility, you schedule your own two-day examination appointment through Prometric, subject to test-centre availability and your eligibility window.

That distinction matters. Searching for a “March 2027 INBDE date” or “September 2027 INBDE sitting” can lead candidates toward unofficial calendars that do not exist. The official planning method is to complete eligibility, receive the JCNDE eligibility email, then select available Prometric dates that fit your authorised testing period.

As of 17 November 2026, JCNDE's current official candidate guide is the 2026 INBDE Candidate Guide, updated 4 August 2026. A separate 2027 candidate guide was not yet published on the JCNDE site. Candidates testing in 2027 should therefore use the current guide for planning and recheck the official INBDE page before applying or testing for any later update.

Quick Answers

  1. Are there fixed INBDE exam dates in 2027? No. Eligible candidates choose available dates through Prometric.
  2. When can you schedule? After JCNDE processes your application and sends an eligibility email.
  3. How long is the normal eligibility window? The current candidate guide states that, after processing, candidates are generally eligible to test for six months, unless another rule shortens the period.
  4. How early should you book? JCNDE currently recommends scheduling 60–90 days before your desired test date.
  5. Does the INBDE have to be taken on consecutive days? No. The two administration days must be completed within seven days of one another and at the same test centre.
  6. Can you extend the eligibility window? The current guide permits one 45-day extension per application, subject to the extension rules and fee.

There Is No Annual INBDE Calendar

The INBDE is delivered through Prometric on available testing dates rather than through a small number of nationwide sitting dates.

The practical sequence is:

DENTPIN → INBDE application → eligibility confirmation → Prometric scheduling → Day 1 + Day 2

This means your personal INBDE “exam date” depends on:

  • when you become eligible;
  • the dates available at your chosen Prometric centre;
  • your six-month eligibility period;
  • whether both administration days can be booked at the same centre within the required seven-day span.

For candidates planning several months ahead, it is better to think in terms of a target testing month rather than a national sitting date.

Step 1: Get Your DENTPIN

A DENTPIN is your personal identifier for JCNDE and other dental education/testing systems.

If you already have a DENTPIN from another dental application or examination, use the existing number rather than creating another one.

Your identity information must be accurate. The JCNDE warns that the name on your examination application must match the identification you bring to Prometric.

Step 2: Submit the INBDE Application

Apply through the official JCNDE INBDE system.

Candidates from dental schools that are not accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation may need a Certification of Eligibility completed by the appropriate school official.

The examination fee is charged when you submit the application and is non-refundable and non-transferable.

Do not apply simply because you are curious about Prometric dates. Once the application is processed, the eligibility clock begins.

Step 3: Wait for the Eligibility Email

After the JCNDE reviews your application and confirms eligibility, it sends an eligibility email.

Only then should you schedule through Prometric.

The current candidate guide states that the normal eligibility period is six months after DTS processes the application, unless retest rules or another eligibility condition creates a shorter window.

If you do not test during the authorised period, the application fee is forfeited and you need a new application and payment unless you successfully use the permitted extension.

Step 4: Schedule Through Prometric

JCNDE currently recommends scheduling your appointment 60 to 90 days before your desired testing date.

Waiting until the final weeks can create problems:

  • your nearest centre may be full;
  • the available Day 1 and Day 2 combination may not fit;
  • travel may become more expensive;
  • you may run close to the end of your eligibility window.

The INBDE is administered at authorised Prometric centres in the United States, U.S. territories and Canada. JCNDE's current INBDE page also notes that the examination is no longer offered in Quebec.

Always use the live Prometric availability shown after your eligibility is active.

Two Test Days: The Rule Candidates Miss

The INBDE administration lasts two days.

The current JCNDE rule does not require consecutive days. Instead:

  • Day 2 must occur within seven days of Day 1;
  • both days must be at the same Prometric test centre.

If you fail to complete both days within that rule, the current guide states that you may be required to retest at your own expense.

Rescheduling a 2027 Appointment

JCNDE's current public INBDE page lists the following Prometric rescheduling/cancellation fees:

Notice before testCurrent fee
30 or more business days$50
5–29 business days$70
1–4 business days$150
No-show or arrival 30+ minutes late and denied admissionFull testing fee forfeited

Saturdays and Sundays are not counted as business days for this schedule.

A rescheduled date still has to fit within your authorised eligibility period.

You cannot solve an expired eligibility window simply by paying a rescheduling fee.

Eligibility Extension

The current candidate guide allows a 45-day extension, once per application.

Important rules include:

  • the extension is available only once for that application;
  • you cannot have a currently scheduled appointment when requesting it;
  • if you do have an appointment, you must cancel it first;
  • the extension fee is non-refundable and non-transferable.

The current guide lists the eligibility-extension fee as $150.

See INBDE Eligibility Window & Extension Rules for a dedicated explanation.

Candidate Planning Examples

Example 1: Targeting March 2027

There is no official “March sitting.” If your eligibility is active and Prometric shows a suitable March combination, you can schedule it.

A sensible approach is to have eligibility completed and begin checking Prometric by December 2026 or January 2027 rather than assuming a national March date will be announced.

Example 2: Targeting Summer 2027

If you want to test in July, work backward from that target. JCNDE's 60–90 day recommendation means you should ideally be ready to schedule during roughly April or May, assuming your eligibility window already covers July.

Example 3: Your preferred centre has only scattered availability

You need both days at the same centre and Day 2 within seven days. Do not book a Day 1 appointment that leaves no valid Day 2 combination.

Example 4: Your six-month window is nearly finished

First check whether a valid appointment still fits. If not, review the one-time 45-day extension rules before allowing the eligibility period to expire.

Common Mistakes

  • Waiting for a national 2027 INBDE calendar that does not exist.
  • Applying too early and wasting part of the six-month eligibility window.
  • Applying too late and expecting a high-demand Prometric centre to have immediate space.
  • Booking the two days at different centres.
  • Assuming the days must be consecutive.
  • Rescheduling outside the eligibility window.
  • Treating the 45-day extension as automatic.

FAQs

1. When is the INBDE offered in 2027?

The INBDE is scheduled through Prometric rather than through fixed nationwide annual sittings. Your available dates depend on eligibility and test-centre capacity.

2. How early should I book?

JCNDE currently recommends scheduling 60–90 days before your desired test date.

3. How long do I have to take the exam after applying?

The current candidate guide says candidates are generally eligible for six months after the application is processed, unless another rule shortens the period.

4. Must Day 1 and Day 2 be consecutive?

No. They must be completed within seven days and at the same test centre.

5. Has JCNDE published a 2027 Candidate Guide?

Not as of 17 November 2026. The current official guide is the 2026 INBDE Candidate Guide updated 4 August 2026. Recheck before your 2027 test.
