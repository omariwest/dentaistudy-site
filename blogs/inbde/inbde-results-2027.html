INBDE Results 2027: Score Reports, Timing and What Happens Next

JCNDE currently says INBDE results are typically available approximately three to four weeks after the examination date. You view them online by signing in with your DENTPIN and password.

The INBDE is officially reported as Pass or Fail. Candidates who pass do not receive a conventional numerical score report showing how far above the standard they performed. Candidates who do not pass receive additional performance information to support remediation, including the overall scale score and performance information for major content areas.

As of 17 November 2026, the current official guide is still the 2026 INBDE Candidate Guide, updated 4 August 2026. The policies below should therefore be rechecked if JCNDE publishes a newer guide before your 2027 examination.

Quick Answers

  1. When are INBDE results released? Usually about 3–4 weeks after the examination.
  2. Where do you check? Sign in through your DENTPIN account.
  3. Is the INBDE reported numerically? Official results are Pass/Fail. Unsuccessful candidates receive an overall scale score and additional performance information.
  4. What is the minimum passing scale score? The current guide explains that 75 represents the minimum passing score on the underlying scale.
  5. Can you request an audit? Yes, within 30 days of the official results-report date.
  6. How soon can you retake after failing? The current policy requires a minimum 60-day wait between unsuccessful attempts.

How Result Timing Works

JCNDE's current INBDE results page says results are normally available approximately three to four weeks after the examination date.

That is an estimate, not a guaranteed release date.

Do not assume:

  • every candidate testing in the same week receives results at the same hour;
  • a result is late simply because another candidate posted theirs online;
  • three weeks is a contractual deadline.

Check your DENTPIN account rather than relying on social-media result threads.

Pass/Fail Reporting

National Board examination results are criterion-referenced.

That means your performance is judged against an established standard, not against the percentage of other candidates who passed in your testing period.

JCNDE reports the INBDE as:

  • Pass, when the overall examination standard is met;
  • Fail, when the overall standard is not met.

The underlying scale ranges from 49 to 99, and the current Candidate Guide explains that 75 represents the minimum passing scale score.

A scale score of 74 does not mean that answering one additional question correctly would necessarily have produced 75. The scale accounts for examination-form characteristics and is not the same as a raw percentage-correct score.

See INBDE Scoring Explained: Scaled 75.

What a Passing Candidate Sees

Candidates who pass receive the official Pass outcome.

JCNDE does not use the passing result as a competitive numerical score for ranking candidates.

For licensing purposes, boards use the official National Board result according to their own jurisdiction requirements.

All U.S. licensing jurisdictions currently recognize the INBDE as fulfilling all or part of the written-examination requirement, but candidates still need to satisfy the other requirements of the state or territory where they seek licensure.

What an Unsuccessful Candidate Receives

For remediation, the current Candidate Guide says JCNDE provides additional information to candidates who fail.

This includes:

  • the candidate's overall scale score;
  • performance information for the three clinical content areas;
  • performance information for the 10 foundation knowledge areas.

The final pass/fail determination is based on the overall scale score, not on independently passing each content-area subscale.

Use the domain information to identify weak areas, but do not treat a subarea report as a separate examination score.

Why You Should Not Compare Raw Question Counts

INBDE forms are not identical.

JCNDE uses psychometric procedures so scores from different forms have comparable meaning even when question difficulty differs slightly.

The program also includes experimental questions that are not scored. These look like normal exam items, so candidates cannot identify which questions count.

Candidates are not penalized for guessing.

This is why unofficial calculations such as “I think I got 75% correct” cannot reliably predict your official result.

Results Audit

JCNDE permits a result audit request within 30 days of the reporting date on your official result.

An audit checks the accuracy of the recorded result. It is not a second academic review of your answers and does not allow you to challenge individual questions simply because you disagree with them.

Current fee discrepancy

The 2026 Candidate Guide updated 4 August 2026 lists a result-audit fee of $105.

The current public JCNDE INBDE results webpage still displays $65.

Because these two official sources conflict, verify the amount shown in your DENTPIN account when requesting an audit.

The public results page currently says audit results are available in approximately four to six weeks.

What Happens If You Pass

Passing the INBDE satisfies the National Board written examination component used by U.S. licensing jurisdictions.

It does not, by itself, create a dental license.

Your next steps depend on your pathway and jurisdiction and may include:

  • completing or graduating from the required dental education program;
  • satisfying a clinical examination or accepted alternative where required;
  • state jurisprudence requirements;
  • background checks;
  • other board-specific documentation;
  • applying to the relevant state dental board.

International dentists using advanced-standing pathways should coordinate the INBDE result with their school/application and eventual licensure plan.

See INBDE After Passing: Residency, Licensure & Next Steps.

What Happens If You Do Not Pass

The current JCNDE retest policy allows an unsuccessful candidate to apply again, but timing restrictions apply.

60-day minimum wait

You must wait at least 60 days between unsuccessful INBDE attempts.

Maximum four administrations in 12 months

The current guide allows a maximum of four INBDE administrations during any 12-month period.

Five Years/Five Attempts Rule

JCNDE's current policy requires candidates to pass within:

  • five years of their first INBDE attempt, or
  • five attempts,

whichever comes first.

The August 2026 guide adds that after the fifth year or fifth attempt, a candidate may test once every six months after the most recent attempt.

This is an important update because older JCNDE material used a 12-month interval after reaching that limit.

For a dedicated remediation plan, see INBDE Retake Strategy.

Candidate Scenarios

Scenario 1: You tested in early January 2027

If JCNDE follows its normal 3–4 week reporting timeframe, you would generally expect the result later in January or early February. That is an estimate, not an official scheduled result date.

Scenario 2: You failed with an overall scale score near 75

Do not assume you only need “one more correct answer.” Use the clinical-content and foundation-knowledge feedback to plan remediation, then respect the 60-day minimum retest period.

Scenario 3: You passed

Save the official result information and verify how your target school, licensing board or application service receives the result. Passing the INBDE is one part of the broader licensure pathway.

Scenario 4: You believe there is an administrative scoring error

If you want an audit, submit the request within 30 days. Understand that an audit checks result accuracy; it is not an opportunity to re-argue exam content.

Common Result Mistakes

  • Expecting a guaranteed result exactly 21 days after testing.
  • Treating Pass/Fail as if successful candidates receive a competitive numeric score.
  • Interpreting a 74 as “one question short.”
  • Comparing recalled raw question counts with other candidates.
  • Missing the 30-day audit window.
  • Retesting before the 60-day minimum.
  • Using an old version of the post-five-attempt retest rule.

FAQs

1. How long do INBDE results take in 2027?

JCNDE currently says results are typically available about three to four weeks after the examination date.

2. Is 75 the passing score?

The underlying scale uses 75 as the minimum passing score. Official candidate results are reported as Pass or Fail.

3. Do candidates who fail receive more detail?

Yes. Current JCNDE guidance provides unsuccessful candidates with the overall scale score and performance information for three clinical content areas and 10 foundation knowledge areas.

4. Can I appeal a low score?

You can request a results audit within 30 days. An audit checks accuracy; it is not a regrading of individual questions.

5. When can I retake after failing?

The current policy requires at least 60 days between unsuccessful attempts, subject to the maximum-four-in-12-months and Five Years/Five Attempts rules.
